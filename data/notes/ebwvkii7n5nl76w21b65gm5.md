For a generation Hong Kong had one of the **freest presses in Asia** — a noisy, competitive media market protected by the Basic Law's guarantee of press freedom, where Reporters Without Borders once ranked the city **18th in the world** (2002). In the space of five years that was dismantled. The two biggest independent newsrooms were **raided and shut**, their editors jailed; a **colonial-era sedition law** was revived and then sharpened into a weapon carrying years in prison; and by 2025 RSF had dropped Hong Kong to **140th of 180**, into the "**red zone**" of "very serious" repression for the first time, placing it alongside mainland China. This page gathers that record — the outlets destroyed, the journalists prosecuted, and the laws used to do it — and links to the newsrooms this archive has preserved.

> Since 2020 the Hong Kong government has prosecuted **at least 28 journalists**, eight of whom were in custody as of 2025 (Reporters Without Borders). *"At RSF, we have never seen such a sharp and rapid deterioration in the press-freedom record of any country or territory."*

## A free press, dismantled year by year

| Year | What happened |
|------|---------------|
| **2020** | The **National Security Law** takes effect (30 June). On **10 Aug**, 200 police raid the **Apple Daily** newsroom and arrest founder **Jimmy Lai** under the NSL. |
| **2021** | **Apple Daily** is destroyed: a second raid (**17 June**) arrests five executives and freezes the company's assets; starved of funds, the paper prints its **last edition on 24 June** — **1 million copies**. **RTHK**, the public broadcaster, is brought to heel under a new civil-servant director: programmes are deleted and archives pulled. On **29 Dec**, police raid **Stand News**, arresting senior staff; the outlet closes the same day. |
| **2022** | **Citizen News**, citing the "sword" hanging over the industry, closes pre-emptively on **4 Jan**. The **FCC** suspends its Human Rights Press Awards rather than risk breaking the law. Independent outlets shut or go quiet across the board. |
| **2024** | Hong Kong's homegrown **Article 23** law — the **Safeguarding National Security Ordinance** (23 Mar) — raises the penalty for **sedition** from 2 years to **7** (10 with an "external force"). In the first journalist sedition convictions since 1997, two **Stand News** editors are jailed (see below). **RFA** closes its Hong Kong bureau, citing the new law. |
| **2025** | RSF's World Press Freedom Index puts Hong Kong at **140/180**, its lowest ever score, in the "**red zone**" for the first time. |
| **2026** | Prosecutions reach **independent online media**: an outlet operator (**Boom News**) and an activist are arrested for "**seditious acts**" over filming a public commemoration — released on bail, no charge laid, then the operator's **personal bank accounts are frozen** (7 Oct) with no formal order shown (see below). Separately, ordinary Hongkongers are jailed for **Facebook posts**: a retiree is given a year for 53 seditious posts attacking the authorities — among them comments tying the [[Wang Fuk Court fire|hong-kong-fire-2025]] to possible 2019-style unrest. |

## The two newsrooms that were destroyed

### Apple Daily (蘋果日報)

The city's largest pro-democracy tabloid, founded by **Jimmy Lai** in 1995. Raided under the National Security Law in August 2020 and again in June 2021, when its assets were frozen and five senior executives arrested; unable to pay staff or suppliers, it published its **final edition on 24 June 2021**, a million copies snapped up across the city. Lai and several editors remain on trial or convicted in the long-running NSL case. The paper's full run is preserved here: [[Apple Daily|sites.appledaily]] and its [[publications & special editions|sites.appledaily.publications]]; Lai's own story is on the [[Jimmy Lai|faces-of-the-crackdown.jimmy-lai]] page.

### Stand News (立場新聞)

The flagship of Hong Kong's post-*Apple* independent media, raided and closed on **29 December 2021**. In the first use of the colonial sedition offence against journalists since the 1997 handover, chief editor **Chung Pui-kuen** and acting chief editor **Patrick Lam** were **convicted on 29 August 2024** — the judge ruling that **11 of 17** articles (mostly opinion pieces and interviews with democrats) had "**seditious intent**" — and **sentenced on 26 September 2024**: Chung to **21 months**, Lam to **11 months** (released immediately on medical grounds and time served over the two-year trial). The parent company, **Best Pencil**, was convicted and fined. Press-freedom groups called it a watershed: *"journalists jailed for doing their job."* Stand News's archive is preserved here: [[Stand News|sites.standnews]].

## The newest front — criminalising the reporter (2026)

On **30 September 2026**, national-security police arrested **Tang Ho-wing** (鄧浩榮, 37), who runs the online outlet **Boom News** (爆炸頭; some **200,000 followers**), and former League of Social Democrats member **Lee Ying-chi** (58), for allegedly "**spreading false information**" with **seditious intent** under the 2024 security law — an offence carrying up to **seven years**; the force confirmed the arrests on **1 October**. The arrests stemmed from an **31 August 2026** gathering outside **Prince Edward MTR station**, where people were found "holding bouquets and shouting slogans" to mark the **2019 Prince Edward incident** — the night riot police stormed a train and beat passengers, an event the authorities now call a "manipulated lie." Tang had posted video from the scene. Boom News's Facebook presence is preserved in this archive as a browsable web capture — see [[Boom News|sites.boomnews]].

Police said those involved were "suspected of spreading false information and attacking government with lies" to "incite hatred." The **Hong Kong Journalists Association** responded that "**reporting is not sedition**," warning that journalists now face arrest simply for covering public events; the **Committee to Protect Journalists** called it "**the latest sign of the criminalisation of journalism**" and demanded the pair's release ([HKFP](https://hongkongfp.com/2026/10/01/hong-kong-confirms-journalists-arrest-for-alleged-seditious-acts-linked-to-2019-prince-edward-mtr-incident-commemorations/)).

Tang was **released on bail shortly after midnight on 2 October**, after almost two days in custody; **no charge has been laid**, and the HKJA — "deeply concerned" — demanded that police disclose exactly which of his actions broke the law. He is one of **eight people** held over the 31 August gathering — three arrested on 3 September, three more on 27–28 September, and Tang and Lee on the 30th; the earlier six were freed on bail, and police have not ruled out further arrests ([HKFP](https://hongkongfp.com/2026/10/02/independent-hk-journalist-tang-ho-wing-released-on-bail-following-sedition-arrest/)). The case marks the frontier moving from the big newsrooms to a lone operator with a phone and a following.

Then the pressure moved from his reporting to his livelihood. On **7 October**, Tang said all of his **personal bank accounts had been frozen** — no advance notice, no formal freezing order shown — leaving him on cash, unable to take even food-delivery work, and, in his words, facing having to "resort to begging." No bank was named and no amount disclosed, and the authorities did not confirm ordering the freeze. It was first reported by the exile outlet **Points Media** (棱角媒體) and documented by the China Media Project's *[Lingua Sinica](https://lingua-sinica.org/frozen-out)*. Freezing the everyday banking of a journalist who has been charged with nothing echoes the asset and funding measures aimed at exiles abroad (see [[🌏 Pursued Abroad|pursued-abroad]]) — the same logic of cutting off money, now turned on someone still in the city.

## The weapons

- **The colonial sedition law.** A dormant offence under the old **Crimes Ordinance** (ss. 9–10), untouched since the 1960s, was revived from 2020 to prosecute speech, slogans, children's books and journalism alike.
- **Article 23 / the Safeguarding National Security Ordinance (2024).** Hong Kong's own security law, enacted 23 March 2024, re-enacted and **expanded sedition** — raising the maximum from two years to **seven** (ten where an "external force" is involved) and widening what counts as seditious intent.
- **The National Security Law (2020).** Beijing-imposed; the basis for the *Apple Daily* collusion case and the detention of [[Jimmy Lai|faces-of-the-crackdown.jimmy-lai]].
- **Money — tax audits and frozen accounts.** Pressure need not go through a courtroom. From **May 2025** the **Inland Revenue Department** ran simultaneous tax audits and backdated demands against at least **six outlets** (including HKFP) and some **20 linked individuals**; HKFP settled for **HK$57,692** after a 20-month probe (with a 135% penalty surcharge), and inMediaHK — found to owe nothing — still spent **HK$40,000** contesting it. On **13 April 2026** the **Hong Kong Journalists Association**, chaired by **Selina Cheng**, was ordered to pre-pay **HK$730,000** in provisional tax "within two days," more than double the previous year's HK$300,000. Cheng said the media audits divert resources from genuine high-value evaders and amount to "undue stress and unfair punishment," echoing tactics used elsewhere to charge journalists with tax offences "to undermine their credibility"; Commissioner Benjamin Chan denied the department weighs a taxpayer's occupation ([HKFP, 4 May 2026](https://hongkongfp.com/2026/05/04/hong-kong-press-union-faces-hk730k-prepaid-tax-demand-accuses-tax-office-of-misallocating-public-resources/)). The same financial squeeze reached individuals: [[Boom News|sites.boomnews]]' **Tang Ho-wing** had all his personal bank accounts frozen days after his bail release.

Together these turned editorial judgement — which article to run, which gathering to film — into potential evidence of a crime, while the tax office and the banks squeezed the outlets and journalists that remained. The same tools reach the archive's pages on [[book censorship|book-censorship]], the [[Tiananmen vigil|tiananmen-vigil]] and [[Glory to Hong Kong|glory-to-hong-kong]].

## Help preserve the record

Hong Kong's free press is being written out of the city's own memory — newsrooms deleted, archives pulled offline, reporting recast as crime. If you have **saved articles, front pages, broadcasts, newsletters or newsroom records** from any of these outlets — or others not yet listed — they belong in the record before they vanish. See [[how to contribute|participation]], or open an issue on our archive. Court reporting on the journalist trials is tracked by [[The Witness|sites.thewitness]].
