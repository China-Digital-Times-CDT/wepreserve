# Critics and Counter-Arguments: What Explains Eileen Chang's (張愛玲) Literary Endurance?

Researcher's notes for testing the thesis that Chang lasts because of a humanist core: attention to, and care for, individual and ordinary lives rather than grand political or national narratives.

Conventions used throughout:
- **"QUOTE"** = exact wording, checked against the page linked. "(quoted in X)" = exact wording taken from a secondary source that quotes it; the primary text was not seen.
- *Paraphrase* = summary, not quotation.
- **UNVERIFIED** = remembered or widely repeated wording that I could not confirm against a fetchable source. Don't print these as quotations without checking the book.
- Simplified and traditional characters are kept as they appear in each source.
- PRC state or Party organs (e.g., 中國作家網 chinawriter.com.cn, 人民網, 文藝報) are not used as authorities. Where a PRC-press attack is the *subject* (e.g., the 1990s–2000s 文化漢奸 campaign in 《文藝報》), it is cited only through independent scholarship that reports it.

## Q1. Fu Lei 傅雷 (as 迅雨), 〈論張愛玲的小說〉 (《萬象》, May 1944), and Chang's reply 〈自己的文章〉

### Takeaway
Fu Lei's essay is the founding critical judgement on Chang, and it argues *for* a humanist reading. He praises 〈金鎖記〉 for the author's "deep compassion," which "summons the reader's compassion" for a weak woman who became a "slave to passion." He attacks 《連環套》 and 〈傾城之戀〉 for technique without substance and for cliché. He also scolds the May Fourth Left for despising technique in favour of "isms." Chang replied in 〈自己的文章〉 with what is still the clearest statement of her humanist-but-anti-heroic creed: ordinary "weak mortals" (軟弱的凡人) represent "the sum total of this age" (這時代的總量) better than heroes do, and 蒼涼 (desolation) beats 悲壯 (tragic heroism).

### Cited Findings

**Publication facts**
- Fu Lei published the essay under the pen name 迅雨 in 柯靈's 《萬象》 in May 1944. Ke Ling cut a passage criticising Ba Jin (巴金) "to avoid exploitation by the enemy-puppet authorities" (為避免敵偽利用), and Fu Lei was furious. Sources for these facts: 張泉, 〈張愛玲「文化漢奸」論質疑──兼談傅雷、柯靈的張愛玲論〉, 《二十一世紀》網絡版 no. 16 (31 July 2003), citing 柯靈 〈懷傅雷〉 (1979) and 〈遙寄張愛玲〉 (1985) — [CUHK 21C PDF](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/online/0305084.pdf)

**Praise of 〈金鎖記〉** (wording as quoted in 劉鋒杰, 〈創作個性與文學轉型的誤讀──重讀傅雷〈論張愛玲的小說〉〉, 《文藝理論研究》 2000 no. 4, reposted on 愛思想 — [aisixiang](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/93385.html))
- "至少也该列为我们文坛最美的收获之一" (at least it should rank among the finest harvests of our literary world).
- "颇有《狂人日记》中某些故事的风味".
- **Textual crux.** Liu Fengjie's own footnote says Fu Lei's printed text reads 《猎人日记》 (Turgenev's *A Sportsman's Sketches*). He believes this "应为'狂人日记'之误印" (should be a misprint for Lu Xun's 'Diary of a Madman'), and he records that 邵迎建 disagrees and holds that 獵人日記 is not a misprint. Many reprints and secondary sources silently print 狂人日記. **Do not resolve this silently.** Check the 1944 《萬象》 text or a facsimile. — [aisixiang](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/93385.html)
- The "most complete work" wording (commonly given as 「《金鎖記》是張女士截至目前為止最完滿之作」) appears in a lecture transcript summarised in search results. I did not see the full sentence on a page I could open. **UNVERIFIED as exact wording.**
- **Compassion: the passage most relevant to the humanism thesis.** "她战败了，她是弱者。但因为是弱者，她就没有被同情的资格了么？弱者做了情欲的俘虏，代情欲做了刽子手，我们便有理由恨她么？作者不这么想。" and "作者深切的怜悯，唤引着读者的怜悯" (the author's deep compassion summons the reader's compassion). Both are quoted in Liu Fengjie — [aisixiang](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/93385.html). Liu notes that Fu Lei still calls 曹七巧 "一个出身低微的轻狂女子" (a frivolous woman of low birth), so he did not build his verdict on that compassion.
- Fu Lei also wished Chang would "成为一个彻底的悲观主义者，把人生剥出一个血淋淋的面目来" (become a thorough pessimist and flay life down to its bloody face) — quoted in Liu Fengjie, [aisixiang](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/93385.html).

**Criticism of 〈傾城之戀〉, the "small" stories, and 《連環套》** (quoted in Liu Fengjie, [aisixiang](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/93385.html))
- On 〈傾城之戀〉: "好似六朝的骈体，虽然珠光宝气，内里却空空洞洞，既没有真正的欢畅，也没有刻骨的悲哀" (like Six Dynasties parallel prose: jewelled outside, hollow inside). Also "仿佛是一座雕刻精工的翡翠宝塔，而非莪特式大寺的一角" (a finely carved jade pagoda rather than a corner of a Gothic cathedral; the page prints 悲翠, which is probably an OCR/typo for 翡翠). And it lacks "悲剧的严肃、崇高，和宿命性".
- On 〈封鎖〉 and 〈年青的時候〉: "迷人的奢侈" (charming luxuries), "充其量也只能制造一些小骨董" (at most they can produce little curios).
- On 《連環套》: "主要弊病是内容的贫乏" (its main fault is poverty of content). Its erotic scenes "简直用起旧小说和京戏——尤其是梆子戏——中最要不得而最叫座的镜头！" And: "俗套滥调，在任何文字里都是毒素！希望作者从此和它们隔离起来。她自有她净化的文体。"
- Fu Lei's own caveat: "沒有《金鎖記》，我決不把《連環套》批評得那麼嚴厲，而且也根本不會寫這篇文章" (without 金鎖記 I would never have criticised 連環套 so severely, nor written this essay at all) — quoted in 張泉, [21C PDF](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/online/0305084.pdf).
- Fu Lei on the theme: "题材只限于男女问题" and "除了男女之外，世界究竟还辽阔得很" (beyond men and women, the world is after all very wide) — quoted in 宋聲泉, 〈重讀《金鎖記》：在"男女"與"時代"之間〉, 《華中師範大學學報》. I saw it only via a chinawriter.com.cn repost, so I used it for the wording of the quotation only, not as an authority — [repost](https://www.chinawriter.com.cn/n1/2020/0909/c419384-31855145.html).

**Technique versus "isms": Fu Lei's real target**
- "我們的作家一向對技巧抱著鄙夷的態度。『五四』以後，消耗了無數的筆墨是關於主義的論戰。彷彿一有準確的意識就能立地成佛似的，區區藝術更不成問題。" — quoted in 張泉, [21C PDF](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/online/0305084.pdf). Zhang Quan reads Fu Lei as using Chang's craft to expose the faults of leftist New Literature: politics first, theme before art, the philosophy of struggle.
- The widely repeated line 「技巧對張女士是最危險的誘惑」 did not turn up on any fetchable page. **UNVERIFIED.**

**Chang's reply, 〈自己的文章〉 (1944)** (exact wording from a full-text repost on 澎湃號·湃客, 19 Aug 2024 — [thepaper.cn](https://m.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_28451163). Recheck against 《流言》 or the Crown 皇冠 edition before printing.)
- On the stable side of life: "我发现弄文学的人向来是注重人生飞扬的一面，而忽视人生安稳的一面。" "而人生安稳的一面则有着永恒的意味，虽然这种安稳常是不安全的，而且每隔多少时候就要破坏一次，但仍然是永恒的。" "好的作品，还是在于它是以人生的安稳做底子来描写人生的飞扬的。"
- **The core humanist statement:** "他们不是英雄，他们可是这时代的广大的负荷者。" "而且我相信，他们虽然不过是软弱的凡人，不及英雄的有力，但正是这些凡人比英雄更能代表这时代的总量。" (They are not heroes, but they are the broad bearers of this age… these weak mortals represent the sum total of the age better than heroes do.)
- Refusing the monument: "一般所说'时代的纪念碑'那样的作品，我是写不出来的，也不打算尝试".
- "我甚至只是写些男女间的小事情，我的作品里没有战争，也没有革命。我以为人在恋爱的时候，是比在战争或革命的时候更素朴，也更放恣的。"
- On 蒼涼 and 參差的對照: "我不喜欢壮烈。我是喜欢悲壮，更喜欢苍凉。" "苍凉之所以有更深长的回味，就因为它像葱绿配桃红，是一种参差的对照。" "他们没有悲壮，只有苍凉。悲壮是一种完成，而苍凉则是一种启示。"
- On her "half-hearted" characters: "所以我的小说里，除了《金锁记》里的曹七巧，全是些不彻底的人物。" "因为他们虽然不彻底，但究竟是认真的。"
- On truth inside falseness: "我不把虚伪与真实写成强烈的对照，却是用参差的对照的手法写出现代人的虚伪之中有真实，浮华之中有素朴".
- Concessions on 《連環套》, as quoted by 張泉: it "欠注意到主題是真" and "有許多地方襲用舊小說的詞句……有時候未免刻意做作". Forty years later she judged it more harshly herself: "儘管自以為壞，也沒想到這樣惡劣，連篇胡扯，不禁駭笑。" — [21C PDF](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/online/0305084.pdf).
- 錢谷融 on the exchange: Chang did not accept Fu Lei's advice "只是由於美學觀和世界觀的不同，她不能違己以屈從罷了", and she wrote "婉轉", with no lapse in courtesy (quoted in 張泉, [21C PDF](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/online/0305084.pdf)).

### Inferences
- The humanism thesis has a strong *primary* anchor in Chang's own manifesto ("凡人比英雄更能代表這時代的總量"). But her model is anti-heroic and unsentimental: 蒼涼 rather than pathos, and "不徹底" people rather than victims to be rescued. The 1944 Fu–Chang exchange is really about whether compassion has to come with moral and tragic grandeur. Fu Lei says yes; Chang refuses.
- Fu Lei's praise is itself humanist ("深切的憐憫"). His criticisms (hollowness, "little curios," cliché) anticipate the later "too small, too worldly" objection (see Wang Anyi, Q6).

### Gaps
- The exact sentences "最完滿之作" and "技巧對張女士是最危險的誘惑" were not confirmed on an accessible page.
- The 狂人日記 / 獵人日記 crux needs the 1944 《萬象》 printing.
- The first-publication venue and date of 〈自己的文章〉 is often given as 《新東方》, May 1944 (later in 《苦竹》 and 《流言》). Zhang Quan says "1944年7月". Not resolved here.

## Q2. C. T. Hsia 夏志清, *A History of Modern Chinese Fiction* (Yale, 1961; 2nd ed. 1971; Chinese ed. 1979)

### Takeaway
Hsia canonised Chang on aesthetic and moral grounds, not populist ones: imagery, a historical sense, moral seriousness without "high-minded ideals," and a pessimism masked by satire. He did it explicitly as a Cold War counter-canon to the leftist tradition. His most cited judgements are "the best and most important writer in Chinese today" (p. 389) and "The Golden Cangue" as "the greatest novelette in the history of Chinese literature."

### Cited Findings
- Hsia called Chang "the best and most important writer in Chinese today" (p. 389, 1961 ed.) — cited by [WashU Libraries exhibit "Love and Desires"](https://digitalexhibits.library.washu.edu/s/love-and-desires/page/biography); the same phrase appears in Jiwei Xiao, "Belated Reunion?", *New Left Review* 111 (May–June 2018) — [NLR](https://newleftreview.org/issues/ii111/articles/jiwei-xiao-belated-reunion).
- The fuller sentence is usually given as: "To the discerning student of modern Chinese literature, Eileen Chang is not only the best and most important writer in Chinese today; her short stories alone invite valid comparison with, and in some respects claim superiority over, the work of serious modern women writers in English: Katherine Mansfield, Katherine Anne Porter, Eudora Welty, and Carson McCullers."
  - **PARTLY UNVERIFIED.** A CUHK bio page quoted in search results gives "...short stories invite valid comparisons with, and in some respects claim superiority over, the work of serious modern women writers in English" — [CUHK RCT bio](https://cuhk.edu.hk/rct/staff/bio/b13.html) (the page timed out when fetched). NLR confirms Mansfield and McCullers are named. I could not confirm Porter and Welty from an accessible text, so check p. 389.
- Hsia: "The Golden Cangue" is "the greatest novelette in the history of Chinese literature" — [Renditions author page, CUHK](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/renditions/authors/zhangal.html) (no page number given).
- Hsia: "Chang does not profess high-minded ideals," and her "registration of the inescapable pettiness and sadness of human endeavour is nearly always morally disturbing." Sheng Yun's LRB review (see Q7) quotes both lines; search-result paraphrases of it add that this is because "she refrains from overt gestures of indignation or protest." Sheng Yun, "Small Feet Were an Advantage," *LRB* 41:15 (1 Aug 2019) — [LRB](https://lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v41/n15/sheng-yun/small-feet-were-an-advantage). This is the single most useful Hsia line for the thesis: moral force *through* restraint, not through protest.
- Hsia's phrases as quoted in Liu Zaifu, "Eileen Chang's Fiction and C. T. Hsia's *A History of Modern Chinese Fiction*," trans. Yunzhong Shu, MCLC Resource Center (2009; Chinese original 2000/2002) — [MCLC](https://u.osu.edu/mclc/?p=4634):
  - "a strong historical awareness" (Hsia p. 396).
  - "a society in transition, where the only constants are the egoism in every bosom" (p. 396).
  - "she is little tempted to follow the dazzling fashions of present-day Western fiction" (p. 397).
  - "A profound pessimist, she can afford at the same time to be a gay satirist" (p. 414).
  - Liu sums up Hsia's two grounds as "historical awareness and moral concern," together with praise of her "rich imagery."
- Hsia on *The Rice-Sprout Song*: Chinese edition 「《秧歌》在中國小説史上已經是本不朽之作。」 — quoted in 胡雅雯, 〈【百年張愛玲・上】為何張愛玲被認定為中國最重要的作家？〉, HK01, 29 Sept 2020 — [HK01](https://www.hk01.com/article/529961/). The English is usually given as "already a classic in Chinese fiction" (**UNVERIFIED** English wording). Hu Yawen notes that Hsia entered Chang's work *through* the anti-Communist 《秧歌》, not through 《傳奇》, and paraphrases Hsia as saying Chang depicted Communist terror through human nature (人性) rather than dialectics.
- Chinese-edition rendering of p. 389: 「張愛玲該是今日中國最優秀最重要的作家」 — HK01 subheading, [HK01](https://www.hk01.com/article/529961/). The fuller Chinese sentence (「對於一個研究現代中國文學的人說來，……僅以短篇小說而論，她的成就堪與英美現代女文豪如曼斯菲爾德……相比，有些地方，她恐怕還要高明一籌」) is **UNVERIFIED** here.
- **Cold War framing.** 張英進 notes that Hsia "毫不掩飾地將張愛玲建構成對抗「一種不同於主要由左翼和無產階級作家書寫的文學傳統」的一個反文典", and quotes Hsia (Chinese ed.) calling Chang 「一位抗拒時代精神的獨行天才，她才可能對那個時代提出終極概括，其意義絕非一群在時代後面亦步亦趨的二流作家所能比擬」 (citing Hsia 1961, pp. 431, 439). Source: 張英進, 〈張愛玲的「超文典」表演書寫〉, 《二十一世紀》 no. 163 (Oct 2017) — [CUHK 21C PDF](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c163-201611024.pdf).
- Variant Hsia line: "the most gifted Chinese writer to emerge in the forties, and certainly the most important" — quoted (no page) in James Jiang, "Love and Desolation: Remembering Eileen Chang," *Sydney Review of Books*, 28 Sept 2020 — [SRB](https://sydneyreviewofbooks.com/essay/eileen-chang/). Source text within Hsia not identified.
- **Dissent from inside the canon: Liu Zaifu 劉再復.** He calls Hsia's "historical awareness"/"moral concern" claims "quite questionable." He argues the best works ("The Golden Cangue," "Love in a Fallen City") are "characterized by their philosophical awareness rather than their historical awareness," "shot through with a feeling of desolation," and that they enter "a philosophical, universal, and eternal world" by transcending politics, nation and history — [MCLC](https://u.osu.edu/mclc/?p=4634).

### Inferences
- Hsia's grounds overlap with the humanism thesis ("moral concern," "egoism in every bosom") but are not the same thing. Hsia values moral *disturbance* and pessimism, not warmth or care. Liu Zaifu moves the explanation from history and morality to a philosophical, universal tragic sense, which is a related but more metaphysical "humanism."
- Hsia's canonisation was openly a Cold War act (Zhang Yingjin). That weakens any claim that her reputation rose purely on apolitical humanist merit (see Q8c).

### Gaps
- No accessible full text of Hsia's chapter 15 (archive.org copies are lending-restricted and search-inside was blocked). Page-level checking of the Porter/Welty wording, the Golden Cangue page, and the Chinese edition's exact sentences is still needed. Archive.org IDs: `historyofmodernc00hsia` (1971), `historyofmodernc2ndhsia`.

## Q3. Leo Ou-fan Lee 李歐梵, *Shanghai Modern* (Harvard UP, 1999), chapter "Eileen Chang: Romances in a Fallen City"

### Takeaway
Lee reads Chang through material, urban "details" that remap Shanghai's spaces, and through a "philosophy of desolation" voiced by her narrator. His reading of 〈封鎖〉 as "a fantasy narrative framed by a realistic setting" stresses irony and anti-romance over sympathy. None of his own wording on 蒼涼 or 參差的對照 could be checked against the book.

### Cited Findings
- The chapter title "Eileen Chang: romances in a fallen city" (with an epilogue "A tale of two cities") is confirmed by a library catalogue — [HCU Hamburg catalogue](https://katalog.hcu-hamburg.de/vufind/Record/252394348).
- Lee, as quoted (no page) by Géraldine A. Fiss, "Response to Presentations," USC centennial symposium *Love, Lust, Caution*, 20 Nov 2020: "With her details, Chang forces our attention to those material 'signifiers' that serve not only to tell a different story about Shanghai's urban life but also to reconfigure the spaces of the city – private, public, small and large – in accordance with her own vision." — [USC PDF](https://libraries.usc.edu/sites/default/files/events/documents/Ge%CC%81raldine%20Fiss%20Response%20to%20Presentations_v2.pdf)
- Lee, as quoted by Shu-Ling Chua (The Margins, AAWW, 23 Dec 2020): Chang's omniscient narrator reveals "the author's own attitude toward life, a philosophy of desolation." — [AAWW](https://aaww.org/a-lifelong-romance-reflections-on-eileen-changs-life-work-and-legacy/)
- Lee on 〈封鎖〉, as quoted by Eric Sandberg, "Eileen Chang's 'Sealed Off' and the Possibility of Modernist Romance," *ARIEL*: it is a "romantic fantasy" (*Shanghai Modern* 285, 289), "a dream framed by reality, a fantasy narrative framed by a realistic setting" (290–91). Sandberg argues *against* Lee, Xudong Zhang and Gunn: the story records "the moment when the temporal, spatial, and social deformations of the wartime environment enable genuine human contact" — [ARIEL](https://journalhosting.ucalgary.ca/index.php/ariel/article/view/36118/52745). (Byline inferred by the subagent; volume and year not confirmed.)
- Lee's Chinese book 《蒼涼與世故：張愛玲的啟示》 (香港：牛津大學出版社, 2006) appears in search results only, as an author-bio listing and a 2019 citation. I did not open a page confirming its details or content (**UNVERIFIED**).

### Inferences
- Lee supports the "urban everyday" half of the thesis. His "fantasy framed by reality" reading points to ironic distance, not "care."

### Gaps
- Lee's exact wording on 蒼涼, on "uneven contrast" (參差的對照), and on her refusal of revolutionary history is **UNVERIFIED**; the chapter text was not reachable. Translators differ on 參差的對照: Fiss gives "contrast in de-cadence"; the SRB translation of 〈自己的文章〉 gives "equivocal contrast."

## Q4. David Der-wei Wang 王德威: 「祖師奶奶」, 張派, the "poetics of involution," history as monster

### Takeaway
Wang turned Chang's influence into a literary-historical category, the 張派 (Chang school). He reads her as the 「祖師奶奶」 of modern Chinese letters. In his account, her "desolate" vision of history and her obsessive rewriting ("derivation over revelation, involution over revolution") quietly counter the linear, revolutionary account of Chinese modernity. That is an argument about her *view of history*, not mainly about compassion.

### Cited Findings
- 「祖師奶奶」. James Jiang calls Chang the "zushi nainai ('Grandma/ster' in Xiao Jiwei's rendering of David Der-wei Wang's epithet) of modern Chinese letters" — [SRB](https://sydneyreviewofbooks.com/essay/eileen-chang/). Wang's own usage, in his essay on the Chang school (aisixiang repost): "更有作者懵懂开笔，写来写去，才赫然发觉竟与"祖师奶奶"灵犀一点通。" — [aisixiang 90955](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/90955.html). An essay often titled 〈張愛玲成了祖師奶奶〉 is attributed to Wang, but I could not locate the text or its first venue (**UNVERIFIED**).
- **Key Chang-school essay:** 王德威, 〈落地的麥子不死──張愛玲的文學影響力與「張派」作家的超越之路〉, first in 蔡鳳儀編《華麗與蒼涼──張愛玲紀念文集》 (台北：皇冠, 1996), pp. 196–210 — bibliographic data from 鈴木基子, 「張愛玲と台湾文学史」, Nihon University economics faculty bulletin no. 82 (2023) — [PDF](https://www.eco.nihon-u.ac.jp/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/82-03.pdf). Later the title of a book: 《落地的麥子不死：張愛玲與「張派」傳人》 (濟南：山東畫報, 2004) — [Douban review](https://book.douban.com/review/1258023/).
- Lines from the essay (aisixiang repost, [link](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/90955.html)):
  - "处在历史的夹缝中，能抓住点什么，管它庸俗零碎，总就对付过了下去。"
  - On her aesthetic: "是她对照参差，'不彻底'的美学观照，还是她苍凉却华丽的末世视野？"
  - "看张作品中的强烈世俗取向，算是极有见地的观察。"
  - On influence: "'影响的焦虑'还是影响的欢喜，张爱玲的魅力，可见一斑。"
  - The essay also uses the phrase "张爱玲的苍凉史观."
- Wang on Wang Anyi as successor (quoted in 張婷, 「对世俗的两种关照」, Aichi University repository, citing 《落地的麦子不死》 p. 47 — [PDF](https://aichiu.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/7641/files/101-04%E8%AB%96%E8%AA%AC%E5%BC%B5.pdf)): "是否有作家能突破限制，另谱张派新腔呢？我以为女作家王安忆是首选。…王安忆…这使她为张的人世风景，真正赋予当代意义。…可是万千细节——历史的，空间的细节——最后都归结到一个平凡女人一生的起落上，这又回到《倾城之恋》的模式上".
- **"Poetics of involution" (回旋與衍生).** USC US–China Institute listing for Wang's talk "Madame White, The Book of Change, and Eileen Chang: On a Poetics of Involution" (UCLA, 17 Apr 2010). It summarises his argument that Chang's rewriting produces "a poetics that values derivation over revelation and involution over revolution," and that because her writing "questions a linear account of history and progress" it offers a critique of Chinese literary modernity "that revolutionary discourse would likely reject" (listing paraphrase, not Wang's own words) — [USC](https://china.usc.edu/event/madame-white-book-change-and-eileen-chang-poetics-involution).
- **History as monster.** Zhang Yingjin summarises Wang (*The Monster That Is History*, UC Press 2004, pp. 131–37) as showing that the starving women of 《秧歌》 foresaw the coming famine, so that "history" in Chang appears as an unfathomable 「怪獸」. He also summarises Wang's 〈小說中國〉 (in 《想像中國的方法》, 三聯 1998) as placing Chang in a tradition that uses 「日常生活對抗政治歷史」 (everyday life against political history) — [21C PDF](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c163-201611024.pdf).

### Inferences
- Wang supplies the strongest scholarly version of an "everyday life against History" reading. It supports the thesis's *anti-grand-narrative* half. His emphasis on "苍凉史观," 末世視野 (end-of-the-world vision) and the 「世俗取向」 points to a worldly, disenchanted sensibility rather than "care."

### Gaps
- No accessible text found for Wang's NYRB or UC Press introductions. His English Focus Features essay (2008), quoted in Q7, gives "premature fin-de-siècle poetics," "ironic observation of human vanities and follies," and "total lack of confidence in human attachment." 反高潮 (anti-climax) as Wang's term was not confirmed.

## Q5. Rey Chow 周蕾, *Woman and Chinese Modernity* (1991)

### Takeaway
Chow is the strongest scholarly support for the "detail against grand narrative" half of the thesis. But her frame is feminist and deconstructive, not humanist. Chang's detail is set against idealist wholes like "Man" and "Self" as much as against "China," "reform" and "revolution." So Chow undercuts any humanism grounded in "Man."

### Cited Findings
- Chow, as quoted by Leo Lee (*Shanghai Modern*, p. 271), as reproduced by Fiss (her quotation marks are garbled; wording approximate): Chang's "world of detail" is always already broken from a presumed "whole." It is "that which is itself cut off, incomplete, desolate, but which is at the same time sensuously local and immediate – rather than the wholeness of idealist notions like 'Man,' 'Self,' or 'China.'" — [USC PDF](https://libraries.usc.edu/sites/default/files/events/documents/Ge%CC%81raldine%20Fiss%20Response%20to%20Presentations_v2.pdf). **Third-hand. Verify against Chow 1991 (probably the chapter "Modernity and Narration—in Feminine Detail").**
- Chow (as quoted by Fiss, no page): Chang portrays "these sensuous, trivial, and superfluous textual presences that exist in ambiguous relation with some larger vision such as reform and revolution, which seeks to subordinate them but which is displaced by their surprising returns." — [USC PDF](https://libraries.usc.edu/sites/default/files/events/documents/Ge%CC%81raldine%20Fiss%20Response%20to%20Presentations_v2.pdf)
- 張英進's summary, citing Chow pp. 114, 120: 「周蕾突出張愛玲作品的細節碎片，以此責問「啟蒙」和「革命」、「『人』、『自我』或『中國』等一系列理想主義概念」」 — [21C PDF](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c163-201611024.pdf). This independently corroborates the "Man / Self / China" passage and gives page numbers.
- USC exhibit "In a Bronze Mirror" (no byline): Chow reads 〈更衣記〉 / "Chinese Life and Fashions" as "a sophisticated theorization of Chinese modernity and modern consciousness linked to the vanishing of superfluous detail" (probably the exhibit's paraphrase) — [USC Scalar](https://scalar.usc.edu/works/eileen-chang-exhibit/chinese-life-and-fashions.101).
- Chow later wrote on Chang's interest in "quotidian living" (as quoted by Sandberg, citing p. 160): Chow, "Seminal Dispersal, Fecal Retention, and Related Narrative Matters: Eileen Chang's Tale of Roses in the Problematic of Modern Writing," *differences* 11.2 (1999): 153–76 — [Project MUSE](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/9596).
- **A rival reading of the same details:** Ban Wang 王斑 (*The Sublime Figure of History*, Stanford 1997, pp. 90, 97–99), as summarised by 張英進, reads them instead as post-modern "floating signifiers" (漂浮的能指) with no deep meaning. They express doubt about language and 「對世事無常的感傷，對蒼涼、毀滅、死亡的悲哀」 — [21C PDF](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c163-201611024.pdf).

### Gaps
- Chow's own wording and page numbers were not seen directly (archive.org lending copy blocked). Both Chow passages above come via Fiss and Leo Lee. Check them against Chow 1991 before printing as quotations.

## Q6. Wang Anyi 王安憶, and other Chinese-language critics (高全之, 張小虹, 張英進, 余秋雨, 柯靈, 胡蘭成, 唐文標)

### Takeaway
Wang Anyi is the most important "insider" dissent from a humanism reading. She grants Chang's beauty but says Chang's love of present, worldly life "comes from fear of life," that her worldview is "nihilistic" (虛無), and that her world is smaller than Wang's own: "我可能永远不能写得像她这么美，但我的世界比她的大。"

### Cited Findings
**Wang Anyi** (quoted in 胡紅英, 〈在海派文學與"五四"文學的傳統之間〉, 《當代文壇》 2021 no. 1, aisixiang repost — [link](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/125293.html))
- "我可能永远不能写得像她这么美，但我的世界比她的大。" (I may never write as beautifully as she, but my world is bigger than hers.) Remarks at a 2000 Hong Kong conference, as recorded by 許子東, 《張愛玲與20世紀中文文學·吶喊與流言》 (上海文藝, 2004), p. 13.
- From 〈世俗的張愛玲〉 (in 《王安憶讀書筆記》, 新星, 2007, pp. 160, 164): "她对现时生活的爱好是出于对人生的恐惧，她对世界的看法是虚无的……并不去追究事实的具体原因，只是笼统地认为……个人是无所作为的". And, contrasting Lu Xun: "我更加尊敬现实主义的鲁迅，因他是从现实的步骤上，结结实实地走来，所以，他就有了走向虚无的立足点，也有了勇敢。"
- Rejecting the Chang-school label: "之前我从没有想到我和张爱玲有什么关系，这是王德威给我的一个褒奖" and "我和她所写不是一类" (〈張愛玲之於我〉, 《小說課堂》, 商務 2012, pp. 288, 293; 2008 Hong Kong lecture).
- From an interview (quoted in 張婷, Aichi U., citing 《王安忆说》 (湖南文艺), p. 154 — [PDF](https://aichiu.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/7641/files/101-04%E8%AB%96%E8%AA%AC%E5%BC%B5.pdf)): "我觉得张爱玲是个徘徊在两端的人，她的世界很矛盾，要么是非常琐碎的一桌子麻将之类的场景，要么就是又虚又悠远的东西。虚无使她必须抓住生活中琐碎具体的细节，日常生活的点滴触动，但她忽略了现实生活和理想。张爱玲从感觉到虚无，没有现实的依据，而我和我的作品是脚踏在现实的土地上的，我的情感范围要比她大一些，我不能在她的作品中得到满足。"
- Zhang Ting's own scholarly summary (paraphrase): Chang's everyday life "只有现在, 没有过去, 更没有未来, 是享乐主义的", used to dissolve her sense of nihilism. Wang Anyi adds a second, intellectual-humanist gaze that judges the limits of the worldly and gives "同情和悲悯" — [PDF](https://aichiu.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/7641/files/101-04%E8%AB%96%E8%AA%AC%E5%BC%B5.pdf).
- The often-repeated claim that Wang Anyi said Chang "stopped at the worldly and lacked transcendence" (超越) is not a verified quotation. The verified substance is the 虛無 / "world bigger than hers" / "emotional range larger" critique above. **Treat "lacked transcendence" as paraphrase.**

**Hu Lancheng 胡蘭成 (1944): the first "humanist" reading came from a collaborator**
- In 〈評張愛玲〉 (《雜誌》, 1944), Hu wrote 「魯迅之後有她。她是個偉大的尋求者」, saying Chang lets 「文學從政治走回人間……尋求……自由、真實而安穩的人生」 (literature returns from politics to the human world). Quoted in 張英進, who dates "張愛玲學" to 1944 — [21C PDF](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c163-201611024.pdf). Hu was then 《中華日報》 chief writer under the Wang Jingwei regime.

**柯靈 Ke Ling, 〈遙寄張愛玲〉 (1984–85)**
- 「偌大的文壇，哪個階段都安放不下一個張愛玲；上海淪陷，才給了她機會」 (quoted in 張英進, [21C PDF](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c163-201611024.pdf)).
- 張泉 quotes the same line as 「偌大的上海文壇，哪個階段都放不下一個張愛玲；上海淪陷，才給了她機會……張愛玲的文學生涯，輝煌鼎盛的時期只有兩年（1943-1945），是命中注定：千載一時，『過了這村，沒有那店』」 — [21C PDF](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/online/0305084.pdf). **Note the textual variant** (文壇 vs 上海文壇; 安放 vs 放).
- Also: 「往深處看，遠處看，歷史是公平的。張愛玲在文學上的功過得失，是客觀存在；認識不認識，承認不承認，是時間問題。」 (quoted in 張英進).

**唐文標 Tang Wenbiao (Taiwan, 1970s)**
- He called Chang's work 「上海百年租界文明的最後的表現」, a 「美麗而蒼涼」 「罌粟花」 (poppy), in 《一級一級走進沒有光的所在》 (1974; see 《張愛玲研究》, 台北：聯經, 1976). Zhang Quan says the attack triggered a Taiwanese debate that spread her fame. He also reports that Tang later revised his view: 「生活在那個年代是要有勇氣的，張愛玲的書文與其說是嘆息，不如說她是在巧笑」; 「她的平淡而固執既是抗議，也是那個時代突起的，生命力的開花結果」 — [21C PDF](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/online/0305084.pdf).

**余秋雨 Yu Qiuyu (obituary, 1995) and 張英進's rebuttal**
- Yu: 「是她告訴歷史，二十世紀的中國文學還存在着不帶多少火焦氣的一角。正是在這一角中，一個遠年的上海風韻永存。」 Zhang Yingjin's rebuttal: 「火焦氣」 (the smell of gunfire) in fact 「不時瀰漫在她的作品之中」 from 〈傾城之戀〉 to 《秧歌》 to 《小團圓》, and the "old Shanghai charm" is a 1990s nostalgia construct — [21C PDF](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c163-201611024.pdf).

**張英進 Zhang Yingjin on why "Chang studies" endures (hypercanon)**
- Abstract: 「本文從「超文典」的視角探索張愛玲作品的經久影響……本文認為「張愛玲學」不能只關心懷舊與神話，更應探索張愛玲作品內部的「後懷舊」機制。」 He argues that Chang studies 「之所以經久不衰，主要是因為張愛玲本身的超文典性」: she can be re-read through New Criticism, modernity theory, feminism and postmodernism. In other words, her endurance is partly a function of *interpretive plasticity*, not of a single humanist core — [21C PDF](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c163-201611024.pdf).

**高全之 Gao Quanzhi (《張愛玲學》)**
- His interview with Richard M. McCarthy (麥卡錫), head of USIS Hong Kong, 〈張愛玲與香港美新處──訪問麥卡錫先生〉 (《張愛玲學》, 桂林：灕江, 2015, pp. 163–70), clarifies that 《秧歌》 was *not* directly commissioned by USIA (cited in 王曉珏, Q8c) — [21C PDF](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c184-202006010.pdf).

**張小虹 Chang Hsiao-hung**
- Her NTU research project places Chang in 1930s–40s Shanghai fashion and commodity culture, and treats fashion as producing "another kind of nostalgia for history" (search-result summary of [NTU Scholars record](https://scholars.lib.ntu.edu.tw/handle/123456789/14462?locale=en); not opened in full). **Thinly sourced.**

### Inferences
- Wang Anyi's critique accepts the premise that Chang is about worldly, ordinary life. She denies that this is *care*: it is fear and nihilism holding on to trivia. She claims the larger, realist, historically grounded compassion for herself (and for Lu Xun). This is the best-sourced form of objection (f).
- There is an irony the archive should note. The first "literature returns from politics to the human world" reading (1944) came from Hu Lancheng. In the 1990s–2000s PRC press, "標榜人性" was listed as a *symptom* of collaboration. Her humanism has been praised and indicted in the same terms.
- 張英進's "hypercanon" thesis offers a third explanation, beside humanism and commerce: she endures because every new critical paradigm can find itself in her.

### Gaps
- No text of 〈世俗的張愛玲〉 itself was seen. Its quotations are taken via 胡紅英 (2021). Check them against 《王安憶讀書筆記》.

## Q7. English-language reviewers and Ang Lee

### Takeaway
English-language critics give the clearest versions of the humanist explanation:
- Haiyan Lee's "poetics of the social"
- Perry Link: she "looks past the grand political system itself and focuses instead on the lives of people"
- Kingsbury: "mental and social life in general"
- Ang Lee: she used "human nature against the backdrop of the glorious war against Japanese"

The same critics stress her cruelty and detachment, and the lack of any "unalloyed compassion." Others locate her endurance in an "aesthetics of desolation" (D. Wang) or in celebrity, sales and nostalgia (NYT 2010, AAWW).

### Cited Findings

**Ang Lee**
- Afterword to *Lust, Caution: The Story* (Anchor, 2007), reposted by Focus Features 17 Sept 2008 — [Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/2019/http://www.focusfeatures.com/article/afterword_to__em_lust__caution__em_):
  - "To me, no writer has ever used the Chinese language as cruelly as Zhang Ailing (Eileen Chang), and no story of hers is as beautiful or as cruel as 'Lust, Caution.'"
  - "She revised the story for years and years–for decades–returning to it as a criminal might return to the scene of a crime, or as a victim might reenact a trauma".
  - "But mimicry and performance are also ways we open ourselves as human beings to greater experience, indefinable connections to others, higher meanings, art, and the truth."
- NPR *Weekend Edition* with Scott Simon, 6 Oct 2007 (transcript; "(unintelligible)" is in the original) — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/15067652):
  - "the war against the Japanese all macho and glorious and you doubt a (unintelligible) patriotism. And this is a very strange short story written by our most beloved writer in modern Chinese literature - Eileen Chang. **Haiyan Lee, "Eileen Chang's Poetics of the Social"** (review of *Love in a Fallen City*), MCLC Resource Center, May 2007 — [MCLC](https://u.osu.edu/mclc/book-reviews/review-of-love-in-a-fallen-city/). I re-checked the key lines myself.

- Hierarchies of class, gender and race "intersect in such a way as to diffuse any possibility of righteous indignation, unalloyed compassion, or solidarity-making."
- "Mainland readers turn to Chang for wisdom, validation, consolation, and nostalgia, just as overseas readers have done so all along during the decades of economic 'take-off' and bourgeoisification."

### Inferences

## Q8. Counter-arguments to the humanism explanation

### (a) Apolitical escapism and indifference to national suffering under occupation

#### Cited Findings
- **The 1990s–2000s 文化漢奸 campaign in the PRC press**, as reported by 張泉 (a Beijing Academy of Social Sciences researcher writing *against* it): 陳遼, 〈「張愛玲熱」要降溫〉, 《文藝報》, 3 May 1996; 〈關於淪陷區文學評價中的幾個問題〉, 《文藝報》, 11 Jan 2000; and a follow-up in 《江蘇行政學院學報》 2001 no. 3. These charged that Chang represented 「以不同方式附敵附偽的作家」, citing her 「宣稱不談政治」, 「寫男女情愛、家長裏短的日常生活」, 「標榜人性」, 「搞色情文學」, 「作無病呻吟」 — [21C PDF](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/online/0305084.pdf).
  - **Directly relevant:** the attackers listed "標榜人性" (flaunting human nature) and writing ordinary domestic life as *evidence of collaboration*. On this view, the humanist quality itself was the indictment.
- They also cited 李子云's image of her occupation-era work as 「好像是在未經清掃的戰爭廢墟之上長出來的一株罌粟花，妖艷眩目卻又象徵著死亡」 (the image originates with 唐文標) — [21C PDF](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/online/0305084.pdf).
- **Zhang Quan's rebuttal:** 「站在中國人的立場上，堅持在日本人的統治下「不談政治」──即游離於統治者的或統治者所要求的意識形態，在政治上無疑是正確的。」 In other words, refusing politics under occupation was itself a defensible stance — [21C PDF](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/online/0305084.pdf).
- Sheng Yun (LRB): "too cosmopolitan to become a nationalist or a patriot" — [LRB](https://lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v41/n15/sheng-yun/small-feet-were-an-advantage).
- Zhang Yingjin's counter-point to the "escapism" picture: the "smell of gunfire" (火焦氣) recurs throughout her work — [21C PDF](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c163-201611024.pdf).
- Perry Lam (quoted in NYT 2010): "She wasn't a nationalist, and patriotism was not a major theme in her works" — [NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2010/10/02/arts/02iht-chang.html).
- **The charge is still live:** in 2008 PRC officials reportedly called *Lust, Caution* a "glorification of traitors and insulting to patriots." The NYT reports that the mainland cut altered the ending to make the heroine's betrayal of her country more ambiguous — [THR](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/lust-star-tang-banned-performance-106500/); [NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2010/10/02/arts/02iht-chang.html).
- Ang Lee frames the same quality as courage: official war stories "don't tell you how he feels killing someone ... I think it's the artist's job to bring them up" — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/15067652).

### (b) The collaborator's-wife stigma: was apoliticism a position or self-protection?

#### Cited Findings
- Chang's 1946 preface to the enlarged 《傳奇》 (增訂本), 〈有幾句話同讀者說〉, answered the 漢奸 label, which (per the HKU CSGC abstract of 蘇偉貞's 2023 lecture) 「當然來自與胡蘭成婚配」 — [HKU CSGC](https://www.csgc.hku.hk/?p=3283).
  - The commonly quoted wording (「我自己從來沒想到需要辯白，但最近一年來常常被人議論到，似乎被列為文化漢奸之一……我所寫的文章從來沒有涉及政治，也沒有拿過任何津貼」) is **UNVERIFIED** on any page I could fetch. One search snippet supports 「我自己從來沒有想到需要辯白」.

### Inferences (on Q8 overall)
- The strongest objections are not that Chang lacks interest in ordinary lives; nearly every critic grants that. They are:
  1. Her attention is **disenchanted rather than caring**. 蒼涼, 虛無, satire and cruelty are terms used by Hsia, Wang Anyi, Liu Zaifu and Jiang.
  2. Her **reputation was manufactured partly by politics**: Hsia's Cold War counter-canon, USIS patronage, and Hu Lancheng's Taiwan circle.
  3. Her **1990s revival** tracks Shanghai nostalgia, publishing monopoly, celebrity voyeurism and film more than any reading of her ethics.
- A fair synthesis: the "ordinary lives against grand narrative" part of the thesis is well supported. The evidence includes Chang's own 「凡人比英雄更能代表這時代的總量」, Wang Dewei's "everyday life against political history," Chow's "detail," Link's "looks past the grand political system," Haiyan Lee's "poetics of the social," and Ang Lee's "human nature against ... the glorious war." The "care" part is contested. Haiyan Lee, an admirer, explicitly says Chang's world defuses "unalloyed compassion," and Ang Lee and D. Wang foreground cruelty and distrust of attachment. "Humanism" in her case may be better described as an *unsentimental fidelity to ordinary people's weakness, self-interest and survival* than as warmth. Fu Lei's "深切的憐憫" (1944) is the strongest early evidence for genuine compassion.
- Haiyan Lee's methodological warning matters for the archive's framing. Reading Chang as subversive *because* she avoided the master narrative is a fashionable reflex. The better claim is about what she positively offers: the textures of social life.
- Endurance is over-determined. The "humanist core" is probably necessary but not sufficient. Cold War canonisation (Hsia, USIS), Taiwanese publishing and coterie (皇冠, Hu Lancheng's 三三 circle), Shanghai nostalgia and celebrity (NYT sales figures, Garbo aura), film (Ann Hui, Stanley Kwan, Ang Lee) and interpretive plasticity (張英進's "hypercanon") all carry real weight in the evidence.
