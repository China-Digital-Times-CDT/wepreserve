# Ordinary lives keep resurrecting Eileen Chang

The thesis holds, but in a narrower and harder form than "Chang lasts because she cared about ordinary people." The evidence supports this version. Chang's work makes the weak, compromised, often selfish ordinary person the unit of historical experience, in place of the hero, the nation or the class. It treats the "stable" texture of daily life as the ground that outlasts every era. She said so herself in 1944: 「正是這些凡人比英雄更能代表這時代的總量」, wording verified in two or more texts. That fidelity explains why she *persists*: why critics keep re-canonising her, why writers keep descending from her, and why her land-reform novel reads as current whenever a state manufactures hunger or orders people to celebrate. It does not explain the *timing* of the roughly nine 張愛玲熱 waves since 1943. Each was set off by something outside the texts:
- Cold War canonisation
- a Taiwanese publishing monopoly
- the post-Mao thaw
- old-Shanghai nostalgia marketing
- her solitary death
- a scandalous film
- a posthumous roman à clef
- a centenary
- this month, a recording of her voice

"Care" also overstates her temper. Her own keyword is 蒼涼 (desolation), and admirers from C. T. Hsia to Ang Lee stress cruelty and moral disturbance, not warmth. Nor does she simply "resist appropriation". Her *image* has been taken up by nearly everyone: Hu Lancheng, the US Information Service, Hsia's anti-communist canon, Taiwan's censors, the PRC heritage industry and Shanghai's 2022 "run" meme. What resists is the *work*. No state or camp has been able to use it whole, and every attempt has needed a cut:
- The PRC publishes the love stories but not 《秧歌》 or 《赤地之戀》.
- Taiwan abridged 《赤地之戀》.
- A New York editor added a burning corpse to make the English *Rice-Sprout Song* more damning.
- The 1990s PRC press had to recast her "humanism" itself as a sign of collaboration.

The contemporary resonance that sources document in Shanghai and Hong Kong runs mostly through her biography rather than her pages: the émigré who left in 1952, the voice on a 1969 tape. The A4 protests show no documented trace of her at all.

## Chang wrote the thesis herself in 1944, as desolation rather than sentiment

The best evidence for the humanist reading is first-hand. **〈自己的文章〉 ("Writing of One's Own", 1944)** was Chang's reply to Fu Lei, who as 迅雨 had published 〈論張愛玲的小說〉 in 《萬象》 in May 1944.
- **First printing.** Song Shengquan places it in **《新東方雜誌》 vol. 9 nos. 4–5, May 1944** ([宋聲泉 2020, via chinawriter repost — state-affiliated, wording only](https://www.chinawriter.com.cn/n1/2020/0909/c419384-31855145.html)). Zhang Quan gives July 1944 ([張泉, 《二十一世紀》 2003](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/online/0305084.pdf)). The date is unresolved.
- **Later texts.** It was collected in 《流言》 (December 1944). The passages below fall on pp. 21–23 of the Crown 1973 《流言》 ([郭玉雯, 《漢學研究》 2007](https://scholars.lib.ntu.edu.tw/bitstreams/aea4a80b-cdea-43cd-8b85-afa4211beeb2/download)).
- **Published English.** Andrew F. Jones, "Writing of One's Own," *Written on Water* (Columbia UP, 2005), pp. 15–22 ([Kam Louie ed., HKU Press 2012](https://hkupress.hku.hk/image/catalog/pdf-preview/9789888083725.pdf)). Leo Ou-fan Lee translated excerpts in *Shanghai Modern* (1999), p. 282.

Jones's exact wording for the 安穩/飛揚 passages was not seen. The claim that the essay was also reprinted in Hu Lancheng's 《苦竹》 is UNVERIFIED.

The essay builds the thesis in three moves. First, the "stable" (安穩) side of life is the permanent ground (底子) under its "soaring" (飛揚) side. Second, ordinary weak people represent the "total" of an age better than heroes do. Third, she refuses to write "monuments of the era". Crucially, Chang does not ignore history here. Song Shengquan counts **時代 fifteen times in the essay's first ~2,000 characters** ([宋聲泉](https://www.chinawriter.com.cn/n1/2020/0909/c419384-31855145.html)). She *re-routes* the era through ordinary lives rather than walking away from it. That is a stronger basis for the archive's thesis than "Chang was apolitical."

### The Hong Kong war memoir asks history for "irrelevant things"

**〈燼餘錄〉 ("From the Ashes")** is dated February 1944 and appeared in 《天地》 no. 5 that month. It applies the poetics to the 1941 Battle of Hong Kong, which Chang lived through as an HKU student. Oliver Stunt's published translation is in *Renditions* 45 (1996) ([CUHK Renditions](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/rct/renditions/sample/b45.html)). Every line below is **V2+** across the [HKU Convocation PDF](https://www.convocation.hku.hk/LUNGYingTai/doc/004.pdf) and [MB](http://www.millionbook.net/mj/z/zhangailing/zalj/009.htm), and several also appear in the 2025 HK Education Bureau teacher-training PDF ([EDB](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/tc/curriculum-development/kla/chi-edu/resources/lit-teacher-training/20251013_Clit_1.pdf)).
- **What she wants from history.** She has no wish to write history, 「可是私下裏總希望他們多說點不相干的話」. Stunt: "but privately I have always wished they might concern themselves a little more with irrelevant matters."
- **Where life's savour lies.** 「人生的所謂『生趣』全在那些不相干的事。」 Stunt: "Man's joie de vivre is solely to be found in life's irrelevancies."
- **Against martyrdom.** On the death of the history teacher N. H. France, she writes 「即使是『光榮殉國』，又怎樣？」 and 「一個好先生，一個好人。人類的浪費……」. Fresh rendering (this report): "Even if it were 'dying gloriously for the nation', what of it? … A good teacher, a good man. The waste of humanity…" This is a direct refusal to let a national-martyrdom frame absorb an individual death.
- **Indicting herself.** She writes of 「我們這些自私的人若無其事的活下去了。」 (HKU text; MB reads 若無其事**地**). Stunt: "We, the selfish, carried on living as if nothing had happened."
- **The closing image.** The memoir ends on 「時代的車轟轟地往前開。」 Stunt renders 時代 as "life": "And the vehicle of life rumbles on." Fresh literal rendering: "The vehicle of the times rumbles forward." A later word in that paragraph varies, 街衢 (HKU) vs 街道 (MB).

The memoir is the strongest proof that Chang's attention is **unsentimental**. She calls herself "an irresponsible, conscienceless nurse" (「我是一個不負責任的，沒良心的看護」) and lists 「我們的自私與空虛，我們恬不知恥的愚蠢」. It is also close to a programme for an archive of small lives: a writer asking historians to "say more irrelevant things".

### "Love in a Fallen City" lets a city fall to complete one woman

The 1943 novella's ending subordinates the fall of Hong Kong to one woman's private fate, with heavy irony. 「在這兵荒馬亂的時代，個人主義者是無處容身的，可是總有地方容得下一對平凡的夫妻。」 is **V2+**: a Taipei reader's transcription of the Crown text ([vocus](https://vocus.cc/article/65436152fd89780001dade29)) and [MB](http://www.millionbook.net/mj/z/zhangailing/000/026.htm). Fresh rendering: "In this age of soldiers and chaos there is no place for an individualist, but there is always room for an ordinary husband and wife." Kingsbury's English of this sentence was not seen.

The "fall" passage is also **V2+** (MB; [EDB](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/tc/curriculum-development/kla/chi-edu/resources/lit-teacher-training/20251013_Clit_1.pdf)): 「香港的陷落成全了她。」 … 「流蘇並不覺得她在歷史上的地位有什麼微妙之點。」 Its **published translation by Karen S. Kingsbury** (*Love in a Fallen City*, NYRB 2007, p. 167) is verified through [Kam Louie's volume](https://hkupress.hku.hk/image/catalog/pdf-preview/9789888083725.pdf): "Hong Kong's defeat had brought Liusu victory. … Liusu did not feel anything subtle about her place in history." The sentence that follows, 「她只是笑盈盈地站起身來，將蚊煙香盤踢到桌子底下去。」, is **V1** (MB only).

Chang's own gloss in 〈自己的文章〉 (**V2+**) keeps the edge: 「因之柳原與流蘇的結局，雖然多少是健康的，仍舊是庸俗；就事論事，他們也只能如此。」 So the accurate claim is that Chang refuses to let a national catastrophe swallow the individual, not that she celebrates the individual.

### Other essays: the same creed, mostly on one text

〈必也正名乎〉 is **V2+**, Crown 1991 via [張小虹, 《清華學報》 2020](https://thjcs.site.nthu.edu.tw/var/file/452/1452/img/4082/329626054.pdf) plus [MB](http://www.millionbook.net/mj/z/zhangailing/zalj/008.htm): 「可是世上有用的人往往是俗人。」 and her wish 「從柴米油鹽、肥皂、水與太陽之中去找尋實際的人生。」 Fresh rendering: "to look for real life among firewood, rice, oil and salt, soap, water and sunlight."

Everything else in this group is **V1**, MB only, and needs a Crown check:
- **〈談女人〉:** 「神是廣大的同情，慈悲，了解，安息。」 ([MB](http://www.millionbook.net/mj/z/zhangailing/000/009.htm)). This is Chang's *own* first-hand use of 慈悲. It parallels the V2+ 「人的神性，也可以說是婦人性」 in 〈自己的文章〉. The page should anchor "compassion" here rather than in the Hu-sourced slogan.
- **〈寫什麼〉:** 「他寫所能夠寫的，無所謂應當。」 ([MB](http://www.millionbook.net/mj/z/zhangailing/zalj/024.htm)). A refusal of assigned "correct" subjects, answering a friend who asked whether she could write proletarian stories.
- **〈《傳奇》再版的話〉 (September 1944):** 「如果我最常用的字是『荒涼』，那是因為思想背景裡有這惘惘的威脅。」 The genuine 「出名要趁早呀！」 comes from the same preface ([MB](http://www.millionbook.net/mj/z/zhangailing/zalj/030.htm)).
- **〈中國的日夜〉 (1946):** 「我真快樂我是走在中國的太陽底下。」, said while carrying groceries home, and the poem's 「補釘的彩雲的人民」 ([MB](http://www.millionbook.net/mj/z/zhangailing/zalj/041.htm)). This is her one sustained affirmation of "China", and it is built from patched clothes and street vendors, not the state.
- **〈公寓生活記趣〉:** 「我喜歡聽市聲。」 ([MB](http://www.millionbook.net/mj/z/zhangailing/zalj/005.htm)). The following clause depends on an emendation, 待意 → 詩意.
- **〈到底是上海人〉:** 「我喜歡上海人，我希望上海人喜歡我的書。」 ([MB](http://www.millionbook.net/mj/z/zhangailing/zalj/002.htm)).
- **〈我看蘇青〉:** 「我們只能各人就近求得自己的平安。」 ([MB](http://www.millionbook.net/mj/z/zhangailing/zalj/038.htm)). An earlier sentence in the same passage has a variant, 更廣大 vs 更大.

One English line belongs here. Chang wrote that American publishers found the characters of her autobiographical novels "too unpleasant, even that the poor are no better". It comes from her entry in *World Authors 1950–1970* (1975) and is attested only via [China Daily (state media), 2011](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/201109/30/WS5a29f293a3101a51ddf8de94.html), so the primary volume should be checked. It fits the memoir: Chang declined to idealise "the people".

### The most-shared "Chang" lines belong to Hu Lancheng, or to nobody

The two most famous lines are known **only from Hu Lancheng's memoir 《今生今世》**, in the chapter 〈民國女子〉 (first published 1958–59). They appear nowhere in Chang's own writings, and no letter or photograph has been produced:
- 「因為懂得，所以慈悲」, reported by Hu as her reply to his letter.
- 「見了他，她變得很低很低，低到塵埃裡，但她心裡是歡喜的，從塵埃裡開出花來。」, reported by Hu as an inscription on a photograph.

Both are **[via Hu]**, and their exact wording and context are **UNVERIFIED against Hu's text** ([Sina 2004](http://news.sina.com.cn/cul/2004-11-08/312.html); [QQ News fake-quote explainer, 2021](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20210912A0020600)). Even the state-affiliated Xinmin Weekly calls the evidence a 「孤證」 and relays that Chang told C. T. Hsia that Hu's account of her was 「纏夾得奇怪」 ([Xinmin Weekly 2022](https://m.xinminweekly.com.cn/content/17290.html), state-affiliated, claim only; the Hsia letter itself was not located). **「現世安穩，歲月靜好」**, from their 1944 marriage document, was by Hu's own account **added by him**. It is not Chang's.

A 2014 "Hu Lancheng love letter" circulating under the 懂得/慈悲 headline is a modern pastiche ([Douban 2014](https://www.douban.com/group/topic/49366596/)). The Tencent explainer lists a cluster of outright fakes ([QQ News](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20210912A0020600)):
- 「因為愛過，所以慈悲；因為懂得，所以寬容。」, which Chinese Wikiquote lists, unsourced
- 「你還不來，我怎敢老去。」
- 「人生太長，我們怕寂寞；人生太短，我們怕來不及」
- 「海上月是天上月，眼前人是心上人」
- 「我們都是戲子，在別人的故事裡，流著自己的眼淚」, adapted from 席慕蓉
- 「時間無涯，剛好趕上」, a rewrite of the genuine 〈愛〉
- several 張小嫻 lines

Chinese Wikiquote's other unsourced entries, such as 「如果你認識從前的我，也許你會原諒現在的我」 and 「我相信人，但我不相信人性」, are **UNVERIFIED and probably spurious** ([zh.wikiquote](https://zh.wikiquote.org/wiki/%E5%BC%B5%E6%84%9B%E7%8E%B2)). Two lines that are often doubted are genuine: 「笑，全世界便與你同聲笑；哭，你便獨自哭。」, quoted inside 〈花凋〉 after Ella Wheeler Wilcox ([MB](http://www.millionbook.net/mj/z/zhangailing/000/005.htm)), and 「出名要趁早」.

There is a pointed irony in the 慈悲 slogan. Per a Douban long-read, Hu himself wrote that Chang was 「慈悲布施全無，非常自私」 ([戴文采 on Douban](https://www.douban.com/group/topic/7801340/)). That is **UNVERIFIED** against Hu's text.

## Nine revival waves, each lit by an outside spark

David Der-wei Wang put the pattern in one sentence: 「以後的四十年與其說張愛玲仍在創作，倒不如說她不斷地「被」創作：被學院裡的評家學者、學院外的作家讀者，一再重塑金身。」 ([王德威〈落地的麥子不死〉](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/90955.html)). The chronology bears him out.

Some dates conflict between sources. Wen Rumin dates Ke Ling's essay to 《讀書》 1984 no. 4. Wang Xiaojue gives a first printing in 《香港文學》 no. 2 (February 1985) and then 《讀書》 in April 1985 ([王曉珏 2021](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c184-202006010.pdf)). Reports of the *Lust, Caution* cuts range from 7 to 12 minutes.

Read as a whole, the table shows **two layers that the "humanist core" thesis tends to merge**. The *persistence* layer is the scholars, the 張派 writers and the readers who return across decades. Its sources consistently name her attention to daily life and to weak, compromised people:
- Fu Lei's "deep compassion"
- Hsia's "pettiness and sadness of human endeavour"
- the 1980s mainland rediscovery of a "small history" grounded in daily life
- 周成蔭's 「政治對張愛玲是次要的。她批判的是人生跟社會」 ([RFA 2020](https://www.rfa.org/mandarin/yataibaodao/kejiaowen/jt-10012020103737.html))

The *timing-and-scale* layer is different. Every mass surge had an external trigger, and several of the best sources say plainly that mass readers came for something other than the humanism. Wen Rumin describes the 1990s fever as a four-stage process: 「先是由學術界發現和定位，然後經大眾傳媒製作並擴大到公共空間，變成文化符號，再被商界借用促銷，轉為一種時尚。」 He adds that most of her white-collar readers **missed the bleakness and cruelty** in her work ([溫儒敏 2000](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/93380.html)). A Hong Kong preface notes that popular chatter about her comes 「不因她是〈金鎖記〉或《秧歌》的作者，而是因她極不尋常的家族背景與個人經歷」 ([CUHK Press](https://cup.cuhk.edu.hk/image/data/preview/9789629966768_preface.pdf)). The writer 何曼莊 put it bluntly in 2020: 「比起作品，很多人追尋張愛玲是看到她的人生和形象」 ([RFA](https://www.rfa.org/mandarin/yataibaodao/kejiaowen/jt-10012020103737.html)).

Two waves still lean toward the thesis. Chiang and Link read the 1990s appetite as a search for an alternative to "mediocre entertainment fiction of the post-Tiananmen era". They read *Little Reunions*' 2009 success as the reaction of middle-class readers feeling "morally adrift" ([NYRB 2018](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2018/06/07/eileen-chang-before-the-revolution/)). Even the biography-driven waves feed on a moral hunger that her work answers.

## Critics concede the ordinary lives and fight over the care

Almost no serious critic denies that Chang writes ordinary lives against grand narrative. The quarrel is over what kind of attention that is. Fu Lei's founding 1944 essay is itself humanist. On Cao Qiqiao in 〈金鎖記〉 he wrote 「她战败了，她是弱者。但因为是弱者，她就没有被同情的资格了么？……作者不这么想。」 and 「作者深切的怜悯，唤引着读者的怜悯」 (quoted in [劉鋒杰 2000](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/93385.html)). Yet he dismissed 〈傾城之戀〉 as 「好似六朝的骈体，虽然珠光宝气，内里却空空洞洞」 and complained that 「除了男女之外，世界究竟还辽阔得很」. His real target was the May Fourth left's contempt for craft in favour of 「主義的論戰」 ([張泉 2003](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/online/0305084.pdf)). The Fu–Chang exchange is really an argument over whether compassion must come with tragic grandeur. Fu says yes. Chang answers with 蒼涼.

Two famous Fu Lei lines are **UNVERIFIED as exact wording**: 「《金鎖記》是張女士截至目前為止最完滿之作」 and 「技巧對張女士是最危險的誘惑」. There is also a textual crux: Fu Lei's printed comparison reads 《獵人日記》, which Liu Fengjie thinks a misprint for 《狂人日記》 and 邵迎建 does not. It should not be silently resolved.

Hsia's grounds overlap with the thesis without matching it. He praised moral seriousness through restraint: "Chang does not profess high-minded ideals," and her "registration of the inescapable pettiness and sadness of human endeavour is nearly always morally disturbing" ([LRB 2019](https://lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v41/n15/sheng-yun/small-feet-were-an-advantage)). He also called her "a profound pessimist" who "can afford at the same time to be a gay satirist" (p. 414, via [Liu Zaifu, MCLC](https://u.osu.edu/mclc/?p=4634)). His canon was openly political. 張英進 shows Hsia building Chang as an anti-canon against the leftist tradition ([張英進 2017](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c163-201611024.pdf)), and HK01 notes that Hsia *entered* her work through the anti-communist 《秧歌》, calling it 「在中國小說史上已經是本不朽之作」 ([HK01 2020](https://www.hk01.com/article/529961/)). Liu Zaifu dissents from inside the canon. He argues the best stories rest on "philosophical awareness rather than … historical awareness" and enter "a philosophical, universal, and eternal world" by transcending politics, nation and history ([MCLC](https://u.osu.edu/mclc/?p=4634)).

Other scholars supply the "everyday against History" version, which supports the thesis's anti-grand-narrative half:
- **David Der-wei Wang** places her in a tradition of 「日常生活對抗政治歷史」 and reads her rewriting as valuing "involution over revolution" ([張英進 summarising Wang](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c163-201611024.pdf); [USC listing](https://china.usc.edu/event/madame-white-book-change-and-eileen-chang-poetics-involution)).
- **Rey Chow** sets Chang's sensuous detail against the "wholeness of idealist notions like 'Man,' 'Self,' or 'China'". The wording is third-hand, via Leo Lee and Fiss ([USC Fiss PDF](https://libraries.usc.edu/sites/default/files/events/documents/Ge%CC%81raldine%20Fiss%20Response%20to%20Presentations_v2.pdf)), with independent corroboration of the substance and page numbers in 張英進. Chow's frame is feminist and deconstructive, not humanist, so it undercuts any humanism grounded in "Man".
- **Perry Link** gives the plainest English statement: Chang "looks past the grand political system itself and focuses instead on the lives of people—how they feel and behave as they adapt to what the system forces upon them" ([NYR Daily 2015](https://www.nybooks.com/online/2015/05/15/china-language-game-eileen-chang/)).
- **Karen Kingsbury**, her translator, says Chang wrote "not just something about Chinese-ness, or about one huge people's modern-day history of struggle and change, but about mental and social life in general" ([Taipei Times 2006](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/feat/archives/2006/10/15/2003331932)).
- **Ang Lee** says she "used female sexual psychology to use the human nature against the backdrop of the glorious war against Japanese", and that official war stories "don't tell you how he feels killing someone … I think it's the artist's job to bring them up" ([NPR 2007](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/15067652)).

The admirers also supply the strongest qualifications. Haiyan Lee's "poetics of the social" locates Chang's poetry "in ordinary weaknesses, small cruelties, and chance delights". In the same review she says Chang's social hierarchies "diffuse any possibility of righteous indignation, unalloyed compassion, or solidarity-making" ([MCLC 2007](https://u.osu.edu/mclc/book-reviews/review-of-love-in-a-fallen-city/)). She also issues the methodological warning the archive should heed: because Chang was marginalised, her trifles are "automatically read as subversive of the dominant ideology … But readers are drawn to her not for what she avoids writing about, but for what she does give them". Ang Lee: "no writer has ever used the Chinese language as cruelly as Zhang Ailing" ([Focus Features afterword, Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/2019/http://www.focusfeatures.com/article/afterword_to__em_lust__caution__em_)). D. Wang notes her "total lack of confidence in human attachment" ([Focus Features 2008, Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20190305001006/http://www.focusfeatures.com/article/eileen_chang_and_lust__caution)).

**The strongest counter-arguments come in three forms.**

The first denies that her attention is care. Wang Anyi, often named as her heir, grants the beauty and rejects the warmth. 「我可能永远不能写得像她这么美，但我的世界比她的大。」 And: 「她对现时生活的爱好是出于对人生的恐惧，她对世界的看法是虚无的」 (both quoted in [胡紅英 2021](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/125293.html), citing 許子東 2004 and 《王安憶讀書筆記》 2007). The claim that Wang said Chang "lacked transcendence" is paraphrase, not a verified quotation. Lin Zou goes further. Her abstract, as captured by a researcher, reads Chang's human relations as "essentially commercial" and her "aesthetics of desolation" as "a petty bourgeois construction for consumption" ([Journal of Asian Studies 2011](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-asian-studies/article/commercialization-of-emotions-in-zhang-ailings-fiction/891A5468EA4AF66AC010039322BD38DF)). That is the sharpest scholarly case that 蒼涼 sells as a 小資 consumable rather than working as humane witness. 唐文標's 1970s attack on a 「死的世界」 without 「道德的批判」 belongs here too. So does his later partial retraction, which matters: 「她的平淡而固執既是抗議，也是那個時代突起的，生命力的開花結果」 ([張泉](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/online/0305084.pdf)).

The second says her reputation was manufactured by politics and patronage:
- Hsia's Cold War canon and the USIS commissions.
- Hu Lancheng, who taught in Taiwan from 1974 and mentored the 朱 family and the 三三 group, a channel of the Taiwanese 張派 ([鈴木基子 2023](https://www.eco.nihon-u.ac.jp/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/82-03.pdf)).
- Crown's monopoly.

The third, the "hypercanon" explanation, may be the most damaging to any single-cause thesis. 張英進 argues that Chang studies endure 「主要是因為張愛玲本身的超文典性」: every new paradigm, from New Criticism to feminism to postmodernism, can find itself in her ([張英進 2017](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c163-201611024.pdf)).

The single most revealing counter-history concerns her humanism directly. It was **praised and indicted in the same terms**. The first "humanist" reading, in 1944, came from Hu Lancheng, a Wang Jingwei-regime propagandist. He wrote that she let 「文學從政治走回人間」 (quoted in 張英進). Half a century later, the PRC press campaign against the "Chang fever" (陳遼, 〈「張愛玲熱」要降溫〉, 《文藝報》, 3 May 1996, and successors) listed 「寫男女情愛、家長裏短的日常生活」 and 「標榜人性」 among the marks of writers who 「以不同方式附敵附偽」. Zhang Quan's rebuttal: refusing to 「談政治」 under Japanese rule, keeping clear of the occupier's ideology, 「在政治上無疑是正確的」 ([張泉 2003](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/online/0305084.pdf)). Her focus on private life has never been politically neutral. It has always been read as a position.

## Hunger, not doctrine, carries the land-reform novels

The two 1954 novels are the hardest test of the thesis, because they are explicitly political and were produced inside a US propaganda apparatus. The evidence suggests her method survived the commission, though not unscathed.

***The Rice-Sprout Song*** (《秧歌》) follows one family near Shanghai after land reform: the model peasant 金根, his wife 月香 and their daughter. Through them it traces hunger, a forced New Year "contribution" drive for army families, a grain riot that cadres put down with gunfire, arson, and a compulsory yangko dance ([王曉珏 2021](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c184-202006010.pdf)). It was written in English in Hong Kong in 1954, rewritten in Chinese and serialised in the USIS 《今日世界》, and published by Scribner's in 1955. Archival work finds it began as 「張愛玲的自主書寫」 and was absorbed into the USIS book programme midway ([王梅香, 《歐美研究》 2015](https://doi.org/10.7015/JEAS.201503_45%281%29.0003)).

In her Chinese postscript Chang insisted that 「《秧歌》裏面的人物雖然都是虛構的，事情卻都是有根有據的」. This is quoted by Wang Xiaojue, citing Crown 典藏版 2010 pp. 203–205; the primary text was not seen. The flagship scenes invert official texts. The riot and a cadre's despairing 「我們失敗了！」 come from a cadre's self-criticism she says she read in *People's Literature*; the hostile critic 丁爾綱 claims he could not find it ([ESWN compilation](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)), and neither side has been independently checked. The granary arson re-reads the propaganda film 《遼遠的鄉村》, where landlords and KMT agents burn the grain, as a peasant woman's revenge ([王曉珏](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c184-202006010.pdf)).

The novel's most propagandistic spectacle was not hers. **Scribner's editor asked for an explicit scene of 月香's death "to strengthen the novel's political condemnation"** (「以增強小說的政治譴責力量」). The 1955 English edition therefore adds a woman's body found sitting upright, glowing pink ([王曉珏, p. 103](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c184-202006010.pdf)).

Chang wanted the book understated. She told Hu Shih that 「《秧歌》這故事太平淡，不合我國讀者的口味」. Hu Shih answered on 25 January 1955 that it had reached 「平淡而近自然」 and that 「這本小說，從頭到尾，寫的是『飢餓』」 ([王曉珏](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c184-202006010.pdf); [ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)). Contemporary US praise survives only in Chinese back-translation. One example: the *Wisconsin Library Bulletin*'s remark that her sympathy 「甚至延伸至地方黨部領導」. The English originals were not seen, so none should be quoted in English.

***Naked Earth*** (《赤地之戀》) is more compromised. Chang told 水晶 (1973, sometimes dated 1971) and Stephen Soong that the story was "commissioned" and "outlined" by someone else ([Hsieh, Open Letters Monthly 2015](https://www.openlettersmonthlyarchive.com/olm/eileen-changs-changes-from-love-in-redland-to-naked-earth)). USIS officer Richard McCarthy insisted in 2002 that she drafted her own outline ([高全之 interview, ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)). The archives side with Chang. The project began as 徐東濱's proposal *Farewell to the Korean Front* under USIS's "China Reporting Program" ([王梅香 2015](https://doi.org/10.7015/JEAS.201503_45%281%29.0003)). Wang Meixiang calls the result 「不自由的自由書寫」.

Critics divide sharply on how much this matters:
- Liu Zaifu: in *Naked Earth* "political elements completely overwhelm artistic elements" ([MCLC](https://u.osu.edu/mclc/?p=4634)).
- Jiwei Xiao: its characters "remain abstractions" and its ending reflects a "pre-assembled storyline". She also gives it one of the best lines for the thesis: "Terror turns a thinking individual into a member of the mob" ([MCLC](https://u.osu.edu/mclc/2016/02/26/naked-earth-review/)).
- Perry Link: the grant's "significance [is] sometimes exaggerated," and "the beauty of Chang's writing makes it hard to view as anyone's propaganda". He describes the campaign language that "descends on a village like a giant cookie cutter" ([NYR Daily 2015](https://www.nybooks.com/online/2015/05/15/china-language-game-eileen-chang/)).

Two Chinese readings frame it in humanist terms. 戴晴 (1996) wrote that Chang cared only about 「各種各樣理念的行為結果，即真的發生了什麼，以及人們出於人的本性對周邊發生的一切的反應」, and kept 「對幼弱的同情」. An anonymous mainland defence argued 「與其說《秧歌》反共，不如說《秧歌》反迫害，反對任何一種制度的迫害」 ([ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)).

The novels' afterlife makes the clearest case for "resisting appropriation", and it is a case about *every* side:
- **The PRC.** Ke Ling dismissed both novels in 1985: 「致命傷在於虛假」. As of 2021 they still 「無法在中國大陸出版」 ([王曉珏](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c184-202006010.pdf)). The 2020 centenary editions came from Crown in Taiwan ([博客來](https://www.books.com.tw/products/0010848731)), and China Digital Times lists both as banned books ([CDT](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/space/%E7%A6%81%E4%B9%A6%E6%94%B6%E5%BD%95)).
- **The anti-communist side.** In Taiwan, 《赤地之戀》 touched on the KMT and Chiang Kai-shek. An abridged edition was not made public there until 1978 ([Hsieh](https://www.openlettersmonthlyarchive.com/olm/eileen-changs-changes-from-love-in-redland-to-naked-earth)), and Taiwanese writers recall it being cut and at one point banned ([古遠清, ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)). Even the camp that paid for it could not use it whole.

Two claims remain unsettled. That Chang joined a 1950 land-reform team is **UNVERIFIED**. The only verified statement is the 1968 interview line 「寫《秧歌》前，她曾在鄉下住了三四個月。那時是冬天。」 ([殷允芃, ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)), which is the interviewer's paraphrase. Her 1946 self-defence, 「我所寫的文章從來沒有涉及政治，也沒有拿過津貼」, is known here only as quoted by a hostile 2007 critic and is **UNVERIFIED** against the 增訂本 preface.

## Today's Shanghai and Hong Kong find her through her life more than her pages

The documented contemporary resonance is real but differs from what the thesis predicts. **During the spring 2022 Shanghai lockdown, Chang went viral as a "run" (润) icon, not as a chronicler of apartments.** China Digital Times records that 「上海封城期间，伴随着'润学'在网络上的流行，民国作家张爱玲突然火了，被网友戏称为'跑路天后'、'润学女神'」. The entry explains the meme by her 1952 departure and contrasts her with writers who stayed (苏青, 关露, 周瘦鹃), who 「大多没有躲过残酷的政治迫害」 ([CDT Lexicon: 润](https://20.chinadigitaltimes.net/3402.html)). The one documented *text*-based link came from the commentator 長平, writing for Deutsche Welle. He argued that people should read *The Rice-Sprout Song* because 「「秧歌」與當下上海的境遇密切相關」 ([CNA, 21 Apr 2022](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/acn/202204210239.aspx)). The DW original was not retrieved.

The search found **no evidence** of the following:
- the 傾城 pun used in lockdown posts
- 〈封鎖〉, 〈公寓生活記趣〉 or 〈中國的日夜〉 circulating in those posts
- Chang-specific deletions

A 2023 line widely shared as Chang's, 「如果孩子的出生，是为了继承自己的劳碌，恐慌，贫困，那么不生，也是一种善良。」 (simplified, as circulated), was used against youth unemployment and official calls to "eat bitterness" ([CDT 2023](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/697571.html)). It **has no traceable source in her work and is probably apocryphal**. It shows her *name* being borrowed for discontent.

For the **November 2022 A4 (white-paper) protests**, the notes found **no Chang reference of any kind**. Standard accounts and CDT's protest-era vocabulary roundup omit her ([CDT 2022 roundup](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/691063.html)). The page should not imply otherwise.

**In post-2019 Hong Kong, Chang functions as heritage rather than protest.** Institutions marked the centenary openly:
- HKU's exhibition 《百年愛玲，人文港大》 (18 November 2020 – 17 January 2021) ([HKU](https://www.hku.hk/press/c_news_detail_21929.html)).
- Ming Pao Monthly's special issue ([明報月刊](https://mingpaomonthly.com/article/details/%E5%8D%B7%E9%A6%96%E8%AA%9E/2020-09/1598427836019/%E5%BC%B5%E6%84%9B%E7%8E%B2%E8%88%87%E9%A6%99%E6%B8%AF%EF%BC%88%E6%BD%98%E8%80%80%E6%98%8E%EF%BC%89)).
- A 2021 Book Fair lecture, 「百年張愛玲：香港的傾城之戀」 ([HK01](https://www.hk01.com/%E8%97%9D%E6%96%87/646474/)).
- The Public Libraries' literary map, which still features the 2020 Crown 《秧歌》 ([HKPL](https://www.hkpl.gov.hk/tc/extension-activities/page/215653/eileen-chang.html)).

Hundreds of political titles were pulled from libraries after the NSL ([RFA 2023](https://www.rfa.org/english/news/china/hong-kong-libraries-05162023134256.html)), but no source names Chang. "Fallen city" readings of today's Hong Kong exist only loosely. The Ark Hong Kong says the city "can be said to be playing similar tunes of violence, confusion, and desolation" ([The Ark Hong Kong 2024](https://thearkhongkong.substack.com/p/review-love-in-a-fallen-city-1947)). Nicholas Jose links her to "a place now under threat" ([SRB 2021](https://sydneyreviewofbooks.com/reviews/goodbye-to-hong-kong)). No sourced op-ed explicitly equates 傾城 with the NSL, and no HKFP or SCMP piece was found.

On the mainland the split is clean. Chen Zishan called the 2020 Shanghai centenary 「不冷不熱，但已經很不錯了」 and 「官方沒說不能研究張愛玲，但也沒有把她看得很重要」. He praised her as a 「特立獨行的女作家」 who wrote daily life and love rather than emperors and generals, and he named Hu Lancheng as a bigger obstacle than the novels ([CNA, 29 Sep 2020](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/firstnews/202009290336.aspx)). This is the decisive point about appropriation. **The state-tolerated Chang is exactly the humanist Chang**, read as harmless. The humanism becomes intolerable only when it reaches land reform.

**The October 2026 Radcliffe recording** fits the biography-first pattern:
- **Origin.** Chai Jing's 17 October episode first aired fragments of a 1 April 1969 lecture of 1h12m, held at the Schlesinger Library under "Reyher". Full and partial copies then spread on Weibo and Xiaohongshu ([World Journal via udn, 19 Oct 2026](https://udn.com/news/story/7332/9823656); [虛詞, 20 Oct 2026](https://p-articles.com/heteroglossia/6275.html)).
- **Reaction.** Reactions centred on intimacy: 「終於聽到張愛玲本人的聲音了」, 「是張迷今年秋天最驚喜的禮物」. HK scholar 梁慕靈, via Ming Pao, said 「學術界都在找尋張愛玲的聲音」 ([udn](https://udn.com/news/story/7332/9823656)).
- **Content.** A secondary thread fastened on her account of literature being steered after 1949. 虛詞's unofficial working transcript gives "Marx was all right, but not Freud" and "like old clothes, no longer fit" ([虛詞](https://p-articles.com/heteroglossia/6275.html)).
- **Chai Jing's framing.** Chai's essay frames Chang's politics as 「从个人感受出发的」, recording people 「被政治浸透了」 ([Chai Jing Substack, 19 Oct 2026](https://chaijing.substack.com/p/82e)). This is the humanist thesis stated by a commentator, not by the crowd.
- **Censorship.** None reported as of 20 October. Chai's own memoir 《看見》 was recalled on the mainland in May 2025 ([CNA 2025](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/acn/202505150141.aspx)). Within days, the voice and the literary talk circulated on domestic platforms, while the political framing travelled mainly off-platform.
- **Coverage.** No major English-language outlet had covered the recording by 20 October.

The archive's existing page records the episode title as 「周恩來為什麼沒留住張愛玲？｜張愛玲錄音公開」 from the YouTube embed, but no fetched press report confirms it. A text of the lecture appears in *PMLA* 130.2 (2015) ([Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/pmla/article/abs/chinese-translation-a-vehicle-of-cultural-influence/95AF6EC0791D5F35ADF5EFA0359A27E8)). Quote that text, not the audio transcript.

## Conclusion

The verdict, component by component:

| Component of the thesis | Verdict |
|---|---|
| A humanist core of attention to individual, ordinary lives is Chang's own stated poetics | **Strongly supported** by V2+ primary text (〈自己的文章〉, 〈燼餘錄〉, 〈傾城之戀〉) |
| That attention is "care" or compassion | **Partly.** It is unsentimental and self-indicting; her word is 蒼涼; admirers stress cruelty. Her own 慈悲 (〈談女人〉) is V1. |
| It is set against grand political or national narratives | **Supported, with a correction.** She routes "the era" *through* ordinary lives rather than avoiding it, and she wrote land reform directly. |
| It explains her longevity | **Supported** for persistence, the canon and the 張派 |
| It explains the repeated resurrections | **Weak.** Every surge had an external trigger, and mass readers often came for biography and nostalgia. |
| It makes her resist appropriation | **Her image no; her work yes.** Every appropriation required an amputation. |
| It makes her read as contemporary in Shanghai and Hong Kong | **Partly documented.** The resonance runs through her life (润, the voice) more than her texts. 長平's 《秧歌》 reading is the one documented textual link. A4: none. Hong Kong: heritage. |

The refined thesis the page can defend: **Chang endures because she made the weak, self-interested, ordinary person, not the hero or the nation, the measure of an age, and she did so without sentiment. That is why every regime that has tried to claim her has had to cut her first.** The useful insight for the curator is that her humanism is *not* apolitical in effect. It has been read as a position by everyone who confronted it: Hu Lancheng praised it, the 1990s PRC press prosecuted it as collaboration, and Shanghai's censors tolerate it only when it stays in the bedroom and the apartment. Her contemporaneity is therefore strongest exactly where the state's tolerance stops, in the hungry village of 《秧歌》, and weakest where the fandom is loudest. A page that leads with "irrelevant things", the "broad bearers of this age" and the unpublished land-reform novel, rather than with 懂得/慈悲, will be both more accurate and more pointed.
