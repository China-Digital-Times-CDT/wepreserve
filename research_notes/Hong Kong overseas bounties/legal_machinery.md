## 1. NSL (30 June 2020) Article 38: extraterritorial reach, and how officials use it

### Takeaway
Article 38 extends the NSL to offences "committed against" the HKSAR from outside Hong Kong by people who are *not* permanent residents. Article 37 already covers permanent residents and Hong Kong-incorporated bodies acting abroad. Together the two articles give the law worldwide reach. Officials defend this as "protective jurisdiction". Since July 2023 they have used it as the basis for HK$1m (and later HK$200k) bounties and warrants against exiles, including foreign citizens.

### Cited Findings
- **[LAW] Art. 36:** "This Law shall apply to offences under this Law which are committed in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region." An offence counts as committed in the Region if an act or consequence of it occurs there. — [Wikisource, NSL text](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Law_of_the_People%27s_Republic_of_China_on_Safeguarding_National_Security_in_the_Hong_Kong_Special_Administrative_Region)
- **[LAW] Art. 37:** "This Law shall apply to a person who is a permanent resident of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region or an incorporated or unincorporated body such as a company or an organisation which is set up in the Region if the person or the body commits an offence under this Law outside the Region." — [Wikisource, NSL text](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Law_of_the_People%27s_Republic_of_China_on_Safeguarding_National_Security_in_the_Hong_Kong_Special_Administrative_Region)
- **[LAW] Art. 38:** "This Law shall apply to offences under this Law committed against the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region from outside the Region by a person who is not a permanent resident of the Region." — [Wikisource, NSL text](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Law_of_the_People%27s_Republic_of_China_on_Safeguarding_National_Security_in_the_Hong_Kong_Special_Administrative_Region). The exact phrase was confirmed by a search on the full sentence ([Bitter Winter](https://bitterwinter.org/hong-kong-national-security-law-article-38/); [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/151438/China-says-Article-38-follows-principle-of-protective-jurisdiction)). *Before publication, check the wording against e-Legislation (A406N). That site's JS loader blocked automated fetching.*
- **[LAW] Art. 43(3)** gives police powers of "freezing of, applying for restraint order, charging order and confiscation order in respect of, and forfeiture of property used or intended to be used for the commission of the offence, proceeds of crime, or other property relating to the commission of the offence." — [Wikisource, NSL text](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Law_of_the_People%27s_Republic_of_China_on_Safeguarding_National_Security_in_the_Hong_Kong_Special_Administrative_Region)
- **[PRC official framing]** In July 2020 a PRC Foreign Ministry spokesperson said Art. 38 "follows the principle of protective jurisdiction", which they called long-standing common-law practice, including in the US. — [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/151438/China-says-Article-38-follows-principle-of-protective-jurisdiction). A Renmin University professor quoted in China Daily linked it to Art. 8 of the PRC Criminal Law and noted it says "non-permanent residents" rather than "foreigners". — [China Daily HK](https://chinadailyhk.com/hk/article/135951) **[PRC state media]**
- **First use for bounties (3 July 2023):** warrants and HK$1m bounties for eight exiles: Ted Hui, Dennis Kwok, Nathan Law, Anna Kwok, Elmer Yuen, Christopher Mung, Finn Lau, Kevin Yam. **[GOV/official statements as reported]** Chief Executive John Lee called the warrants an "effective and just way of safeguarding national security" and said the suspects would "continue to live in fear". The Security Bureau said extraterritoriality follows "protective jurisdiction", which it called "well-recognised" in international law. — [HKFP, 4 Jul 2023](https://hongkongfp.com/2023/07/04/western-countries-decry-hong-kongs-nat-sec-arrest-warrants-as-leader-john-lee-defends-laws-extraterritorial-reach/)
- **Foreign citizens targeted:** the July 2025 "Hong Kong Parliament" warrants named Australian citizen Prof. Feng Chongyi (UTS) and US citizen Sasha Gong (ex-VOA journalist). — [Al Jazeera, 5 Aug 2025](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/8/5/hong-kong-cancels-passports-bans-financial-support-for-wanted-activists). The December 2024 round included two Canadians. — [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/world/hong-kong-arrest-warrants-bounties-activists-1.7418476)
- **Scale of warrants:** July 2023, 8 people (HK$1m each). December 2023, 5 more. December 2024, 6 more (HK$1m each). 25 July 2025, 19 people over the "Hong Kong Parliament", described as the largest tally yet: 4 already wanted at HK$1m, and HK$200,000 each on the other 15. — [US News/Reuters, 25 Jul 2025](https://www.usnews.com/news/world/articles/2025-07-25/hong-kong-issues-arrest-warrants-for-19-overseas-activists-offers-bounties-for-15); [news.gov.hk, 14 Dec 2023](https://www.news.gov.hk/eng/2023/12/20231214/20231214_183038_383.html); [GOV, 24 Dec 2024 wanted notice](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202412/24/P2024122400514.htm); [GOV, 25 Jul 2025](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202507/25/P2025072500736.htm)
- **[GOV] August 2025:** the government said it has "the responsibility" to pursue suspects for "offences endangering national security outside Hong Kong". It said the 19 are wanted "not because they 'exercised freedom of expression'". It called foreign criticism "hypocrisy with double standards". — [info.gov.hk, 9 Aug 2025](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202508/09/P2025080900338.htm)

### Inferences
- Art. 37 (residents) and Art. 38 (non-residents) together mean almost any overseas critic can be alleged to have committed an NSL offence. The practical limit is enforcement, not jurisdiction. This is why later measures (SNSO absconder regime, pressure on family, asset confiscation) act on people and property *inside* Hong Kong.

## 2. Safeguarding National Security Ordinance (Cap. 6, Ord. 6 of 2024): the "specified absconder" regime

### Takeaway
SNSO Part 7, Division 2 (ss. 89–98) lets the Secretary for Security specify, by Gazette notice, a person wanted on a national-security arrest warrant who is believed to be abroad. The Secretary can then apply some or all of seven "specified measures". Three of them are offences for *other people*: making funds available or dealing with funds, property leasing, and joint ventures, each punishable by 7 years' imprisonment. The other measures operate automatically by deeming. A six-month waiting period in the draft bill was removed by amendment before passage. I found no provision for appeal or judicial review of a specification. The only exits are revocation by the Secretary or a licence from the Secretary.

### Cited Findings
- **Dates:** bill gazetted and first read 8 March 2024. Passed 89–0 on 19 March 2024. Signed 22 March 2024. In force 23 March 2024. — [Wikipedia: SNSO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safeguarding_National_Security_Ordinance) (citing [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/european-union-un-criticise-new-hong-kong-security-law-2024-03-20/) and [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2024/03/19/china/hong-kong-second-national-security-law-passed-intl-hnk/index.html))
- **[LAW] s.89(2), who can be specified:** a person who (a) is subject to a warrant to arrest issued by a court for an offence endangering national security; (b) has been reasonably informed of the warrant or is reasonably believed to know of it; (c) has not been brought before a judge or magistrate; and (d) is reasonably believed by the Secretary for Security to be outside the HKSAR. — [Wikisource, SNSO Part 7](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Safeguarding_National_Security_Ordinance/Part_7)
- **[LAW] s.89(1):** the test is that the Secretary "reasonably believes that it is necessary for safeguarding national security". **s.89(4):** the Secretary may specify which measures apply. **s.89(5):** variation or revocation by Gazette notice. **s.89(3):** the Secretary "must revoke a specification" if the warrant is revoked or the person is brought before a judge or magistrate. — [Wikisource, SNSO Part 7](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Safeguarding_National_Security_Ordinance/Part_7)
- **Six-month wait removed:** the draft bill required six months after a warrant before someone could be specified. Government committee-stage amendments, after pro-Beijing lawmakers called the draft "too lenient", removed this, so measures can apply any time after a warrant. — [Wikipedia: SNSO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safeguarding_National_Security_Ordinance), citing [HKFP, 14 Mar 2024](https://hongkongfp.com/2024/03/14/article-23-hong-kong-seeks-to-tighten-absconder-measures-and-empower-leader-to-draft-new-laws/), [SCMP, 14 Mar 2024](https://www.scmp.com/news/hong-kong/law-and-crime/article/3255285/hong-kongs-article-23-bill-amendments-toughen-legislation-give-chief-executive-more-power) and [HKFP, 12 Mar 2024](https://hongkongfp.com/2024/03/12/article-23-hong-kong-may-tighten-measures-against-absconders-after-lawmakers-say-draft-bill-too-lenient/). The Wikisource text of s.89 contains no six-month period. **Conflict:** [Hong Kong Watch (12 Jun 2024)](https://www.hongkongwatch.org/all-posts/2024/6/12/hong-kong-authorities-cancel-passports-of-six-pro-democracy-activists-with-bounties-in-uk), an [RFA report](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/china/2024/06/china-240612-rfa04.htm) and the [UK CECC report](https://www.cecc.gov/sites/evo-subsites/cecc.house.gov/files/2024-12/2024-CH20-Human-Rights-Violations-in-the-U.S.-and-Globally.pdf) still describe a six-month minimum. These appear to be based on the draft bill. The enacted text, as reproduced on Wikisource, supports "no waiting period". Verify on e-Legislation (Cap. 6, s.89).
- **[LAW] The specified measures** (each applies only if named in a s.89(4) notice; the person is a "relevant absconder" while the specification is in force) — [Wikisource, SNSO Part 7](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Safeguarding_National_Security_Ordinance/Part_7):
  - **s.90** "Prohibition against making available funds etc. or dealing with funds etc.": no one may make funds or economic resources available to, or deal with funds of, a relevant absconder, except under a s.97 licence.
  - **s.91** "Prohibition against certain activities in connection with immovable property": no leasing, or otherwise making property available, to or from the absconder.
  - **s.92** "Prohibition in connection with joint ventures or partnerships with relevant absconders". A saving clause added at committee stage protects arrangements under contracts made before the specification. — [CGCC](https://www.cgcc.org.hk/en/temp18_detail.php?cid=2&sid=19&tid=0&tabid=0&id=325)
  - **s.93** "Suspension of qualification to practise": the qualification "is, for all purposes, to be regarded as suspended".
  - **s.94** "Permission or registration for carrying on business or for employment not in effect temporarily".
  - **s.95** "Temporary removal from office of director".
  - **s.96** "Cancellation of HKSAR passports etc.": a valid HKSAR passport is treated as cancelled when the specification is made, and new applications are treated as invalid.
- **[LAW] Penalties:** ss.90(3), 91(3) and 92(3) carry imprisonment for 7 years on conviction on indictment. **s.97:** the Secretary may grant licences, but only if satisfied the act is "reasonable and necessary, and would not be contrary to the interests of national security". **s.98:** false information to obtain a licence carries 3 years. ss.93–96 have no separate penalty because they operate by deeming. — [Wikisource, SNSO Part 7](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Safeguarding_National_Security_Ordinance/Part_7). The government's own releases confirm the 7-year figure. — [GOV, 24 Dec 2024](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202412/24/P2024122400244.htm)
- **No review route found:** the Division 2 text contains no provision for an absconder to apply to vary or revoke measures, and no appeal mechanism. The s.85 review mechanism applies only to movement-restriction orders for people on bail. — [Wikisource, SNSO Part 7](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Safeguarding_National_Security_Ordinance/Part_7). *(This is my reading of the text via a summarising tool. Have a lawyer check it before stating it categorically.)*
- **Related power, s.60 (banning organisations):** first used against overseas groups on 2 Dec 2025, when the Secretary for Security banned the Canada-based "Hong Kong Parliament" and the Taiwan-based "Hong Kong Democratic Independence Union". Membership, attending meetings, recruiting, or giving financial or other support carries up to 14 years and a HK$1m fine. — [GOV, 2 Dec 2025](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202512/02/P2025120200269.htm); [news.gov.hk](https://www.news.gov.hk/eng/2025/12/20251202/20251202_121950_359.html); [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/hong-kong/politics/article/3334881/hong-kong-invokes-security-law-first-time-ban-canada-taiwan-based-groups); [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/sup/national-security/articlecontent.php?article_id=318302&section_id=2)
- **[GOV] Stated purpose:** the measures "aim at addressing, combating, deterring and preventing acts of abscondment" and at "procuring the return of absconded persons to Hong Kong to face law enforcement and judicial proceedings". The framework "is fully in compliance with the international standard for the protection of human rights", and "the United States, the United Kingdom and Canada would also impose such measures on wanted criminals". — [info.gov.hk, 14 Jun 2024](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202406/14/P2024061400813.htm). On 12 June 2024 Secretary for Security Chris Tang said cancelling passports did not breach the ICCPR, and that the measure also bars the six from applying for passports in future. — [HKFP, 12 Jun 2024 *(snippet; full text not fetched because of 429)*](https://hongkongfp.com/2024/06/12/cancelling-hong-kong-democracy-activists-passports-not-a-breach-of-un-rights-treaty-security-chief-says/)

### Inferences
- The three measures with criminal penalties (ss.90–92) are aimed at people *in* Hong Kong, such as relatives, banks, insurers and landlords. The passport, qualification and director measures act on the absconder's legal status there. This is how a regime against people abroad gets enforcement leverage at home.

- I could not fetch the official Cap. 6 text from elegislation.gov.hk (JS loader). Section numbers come from the Wikisource transcription and agree with the government's 2024 press releases (ss.89(1), 89(4)). The definitions section (s.88 or similar) was not retrieved.

## 4. Offence of handling funds for absconders: prosecutions of relatives

### Takeaway
The first and only confirmed conviction under the SNSO absconder provisions is of **Kwok Yin-sang**, father of Anna Kwok. He was arrested on 30 April / 2 May 2025 and convicted on 11 February 2026 of attempting to deal with funds of a relevant absconder: he had tried to surrender a 1999 AIA policy taken out for his children (about HK$88,000–90,000). He was sentenced to 8 months on 26 February 2026. Before the SNSO, several arrests for funding absconders were made under NSL-era powers.

### Cited Findings
- **Arrest:** national security police arrested two men (aged 68 and 35) on suspicion of handling an absconder's assets, alleging they helped change an insurance policy and tried to withdraw its value. The 68-year-old was charged and the other was bailed. The charge cites the SNSO and **s.159G of the Crimes Ordinance** (attempt). — [GOV, 2 May 2025](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202505/02/P2025050200494p.htm) *(snippet)*
- **Trial:** on 10 October 2025 the court ruled he had "a case to answer". A witness said he had met his daughter abroad. — [HKFP, 9 Oct 2025](https://hongkongfp.com/2025/10/09/man-accused-of-attempting-to-handle-funds-belonging-to-absconder-met-with-fugitive-daughter-court-hears/); [HKFP explainer, month 64](https://hongkongfp.com/2025/11/09/explainer-hong-kongs-national-security-crackdown-month-64/) *(snippets)*
- **Conviction (11 Feb 2026):** he pleaded not guilty and did not testify. The court held that the policy balance could be "funds" ("the policy balance could be determined through calculation"). ## 5. Extradition and Interpol

### Takeaway
There is no working treaty route from the main host countries. Canada, the UK, Australia, New Zealand, Germany, France and the US all suspended or ended extradition arrangements in July–August 2020, and Beijing reciprocally suspended Hong Kong's treaties. In 2025 the UK *removed* Hong Kong's Part 2 designation but left open case-by-case requests under s.194 of the Extradition Act 2003. Critics fear this. I found no case of it being used. Interpol said in July 2023 it had received no Red Notice requests for the eight exiles. Its constitution bars political cases, and Hong Kong officials concede they cannot arrest exiles unless they return.

## 6. Convictions or proceedings in absentia

### Takeaway
I found **no criminal trial in absentia under the NSL or SNSO** for any overseas activist up to Oct 2026, and no enacted 2024 or 2025 amendment creating such trials. What exists is (a) **confiscation proceedings against absconders without a conviction**, under Schedule 3 to the NSL Art. 43 Implementation Rules (Ted Hui, Feb 2025), and (b) Ted Hui's 2022 sentence for contempt after he skipped bail.

### Cited Findings
- **Ted Hui confiscation order** (CFI, 17 Feb 2025) under Sch. 3 s.9. The test is that the court is satisfied "the absconded defendant could have been convicted". Notice of proceedings is given by newspaper (Sch. 3 s.9(3)(b)(ii)). — [GOV, 18 Feb 2025](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202502/18/P2025021800005.htm); [GOV, 24 Dec 2024](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202412/24/P2024122400244.htm)
- Ted Hui, who left on bail in 2020, "was sentenced to three years and six months' imprisonment for contempt of court" in 2022. — [GOV, 24 Dec 2024](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202412/24/P2024122400244.htm). An RFA-sourced summary describes it as an in-absentia trial with a "nearly four-year" term and says such trials had been rare. — [RFA via GlobalSecurity, 2022](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/china/2022/10/china-221003-rfa01.htm) *(snippet; minor conflict on length, so prefer the government's 3.5 years)*

### Gaps

## 7. Critique, and the government's defence

### Takeaway
UN treaty bodies and experts, foreign governments and NGOs criticise the extraterritorial claims, the bounties and the absconder measures. They call them transnational repression, collective punishment of families, and passport cancellation incompatible with ICCPR Art. 12. The HKSAR government replies that the measures comply with human rights, mirror Western practice, target "a very small number" of people, and aim to secure their return to face justice.
