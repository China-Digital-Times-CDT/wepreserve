For **thirty years**, on the evening of **4 June**, tens of thousands of Hongkongers filled the football pitches of **Victoria Park** with candles to mourn those killed in Beijing's **1989 Tiananmen crackdown**. It was the **largest annual commemoration of June 4th anywhere on Chinese soil** — the one place under the Chinese flag where the massacre could be remembered in the open, year after year. Organised by the [[Hong Kong Alliance|sites.hka]], the vigil was a fixture of the city's civic life from 1990 until 2019. Then, within two years, it was **banned, criminalised, and erased** — its site given over to a pro-Beijing carnival, its organisers imprisoned, and the very act of lighting a candle in the park turned into grounds for arrest.

This page preserves the vigil's history, and asks for your help completing its record.

## Born from the massacre (1989–1990)

As the 1989 democracy movement swelled in Beijing, Hong Kong — then a British colony — watched with alarm and solidarity. On **21 May 1989** activists founded the **Hong Kong Alliance in Support of Patriotic Democratic Movements of China** (支聯會), and in the days around the **4 June** crackdown as many as **a million** people marched in the city. The next year, on the **first anniversary in 1990**, the Alliance held the first formal **candlelight vigil** in Victoria Park; an estimated **150,000** attended. It would recur every year for three decades — a disciplined, peaceful ritual of songs, speeches, a minute's silence, and a field of flickering candles, closing with the vow to "**never forget June 4th**" (毋忘六四) and the demand to "**vindicate the 1989 democracy movement**."

## Thirty years of candlelight

Through the 1990s and 2000s the vigil endured as the emotional centre of Hong Kong's democracy movement, its crowds swelling on landmark anniversaries. Attendance figures were always contested — organisers counting high, police low — but the scale was unmistakable:

- **1990** — first anniversary vigil: ~**150,000**
- **2009** (20th anniversary): organisers **150,000**; police 62,800
- **2012**: ~**180,000**, swelled by anger over the death of dissident Li Wangyang
- **2014** (25th anniversary): ~**180,000**, on the eve of the Umbrella Movement
- **2019** (30th anniversary): **180,000+** — the **last** vigil ever held in the park

The 2019 gathering, weeks before the anti-extradition-bill protests erupted, was among the largest ever. No one there knew it would be the final one.

## The banning (2020–2021)

In **2020**, for the first time in three decades, police **banned** the vigil, citing Covid-19 restrictions — days before Beijing imposed the **National Security Law** on 30 June. Thousands **defied the ban anyway**, streaming into Victoria Park to light candles; some two dozen activists — among them [[Joshua Wong|faces-of-the-crackdown.joshua-wong]] and Alliance chair **Lee Cheuk-yan** — were later prosecuted and jailed for taking part in or inciting the "unauthorised assembly."

In **2021** the ban returned, now backed by the NSL. Police **sealed off Victoria Park** behind barriers and deployed thousands of officers to keep mourners out. On the morning of 4 June, Alliance vice-chair **[[Chow Hang-tung|faces-of-the-crackdown.chow-hang-tung]]** was **arrested** for promoting the banned vigil. That September, under mounting national-security pressure, the **Alliance voted to disband**; its leaders were remanded in custody, its assets frozen, and its [[8964 Museum|extra.8964]] driven online and overseas.

## Erasure (2022–)

![Riot police ring an empty, sealed-off Victoria Park on the June 4th anniversary, 2021](/assets/vigil-victoria-park-empty-2021.jpg)
*The same football pitches, sealed and empty on the 2021 anniversary, ringed by police. Photo: Studio Incendo, [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), via [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:6489_memorial_2021_%E2%80%93_Empty_fields_Victoria_Park.jpg).*

From **2022** the erasure became deliberate. The football pitches where the candles once burned were handed each June to a **pro-Beijing "Hometown Market" carnival**, filling the site with food stalls and patriotic displays on the anniversary. Heavy police patrols swept Causeway Bay; officers stopped, searched and **detained people merely for mourning** — carrying an electric candle, holding flowers, wearing a **Goddess of Democracy** T-shirt, or displaying the number **8964**. Performance artists were escorted away; the owner of a car with a "US 8964" plate shipped it abroad after harassment.

Yet the commemoration did not die — it **scattered**. As Victoria Park fell silent, June 4th vigils multiplied across the diaspora: in **2025**, more than **30 events in 20 cities** — from Taipei and London to Vancouver and Washington — kept the candles lit beyond Beijing's reach. And from behind bars, Chow Hang-tung marked each anniversary with a **hunger strike**.

## The keepers of the flame

The vigil's disappearance is inseparable from the fate of the people who ran it. In **2026** the [[Hong Kong Alliance|sites.hka]] and its leaders were convicted and jailed for **incitement to subversion** — **[[Chow Hang-tung|faces-of-the-crackdown.chow-hang-tung]]** (7 years 3 months), **Lee Cheuk-yan** (7 years) and founding member **Albert Ho** (5 years 2 months) — the state treating three decades of peaceful remembrance as a crime against itself. The Alliance's Tiananmen relics were scattered too: its **[[Goddess of Democracy and Pillar of Shame|statues]]** were removed and seized, and its museum survives only online, from overseas (the [[8964 Museum|extra.8964]]).

## Help preserve the vigil — a call for submissions

The physical vigil is gone, and inside Hong Kong even remembering it now carries risk. That makes the **record** more precious. We are gathering materials to preserve the memory of the Victoria Park vigil and its diaspora successors, and we invite you to contribute:

- **Photographs and video** of any June 4th vigil — Victoria Park or overseas, any year. Wide crowd shots, candles, banners, stage programmes, the sea of lights.
- **Personal memories and oral histories** — your first vigil, why you went, what it meant, what changed.
- **Ephemera** — programmes, leaflets, candle holders, badges, banners, songbooks, tickets, photographs of the annual booths.
- **Diaspora records** — footage, posters and accounts from vigils held abroad since 2020.

If you have material to share, or can help preserve it, please see **[[Participation|participation]]** and **[[How to archive|howto]]** — and take care to protect your identity when you do (Signal, Tor, anonymised files). Even a single photograph helps keep the candlelight from going dark.
