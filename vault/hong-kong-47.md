The **Hong Kong 47** (民主派初選案) is the largest single prosecution ever brought under the 2020 **National Security Law** — a case that put nearly the entire front rank of the city's opposition in the dock at once. In the winter of 2021, **47 pro-democracy figures** — former lawmakers, district councillors, unionists, a journalist, students and academics — were charged with **conspiracy to commit subversion** for organising or standing in an **unofficial primary election**. Their alleged crime was to have tried to win a majority in the Legislative Council through the ballot box. By the time the courts were done, **45 of the 47 had been convicted** and jailed for terms of up to **10 years**, in a trial held **without a jury** before judges hand-picked for national-security cases.

## The "35+" plan

In July 2020, weeks after the NSL took effect, the pro-democracy camp held a **primary election** to pick a united slate for that autumn's Legislative Council poll. Despite official warnings that it might breach the new law, **more than 600,000 Hong Kong voters** turned out over the weekend of **11–12 July 2020** to choose candidates.

The strategy behind it — dubbed **"35+"** — was authored by legal scholar **Benny Tai Yiu-ting** (戴耀廷), a co-founder of the 2014 Occupy movement. The opposition had never held a majority in the partly-elected legislature; Tai argued that if democrats could win **35 or more** of the 70 seats, they could use the legislature's constitutional powers to **veto the government budget**. Under the Basic Law, a twice-rejected budget would force the Chief Executive either to dissolve the legislature or to **resign** — leverage the camp hoped to trade for the 2019 protest movement's "five demands," including universal suffrage.

Prosecutors cast this plan not as ordinary politics but as a plot to **paralyse the government** and "**overthrow**" the constitutional order. The trial judges agreed, describing the scheme as a "**constitutional mass destruction weapon**" aimed at toppling the Hong Kong government.

## The trial and verdict

The 47 were charged on **28 February 2021** and most were **denied bail**, held on remand for years before and during their trial. Of the 47, **31 pleaded guilty** and **16 pleaded not guilty**, contesting a case heard from early 2023 by **three designated national-security judges** rather than a jury.

On **30 May 2024**, the court **convicted 14** of the 16 who had fought the charge and **acquitted two** — barrister **Lawrence Lau Wai-chung** (勞偉聰) and district councillor **Lee Yue-shun** (李予信). Together with the 31 who pleaded guilty, **45 of the 47 stood convicted**. The Department of Justice, unhappy with the two acquittals, **appealed** them.

## The sentences (November 2024)

On **19 November 2024** the court handed down sentences ranging from **4 years 2 months to 10 years**, sorting the defendants into tiers by their role — "principal organisers," active participants, and others. Those who pleaded guilty received the customary one-third discount; four who **testified for the prosecution** (Au Nok-hin, Andrew Chiu, Ben Chung and Mike Lam) had their terms cut further.

## The appeals

- **23 February 2026** — The **Court of Appeal rejected the appeals of all 12** activists who had challenged their convictions or sentences, endorsing the trial court's "constitutional mass destruction weapon" reasoning. In the same ruling it **dismissed the Department of Justice's appeal** against Lawrence Lau's acquittal, upholding his not-guilty verdict. Amnesty International called the decision "a missed opportunity to start restoring justice" ([HKFP](https://hongkongfp.com/2026/02/23/breaking-court-rejects-appeals-of-all-12-activists-in-landmark-hong-kong-47-national-security-case/) · [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/2/23/hong-kong-court-rejects-appeal-in-landmark-subversion-case) · [Amnesty](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2026/02/hong-kong-rejected-appeals-in-hk-47-case-a-missed-opportunity-to-start-restoring-justice/)).
- **10 August 2026** — The Court of Appeal granted **11 of the jailed democrats leave to appeal to the Court of Final Appeal**, the city's apex court — the **first time** the case's core national-security questions will be tested at that level. The three appellate judges, led by High Court Chief Judge **Jeremy Poon**, said the appeals "merit the consideration by the Court of Final Appeal." Among the eleven are **Gwyneth Ho** and **Leung Kwok-hung** ([HKFP](https://hongkongfp.com/2026/08/10/hong-kong-47-11-jailed-democrats-granted-permission-to-appeal-to-top-court/)).

By early 2026, some of the shorter-sentenced defendants — credited with years already spent on remand — had begun to be **released**, even as the longest-serving face years still to run and the final appeal remains pending.

## Why it matters

The case criminalised the ordinary machinery of opposition politics — a primary, a manifesto, a plan to win seats and use them. Prosecutors did not allege violence; the "subversion" lay in the **intention to win a legislative majority and use its budget veto**. That theory, now upheld on appeal, marks how far the NSL has redrawn the limits of dissent: the same years that saw the [[Hong Kong Alliance|sites.hka]] convicted for a Tiananmen slogan, [[jimmy-lai]] jailed for his newspaper, and the city's [[bookshops raided|book-censorship]], also saw an entire pro-democracy generation — its scholars, students, unionists and reporters — imprisoned for contesting an election.
