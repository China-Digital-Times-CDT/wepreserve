# The human cost of Hong Kong's overseas bounties: families, associates and diaspora communities (July 2023 – October 2026)

Scope note for the report writer: these notes cover publicly reported measures and incidents only. **Latest information found is dated 18 June 2026** (UK sentencing in the HKETO case), with the CIVICUS round-up dated 10 June 2026. Searches for July–October 2026 developments (new relative questionings, new letters, appeals) found nothing; treat the period after June 2026 as **not covered**, not as "nothing happened".

Safety handling applied throughout: I have stripped housing estates, police-station names, towns and neighbourhoods that appear in source reporting (several sources name the English town where Carmen Lau lived and the Hong Kong districts where relatives live; **do not reproduce these**). Cities appear only where the person is publicly identified with that city in the cited source (Kevin Yam – Melbourne; Ted Hui – Adelaide). Relatives are given by relationship, except Kwok Yin-sang (Anna Kwok's father), who is named in all mainstream reporting because he was charged, convicted and jailed.

Bounty-count context (needed to read the rest): 8 people in July 2023; 5 more in December 2023; 6 more in December 2024 (19 total, per [HRW, 4 May 2025](https://www.hrw.org/news/2025/05/04/hong-kong-targeting-exiled-activists-families-escalates)); 19 more in July 2025 over the "Hong Kong Parliament" election (4 at HK$1m, 15 at HK$200,000), giving 34+ by late 2025 ([Reuters via MarketScreener, 12 Dec 2025](https://uk.marketscreener.com/news/exiled-hong-kong-democracy-activists-say-they-re-targeted-by-fake-sexualised-images-ce7d50dbdf8ff32c); [EU SEE/Hivos alert, 20 Oct 2025](https://eusee.hivos.org/alert/hong-kong-police-summon-family-members-of-two-additional-hong-kong-parliament-activists-for-questioning/)).

## 1. Relatives questioned, detained or prosecuted in Hong Kong

### Takeaway
Since July 2023, Hong Kong national security police have repeatedly taken in parents, siblings, in-laws, cousins, aunts and uncles of wanted exiles for questioning. HRW says "dozens" of relatives have been affected, and some activists say the summonses are timed to their advocacy. The first prosecution of a relative came in April 2025, when Anna Kwok's father was arrested over an insurance policy he had bought for her as a toddler. He was convicted on 11 February 2026 and jailed for eight months on 26 February 2026. HRW calls this the first conviction of a family member over an exile's peaceful advocacy.

### Cited Findings

**Overall pattern**
- HRW: authorities "have sought to intimidate dozens of their family members, primarily by interrogating them"; HRW calls the targeting of relatives "collective punishment, prohibited by international human rights law" — [HRW, 4 May 2025](https://www.hrw.org/news/2025/05/04/hong-kong-targeting-exiled-activists-families-escalates); [HRW, 11 Feb 2026](https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/02/11/hong-kong-conviction-of-activists-father-a-grim-milestone)
- More than 20 relatives and friends of the first eight wanted activists were questioned between the 3 July 2023 bounty announcement and mid-September 2023 — [HKFP, 12 Sep 2023](https://hongkongfp.com/2023/09/12/self-exiled-hong-kong-democrat-ted-huis-in-laws-questioned-by-national-security-police-reports/) (figure as summarised in search results; the article itself was not fetched)
- 87 international and diaspora groups signed a joint statement in May 2025 condemning the arrests of relatives and calling for the charges to be dropped — [HRW, 7 May 2025](https://www.hrw.org/news/2025/05/07/hong-kong-87-groups-condemn-arrests-activists-relatives); [Freedom House joint statement](https://freedomhouse.org/article/joint-statement-civil-society-groups-hong-kong-governments-targeting-exiled-activists)

**The Kwok Yin-sang prosecution (verified across HRW, Reuters, AP/NBC, NPR)**
- Arrested on 30 April 2025 under section 90 of the Safeguarding National Security Ordinance (Article 23), which criminalises direct or indirect dealings with the funds of an "absconder", and charged on 2 May 2025 — [HRW, 11 Feb 2026](https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/02/11/hong-kong-conviction-of-activists-father-a-grim-milestone)
- The case concerned an AIA insurance policy he bought for his daughter when she was two. Prosecutors said he tried to cancel it in 2025 and withdraw about HK$88,609 (~US$11,342). Anna Kwok says the policy was never in her name — [Reuters via WDEZ, 26 Feb 2026](https://wdez.com/?p=914130); [AP via NBC, 11 Feb 2026](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/asia/father-us-based-hong-kong-activist-convicted-national-security-law-rcna258491)
- Pleaded not guilty and did not testify. Convicted on 11 Feb 2026 at West Kowloon Magistrates' Court. The acting principal magistrate found he "must have known" his daughter was an absconder and was trying to handle her assets. Defence counsel argued there was no evidence the money was meant for her and asked for 14 days — [AP via NBC, 11 Feb 2026](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/asia/father-us-based-hong-kong-activist-convicted-national-security-law-rcna258491)
- Sentenced to **eight months** on 26 Feb 2026. The offence carries up to 7 years, but magistrates' courts normally impose at most 2. He is the first person charged and convicted under this offence — [Reuters via WDEZ, 26 Feb 2026](https://wdez.com/?p=914130); [NPR, 26 Feb 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/02/26/nx-s1-5727437/father-activist-sentenced-hong-kong)
- Magistrate Andy Cheng: "There is no such thing as … collective punishment"; the case "has absolutely nothing to do with whether the defendant and the fugitive are family" — [Reuters via WDEZ, 26 Feb 2026](https://wdez.com/?p=914130)
- HRW (Elaine Pearson): "Prosecuting a democracy advocate's parent is an unlawful form of collective punishment." — [HRW, 11 Feb 2026](https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/02/11/hong-kong-conviction-of-activists-father-a-grim-milestone). US House China Select Committee chair John Moolenaar called it "reprehensible" — [House Select Committee](https://chinaselectcommittee.house.gov/media/press-releases/moolenaar-statement-on-the-ccp-s-reprehensible-prosecution-of-dissident-s-father)
- Age is given as 68 (HRW) or 69 (AP/Reuters/NPR at sentencing). Both may be correct at different dates.

**Financial measures touching families**
- In February 2025 a High Court order allowed the confiscation of HK$800,000 of Ted Hui's assets. The government said he had transferred nearly HK$2.5m to his mother and wife as "gifts" around the time he left in December 2020. Hui called the order "absurd" — [SCMP](https://amp.scmp.com/news/hong-kong/law-and-crime/article/3299071/hong-kong-government-slams-wanted-activist-criticising-hk800000-confiscation); HRW describes it as confiscation "from the family" — [HRW, 11 Feb 2026](https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/02/11/hong-kong-conviction-of-activists-father-a-grim-milestone). (This is separate from the December 2020 freezing of his parents' and wife's accounts, which predates the scope — [HKFP, 7 Dec 2020](https://hongkongfp.com/2020/12/07/hsbc-re-freezes-accounts-belonging-to-family-of-exiled-democrat-ted-hui-amid-hong-kong-police-money-laundering-probe/).)
- Passports of 13 wanted activists were cancelled in June and December 2024 — [HRW, 4 May 2025](https://www.hrw.org/news/2025/05/04/hong-kong-targeting-exiled-activists-families-escalates)

### Inferences
- Summonses follow the bounty announcements (July 2023, December 2023, December 2024, July 2025) and specific advocacy moments, such as the Feb 2025 London embassy protest and the April 2025 US sanctions. This matches the activists' claim that the questioning is meant as a deterrent rather than an investigation, but it is an inference from timing.
- The Kwok case turns the Article 23 "absconder funds" provisions into a direct criminal risk for relatives. Any family financial tie, even one set up decades earlier, can become an offence. The eight-month sentence is short against the 7-year maximum, but the precedent matters more than the term.
- Questioning extends beyond close family to cousins, in-laws, aunts and uncles, and former colleagues.

## 2. Anonymous letters and leaflets to neighbours and employers abroad

### Takeaway
In February–March 2025, letters copying Hong Kong police "wanted" notices reached neighbours of Carmen Lau and Tony Chung in the UK and residents near places linked to Kevin Yam in Melbourne. The letters offered HK$1 million for information or for taking the person "to the Chinese embassy". Australia raised the letters with Beijing, and UK counter-terror policing gave assurances. In 2025 the campaign escalated into sexualised, AI-altered images: letters to Lau's former neighbours (around Christmas 2025, posted from Macau) and a poster of Ted Hui's wife emailed to his former Australian workplace (August 2025). No arrests have been reported in any country. Hong Kong's government denies sending anonymous letters.

### Cited Findings

**UK – Carmen Lau and Tony Chung (Feb–Mar 2025)**
- At least five of Lau's neighbours reported receiving letters, according to her MP Joshua Reynolds. He showed NBC three identical posters with her photo, address, date of birth, ethnicity, build and height. They offered HK$1m (~US$130,000) for information or for taking her to the Chinese embassy, with tips to an email or a Hong Kong Police UK WhatsApp number. The envelopes carried Hong Kong stamps — [NBC News, 1 Mar 2025](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/world/uk-bounty-hong-kong-democracy-activist-china-rcna194068)
- Tony Chung's neighbours received letters with the same wording, which included a Hong Kong national security police email for "claiming the reward". One letter read: "A reward of one million Hong Kong dollars is being offered" — [RFA, 5 Mar 2025](https://www.rfa.org/english/china/2025/03/05/china-uk-hong-kong-wanted-activists-letters/)
- UK Security Minister Dan Jarvis told MPs on 4 March 2025: "We have received assurances from counter-terror policing". He said the government would not tolerate attempts to "coerce" critics. ### Inferences
- The Macau and Hong Kong postmarks and email origins, the wording copied from official notices, and the use of the HK police email/WhatsApp point to an organised campaign. No government has publicly attributed it, and Hong Kong denies involvement.
- The move from "wanted" posters to sexualised deepfakes of women activists (Lau) and an activist's wife (Hui) points to gendered tactics aimed at humiliation and social isolation, not only fear.

## 3. Other transnational repression: surveillance, harassment, prosecutions abroad

### Takeaway
The best-documented case is the UK prosecution of Bill Yuen Chung-biu, office manager of the Hong Kong Economic and Trade Office in London, and Peter Wai Chi-leung, a Border Force officer. They were convicted at the Old Bailey on 7 May 2026 of assisting a foreign intelligence service under the National Security Act 2023, and jailed on 18 June 2026 for 8 and 10 years respectively. Prosecutors said the operation surveilled UK-based Hong Kong dissidents, whom Hong Kong-linked individuals called "cockroaches", and misused Home Office databases. Other reported harassment includes rape and death threats online, a reported online campaign urging far-right attacks, a Canadian politician's suggestion that people claim Joe Tay's bounty, and suspected surveillance.

### Cited Findings

**UK: HKETO / National Security Act case (R v Yuen and Wai)**
- CPS: Chung Biu Yuen, 65, a former Hong Kong police officer working at HKETO London, was jailed for 8 years on 18 June 2026 for assisting a foreign intelligence service (s.3 NSA 2023). Chi Leung Wai, 40, a former UK Border Force officer and City of London special constable, was jailed for 10 years for the same offence plus misconduct in public office — [CPS, updated 18 Jun 2026](https://www.cps.gov.uk/cps/news/updated-sentence-cps-secures-convictions-hong-kong-unlawful-surveillance-case)
- CPS: the defendants researched UK-based dissidents, "called 'cockroaches'" by Hong Kong-linked individuals, seeking the cars they drove, where they lived and their social media accounts. Wai misused Home Office systems off duty and shared sensitive data. CPS's Frank Ferguson: "These convictions send a clear message." — [CPS](https://www.cps.gov.uk/cps/news/updated-sentence-cps-secures-convictions-hong-kong-unlawful-surveillance-case)
- **Foreign-interference counts (s.13):** CPS says the jury "could not reach verdicts". Wikipedia says both were "acquitted". **Treat CPS as authoritative (hung jury)**. The Standard (HK) and a Substack analysis also say no verdict was reached. Retrial status not reported — [CPS](https://www.cps.gov.uk/cps/news/updated-sentence-cps-secures-convictions-hong-kong-unlawful-surveillance-case); contra [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Hong_Kong_trade_office_spy_case)
- Verdicts on 7 May 2026 (10–2 majority on the s.3 count). ## 4. In their own words: first-person testimony

### Takeaway
The people targeted describe three things. They cut contact with family pre-emptively to protect them (Law, Cheng, Hui, Kwok, Chung Kim-wah). They feel the pain of seeing relatives used as leverage (Cheng watching his father "dodging the camera"; Kwok calling it "guilt by blood… hostage taking"). And they refuse to stop (Lau, Yam, Chung, Cheung, Kwok).

### Cited Findings

## 5. Impact on the wider diaspora

### Takeaway
A Hong Kong Watch survey of 1,000+ Hong Kongers in the UK (May 2026) found that 66% feel at risk of transnational repression and 86% believe taking part in public events in the UK puts their family in Hong Kong at risk. Rights groups describe self-censorship, mistrust and infiltration fears across the roughly 220,000 BNO-route arrivals.

### Cited Findings
- 66% feel at risk of TNR (17% "majorly"); 16.8% personally experienced infiltration of a Hong Kong group by hostile actors in the past year; 86% said participating in UK public events puts family in Hong Kong at risk. Sample: over 1,000 Hong Kongers in the UK. Fieldwork dates not stated — [Hong Kong Watch, 11 May 2026](https://www.hongkongwatch.org/all-posts/2026/5/11/new-hong-kong-watch-survey-research-on-tnr-calls-for-enhanced-foreign-interference-measures)
- Thomas Benson (HKW): "transnational repression is affecting broad swathes of the Hong Kong diaspora"; "Many ordinary Hong Kongers living in the UK fear the impact on their families" — [Hong Kong Watch, 11 May 2026](https://www.hongkongwatch.org/all-posts/2026/5/11/new-hong-kong-watch-survey-research-on-tnr-calls-for-enhanced-foreign-interference-measures); commentary: [The Diplomat, May 2026](https://thediplomat.com/2026/05/transnational-repression-of-hong-kongers-in-the-uk-is-disturbingly-common/)
- HKW is calling for China to be placed on the enhanced tier of the UK's Foreign Influence Registration Scheme — [Hong Kong Watch, 11 May 2026](https://www.hongkongwatch.org/all-posts/2026/5/11/new-hong-kong-watch-survey-research-on-tnr-calls-for-enhanced-foreign-interference-measures)
- Hong Kong Democracy Council evidence to Parliament: an "atmosphere of fear has fueled self-censorship, mistrust, hesitation and a pervasive sense of insecurity within the community" — [HKDC written evidence TRUK0070, UK Parliament JCHR](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/137989/pdf/)
- About 220,000 Hong Kongers have settled in the UK via the BNO visa — [RFA, 5 Mar 2025](https://www.rfa.org/english/china/2025/03/05/china-uk-hong-kong-wanted-activists-letters/)
- G7 members and others issued a joint statement (2025) calling bounties on people "exercising their freedom of expression" transnational repression — [Globe and Mail, 13 Aug 2025](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/world/article-hong-kong-police-question-father-of-canada-based-activist-targeted/). The UK and US condemned the July 2025 bounties — [Al Jazeera, 26 Jul 2025](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/7/26/uk-blasts-hong-kong-rewards-for-help-to-catch-activists-overseas)
- Hong Kong's government has said it will pursue absconders "for life" and cut off funding sources — [RFA, 5 Mar 2025](https://www.rfa.org/english/china/2025/03/05/china-uk-hong-kong-wanted-activists-letters/); [ABC, 18 Mar 2025](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-03-18/melbourne-residents-receive-letter-offering-money-kevin-yam/105065392)

### Inferences
- The 86% figure suggests the families-as-leverage tactic reaches far beyond the 34 named targets. Ordinary diaspora members, not just bounty targets, appear to weigh family safety before taking part in public life.
