# Waves of Eileen Chang (張愛玲) revival, 1943–2026, and what caused each

Source-quality notes for the report writer:
- **Sixth Tone** is owned by Shanghai United Media Group (state-owned). It is used below only for quoted views of named scholars and for broad framing, flagged **[state-owned outlet]**. **The Paper / 中国作家网 (China Writers Association)** were deliberately not used as authorities.
- **aisixiang.com (爱思想)** is a mainland academic-essay aggregator. It reprints peer-reviewed or newspaper scholarship (Wen Rumin, Wang Weiping & Ma Lin, David Der-wei Wang). The original venues are given where known.
- NYT, the Paris Review and Asia Society blocked fetching. The NYT 1995 obituary is cited from a verbatim reproduction on Douban, flagged as such.

## 1. 1940s Shanghai fame (1943–45): rapid rise under the Japanese occupation, and what readers responded to

### Takeaway
Chang went from her 1943 debut to bestseller within about a year. *Romances* (傳奇, 1944) sold out quickly, and the leading critic Fu Lei wrote at length on her within months. Contemporary critics answered her technique, her imagery and her sympathy for flawed characters. Hu Lancheng, her future husband, specifically praised her "underlying sympathy for her characters." From the start, though, her fame was also tied to the wartime, occupation-era literary scene and to her romance with a collaborator.

### Cited Findings
- In 1943 Chang gave manuscripts to the editor Zhou Shoujuan (周瘦鵑). With his backing she "became Shanghai's most popular new writer"; *Romances* (1944) "instantly became a bestseller in Shanghai"; the essay collection *Written on Water* (流言) followed in 1945. — [Wikipedia: Eileen Chang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eileen_Chang) (tertiary)
- 《傳奇》 became the best-selling book in Shanghai literary circles soon after it came out. Readers and critics praised her talent and technique. — [王卫平、马琳〈张爱玲研究五十年述评〉, 《学术月刊》1997 no. 11, via aisixiang](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/93502.html)
- Fu Lei (傅雷, pen name 迅雨) published 〈論張愛玲的小說〉 in 《萬象》 in May 1944. He called 〈金鎖記〉 "張女士截至目前為止最完滿之作" and criticized 〈連環套〉 and the imbalance of 〈傾城之戀〉. Hu Lancheng's 〈評張愛玲〉 appeared in 《雜誌》 the same month and stressed her creative sources and her underlying sympathy for her characters. — [Wang & Ma 1997](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/93502.html)
- Chang "made her debut as a prodigy in the 1940s in Japanese-occupied Shanghai." — [Sheng Yun, "Small Feet Were an Advantage," LRB 41:15, 1 Aug 2019](https://lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v41/n15/sheng-yun/small-feet-were-an-advantage)
- "Chang quickly found a large following." — [Louisa Chiang & Perry Link, "Before the Revolution," NYRB, 7 Jun 2018](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2018/06/07/eileen-chang-before-the-revolution/)
- She married Hu Lancheng, who was branded a traitor for collaborating with the Japanese, in a private 1944 ceremony. They divorced in 1947. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eileen_Chang)
- Her contemporaries "misread" her, and she "suffered the jabs of fellow intellectuals" over the romance with a man publicly known as a Japanese collaborator. — [Antoaneta Bezlova, IPS, 26 Nov 2007](https://www.ipsnews.net/2007/11/culture-china-lust-caution-politically-incorrect-box-office-grosser/)
- A CUHK Press preface says that in the post-war Shanghai tabloids she was gossiped about as the wife of "胡「逆」蘭成" and as a 「吉普女郎」 (a "jeep girl" consorting with American GIs). — [〈序：課室內的張愛玲〉, CUHK Press, ISBN 9789629966768 (2015), preview PDF](https://cup.cuhk.edu.hk/image/data/preview/9789629966768_preface.pdf). The preface's author says he met Chang once and helped her find work. That is consistent with Joseph S.M. Lau 劉紹銘, but the author's name is not confirmed from the preview.

### Inferences
- Even the earliest serious critics (Fu Lei, Hu) responded to craft and to her sympathy for weak, compromised people. That supports the humanist reading. But her celebrity was entangled with scandal (Hu, tabloid gossip) from the very first wave.

### Gaps
- No sourced print-run figure for *Romances* 1944. The often-repeated claim that it "sold out in four days" was **not** found in any source checked.
- No contemporary reader-response data, such as letters or magazine circulation, was found.

## 2. After 1949: marginalization and erasure on the mainland

### Takeaway
Chang stayed in Communist Shanghai until 1952, then left for Hong Kong. There she wrote two anti-communist novels commissioned by the US Information Service. Her work then vanished on the mainland for roughly 30 years and was banned "until the 1980s." The sources give three causes: the "petit bourgeois" label, the anti-communist novels, and the Hu Lancheng collaborator taint. Some sensitivity reportedly persisted into 2020.

### Cited Findings
- "張愛玲這位四十年代在上海名噪一時的才女，解放後差不多三十年時間，其作品在大陸銷聲匿跡。" — [温儒敏〈「张爱玲热」的兴发与变异——对一种接受史的文化考察〉, 《中华读书报》 27 Dec 2000, via aisixiang](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/93380.html)
- For about 30 years after 1949 no research papers on her were published on the mainland, and her name did not appear in several major literary histories. — [Wang & Ma 1997](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/93502.html)
- "Her works were banned in China until the 1980s." — [Chiang & Link, NYRB 2018](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2018/06/07/eileen-chang-before-the-revolution/)
- Her works were "regarded as full of petit bourgeois sentiment" and "disappeared." By the late 1980s her name "had been largely forgotten on the Chinese mainland for almost 40 years." — [Cai Yiwen, Sixth Tone, 29 Dec 2020](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1006607) **[state-owned outlet]**
- Her works "had been suppressed in mainland China until recently, largely because two novels, *Rice Sprout Song* and *The Naked Earth*, criticized the Communists." — [Robert McG. Thomas Jr., NYT obituary, 13 Sep 1995](https://www.nytimes.com/1995/09/13/obituaries/eileen-chang-74-chinese-writer-revered-outside-the-mainland.html), text read via a [Douban reproduction](https://site.douban.com/137987/widget/notes/6446811/note/671076989/) because NYT blocked the fetch.
- "In 1952, she moved to Hong Kong, and soon published two anti-communist novels in English," both "commissioned by the United States Information Service." After 1949 her "relationship with a Japanese collaborator had also to be taken into account." — [Sheng Yun, LRB 2019](https://lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v41/n15/sheng-yun/small-feet-were-an-advantage)
- She left in 1952, "realizing her writing career in Shanghai was over." *Naked Earth* (1954) was written at USIS's direct request from a plot outline its agents supplied. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eileen_Chang)
- In 2020, official attitudes were still described as cautious. Per Taiwan's CNA, this was partly because of the land-reform novels 《秧歌》 and 《赤地之戀》 and her tie to Hu Lancheng, whom the CCP regarded as a collaborator. — [RFA Mandarin, 唐家婕, 1 Oct 2020](https://www.rfa.org/mandarin/yataibaodao/kejiaowen/jt-10012020103737.html) (secondary)

### Inferences
- The erasure had several causes: class-aesthetic criteria (petit bourgeois), Cold War politics (the USIS novels) and the taint of collaboration. It worked against her as both "too private" and "too political."

### Gaps
- Not checked here: her post-1949 work in Shanghai (*十八春*/*小艾* under the pen name 梁京, the 1950 Shanghai literary congress) and specific literary histories that omitted her, such as 王瑤 and 唐弢. Wikipedia links 梁京 only to 《小艾》 (1950).

## 3. C.T. Hsia 夏志清, *A History of Modern Chinese Fiction* (Yale, 1961)

### Takeaway
Hsia canonized her in English. He gave her an exceptionally long chapter (46 pages in the Chinese edition), called her "the best and most important writer in Chinese today," and called *The Golden Cangue* "the greatest novelette in the history of Chinese literature." That set up both her Western reputation and the later Taiwan/HK and mainland revivals. Critics note that his praise of *Rice-Sprout Song* and *Naked Earth* was coloured by his anti-communism.

### Cited Findings
- Hsia: "Eileen Chang is not only the best and most important writer in Chinese today; her short stories alone invite valid comparisons with, and in some respects claim superiority over, the work of serious modern women writers in English." This is quoted secondhand; the 1961 text was not checked directly. — [Jiwei Xiao, "Belated Reunion?", New Left Review 111, May–Jun 2018](https://newleftreview.org/issues/ii111/articles/jiwei-xiao-belated-reunion); paraphrased in [LRB 2019](https://lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v41/n15/sheng-yun/small-feet-were-an-advantage). In Chinese: "對於一個研究中國現代文學的人說來，張愛玲是今日中國最優秀最重要的作家" — [Wang & Ma 1997](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/93502.html)
- Hsia on *The Golden Cangue*: "the greatest novelette in the history of Chinese literature" — [Renditions, CUHK author page](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/renditions/authors/zhangal.html). In Chinese: 「中國從古以來最偉大的中篇小說」 — [Wang & Ma 1997](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/93502.html)
- Hsia gave Chang 46 pages, an exceptional allotment (「破例」). Wang & Ma call his praise of 《秧歌》 and 《赤地之戀》 "不足取的" because of his anti-communist stance. — [Wang & Ma 1997](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/93502.html)
- With the 1961 chapter, "上海的通俗女作家首度與魯迅、茅盾等大師平起平坐." — [王德威〈落地的麥子不死〉, via aisixiang](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/90955.html)
- Hsia praised her moral seriousness: "her registration of the inescapable pettiness and sadness of human endeavour is nearly always morally disturbing." She "was canonised in the 1960s by C.T. Hsia." — [LRB 2019](https://lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v41/n15/sheng-yun/small-feet-were-an-advantage)
- Hsia had earlier called her "the most gifted Chinese writer to emerge in the 40's" and compared her favourably with Flannery O'Connor and Kafka. — [NYT obituary 1995, via Douban reproduction](https://site.douban.com/137987/widget/notes/6446811/note/671076989/)
- Hsia's career "was in many ways a result of Cold War politics." He championed Qian Zhongshu, Shen Congwen and Chang (David Der-wei Wang). — [Columbia University Press blog, 6 Jan 2014](https://cupblog.org/2014/01/06/c-t-hsia-1921-2013/)
- On the mainland, Wen Rumin recalls reading Hsia's English *History* around 1979. He counts Hsia's influence as one cause of the 1980s revival. — [Wen Rumin 2000](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/93380.html)

### Inferences
- Hsia's verdict was humanist-moral: "pettiness and sadness of human endeavour." But its Cold War context and his praise of the anti-communist novels show that the canonization itself was partly political. That is a nuance against a purely apolitical-humanist thesis.

### Gaps
- Hsia's exact 1961 wording on *The Rice-Sprout Song* (for example, whether he called it "already a classic") was **not verified**. Do not quote it.
- Exact chapter and page numbers in the 1961 Yale edition were not checked. The 46-page figure refers to the Chinese edition (友聯, 1979).

## 4. 1960s–80s Taiwan and Hong Kong: Crown editions, the "張派," the gossip-driven 張迷 culture, and film adaptations

### Takeaway
From 1966, Crown (皇冠) became her exclusive publisher, starting with 《怨女》 in 1968 and 《半生緣》 in 1969. A Taiwan "張愛玲熱" followed in the 1970s–80s, producing generations of "張派" writers. Hong Kong cinema began adapting her in 1984. This wave also had a strong voyeuristic, biographical streak: newspapers raced to print her daily routine, and a journalist went through her trash.

### Cited Findings
- From 1966, brokered by Stephen Soong 宋淇 and C.T. Hsia, Chang worked with Crown and granted it exclusive book rights, with one or two exceptions. After her death the estate passed to the Soongs, and Crown remains exclusive worldwide agent. — [星島頭條](https://www.stheadline.com/china-topics/3408530/張愛玲瓊瑤深得英文暢銷小說神髓-而合中國國情)
- Crown titles: 《怨女》 (1968), 《半生緣》 (1969), 《張看》 (1976), 《紅樓夢魘》 (1977), 《餘韻》 (1987). — [NCCU thesis record](https://nccur.lib.nccu.edu.tw/handle/140.119/39071)
- A 2026 op-ed says 平鑫濤's regard for Chang won Crown its exclusive rights and that re-collecting her scattered old work set off the 「張愛玲熱」, influencing 朱天文 and 朱天心. — [中國時報 opinion, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.chinatimes.com/opinion/20261015000005-262106) (opinion). The same search reports that *Crown* magazine (founded 1954) will close with issue 874 in December 2026; that is from the search summary and was not fetched.
- She "became a literary phenomenon again in Hong Kong and Taiwan in the 1970s." — [LRB 2019](https://lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v41/n15/sheng-yun/small-feet-were-an-advantage). The "Chang School" of writers formed in 1970s Taiwan. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eileen_Chang)
- The "張派" lineage (David Der-wei Wang): 白先勇 and 施叔青 in the 1960s; 鍾曉陽; the 三三 group (朱天文, 朱天心, 蔣曉雲 and others); 蘇偉貞, 袁瓊瓊, 蕭麗紅; on the mainland, 蘇童, 葉兆言, 須蘭, and 王安憶 (《長恨歌》, 1995). Wang's key line: "以後的四十年與其說張愛玲仍在創作，倒不如說她不斷地「被」創作：被學院裡的評家學者、學院外的作家讀者，一再重塑金身。" — [王德威〈落地的麥子不死〉](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/90955.html). The 2004 book version, 《落地的麥子不死——張愛玲與「張派」傳人》 (山東畫報), is noted in a [Douban review](https://book.douban.com/review/1258023/).
- Taiwan critics split. 水晶's 《張愛玲的小說藝術》 (Taipei 1973) was adulatory. 唐文標's 《張愛玲研究》 (1983) attacked her 「趣味主義」 and a 「死的世界」 without 「道德的批判」, and his 《張愛玲資料大全集》 came out in 1984. Hong Kong was cooler: 司馬長風's literary history gave her no place. — [Wang & Ma 1997](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/93502.html)
- "台灣在上世紀七八十年代吹起了「張愛玲熱」，大小報章都搶著刊登奶奶的起居註." 戴文采, commissioned by a newspaper, rented the apartment next to Chang's and went through her trash. The preface also says gossip about Chang among ordinary people came "不因她是〈金鎖記〉或《秧歌》的作者，而是因她極不尋常的家族背景與個人經歷." — [CUHK Press preface (2015)](https://cup.cuhk.edu.hk/image/data/preview/9789629966768_preface.pdf)
- The same preface reports that 張小虹 labelled the cultural phenomenon around Chang, before and after her death, with two terms: "coprophilia" and "necrophilia." — [CUHK Press preface](https://cup.cuhk.edu.hk/image/data/preview/9789629966768_preface.pdf) (secondhand; 張小虹's original text was not located)
- Her withdrawal "led to a frenzy of voyeurism": fans "tracked down her apartment in Los Angeles and dug through her dustbin." Hu Lancheng "tried to capitalise on her fame." — [LRB 2019](https://lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v41/n15/sheng-yun/small-feet-were-an-advantage)
- Film and TV: *Love in a Fallen City* (Ann Hui, 1984), *Rouge of the North* (1988), *Red Rose White Rose* (1994), *Eighteen Springs* (1997); TV *The Legend of Eileen Chang* (2004) and *Love in a Fallen City* (2009). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eileen_Chang). Ann Hui's three Chang adaptations are 1984, 1997 and 2020. — [Screen Daily](https://screendaily.com/reviews/love-after-love-venice-review/5152702.article)
- At her death she had "a huge devoted following in Taiwan, Hong Kong," and her death drew "front-page tributes in Taiwan and Hong Kong." — [NYT 1995 via Douban](https://site.douban.com/137987/widget/notes/6446811/note/671076989/)

### Inferences
- The Taiwan/HK wave had two layers. Writers responded to her style and her "蒼涼" view of history (the 張派). The press and public consumed her as a mystery-celebrity. Tang Wenbiao's "no moral judgement" attack is an early counter-reading to the humanist thesis.

### Gaps
- No Crown sales or print-run figures were found.
- No fuller dating of the 戴文采 trash episode. It is commonly placed in 1988, but that is not verified here.

## 5. Mainland rediscovery in the 1980s–90s, and the 1995 death

### Takeaway
The thaw came through academia (Hsia, 1981 and 1982–83 essays) and Ke Ling's 〈遙寄張愛玲〉 (1984–85). 《收穫》 reprinted 〈傾城之戀〉 in 1985 and People's Literature published a facsimile 《傳奇》 in 1986. Mass popularity followed in the 1990s: a four-volume collected works (1992, about 100,000 sets), four biographies (1992–95), film adaptations, and a market-era nostalgia boom. Her 1995 death alone in Los Angeles set off a media surge. The most explicit scholarly account, Wen Rumin's, describes the process as academic discovery, then media amplification, then cultural symbol, then commercial fashion.

### Cited Findings
- Chronology per Wen Rumin, 《中華讀書報》, 27 Dec 2000 ([aisixiang](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/93380.html)):
  - Nov 1981: 張葆莘's 〈張愛玲傳奇〉 in 《文匯月刊》, the first public comment on her since reform.
  - 1984–85: Ke Ling's 〈遙寄張愛玲〉 in 《讀書》 (1984 no. 4) and 《收穫》 (1985 no. 3). 《收穫》 also reprinted 〈傾城之戀〉, "the first appearance of her work on the mainland after the Cultural Revolution."
  - 1984: 黃修己's 《中國現代文學簡史》 introduced 〈金鎖記〉.
  - 1986: People's Literature Publishing House (人民文學出版社) issued a facsimile 《傳奇》, followed by local reprints.
  - 1992: Anhui Literature and Art (安徽文藝) published the four-volume 《張愛玲文集》, about 100,000 sets, followed by piracy.
  - 1992–95: four biographies (王一心, 于青, 阿川, 余斌).
  - 1994: a popular ranking of modern writers placed her 8th (「冷月情魔」).
  - 1995: her death was given unusually prominent coverage in Chinese papers.
  - Wen conservatively estimates her readership at nearly one million.
- **Conflict on Ke Ling's essay.** One search summary says it was written at the request of 劉以鬯 and first appeared in 《香港文學》 (Feb 1985, no. 2), and that 黃心村 (Nicole Huang) said it "signalled" her work could be republished. That came from a search summary of mainland sources (chinawriter/aisixiang), not a page fetched here. It sits awkwardly with Wen's 《讀書》 1984 no. 4 date. **Treat dates as unresolved** — [search context: aisixiang 93380](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/93380.html)
- Further mainland milestones (Wang & Ma): early essays by 顏純鈞 (1982) and 趙園 (1983); 陳子善's recovery of lost works, starting with 《小艾》 in 1987; inclusion in 《中國現代文學三十年》 (1987) and in 嚴家炎 (1989), who called her work 「一個小小的高峰」. — [Wang & Ma 1997](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/93502.html)
- Causes named by Wen Rumin:
  - Post-Mao intellectual liberation, Hsia's influence, and young readers' attraction to "marginal" writers once the 翻案 trend had worn thin.
  - The 1980s 「方法熱」 drew critics to her technique.
  - From the mid-1990s: the biography boom, commercial publishing, and the consumer-society 「精英文化與大眾文化相融合」.
  - Her work answered market-era anxieties: materialism, nostalgia, private life over grand narratives.
  - Publishers packaged her as a brand, with vintage photos and Republican-nostalgia books, for example 《重現的玫瑰 張愛玲相冊》 (光明日報出版社, 1999).
  - Film adaptations: 《滾滾紅塵》 (1990, 三毛 script), 《紅玫瑰與白玫瑰》, 《傾城之戀》, 《半生緣》.
  - Summary line: 「先是由學術界發現和定位，然後經大眾傳媒製作並擴大到公共空間，變成文化符號，再被商界借用促銷，轉為一種時尚。」
  - Readers were mostly educated urban white-collar workers and students, most of whom, he argues, **missed the bleakness and cruelty** in her work.
  - [Wen Rumin 2000](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/93380.html)
- In the 1990s, readers sought an alternative to "mediocre entertainment fiction of the post-Tiananmen era" on one side and avant-garde modernism on the other. — [Chiang & Link, NYRB 2018](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2018/06/07/eileen-chang-before-the-revolution/)
- Scholar Dai Jinhua (戴錦華) on first reading her in 1987: "Without a single line of introduction, I read 'Love in a Fallen City,' and I fell head over heels for it." By the end of the decade Chang "was once again a household name." Researchers recast her as "a 'small history' grounded in daily life and closer to the humanity of ordinary people." Her revival also rode "a rising middle class hungry for tales of urban elegance and romance" and a Republican-era nostalgia "fever," and her commercial appeal "often misled readers into thinking of her as a romance novelist." — [Sixth Tone, 29 Dec 2020](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1006607) **[state-owned outlet; the scholar quotes are usable as attributed views]**
- Xudong Zhang reads 1990s Shanghai nostalgia as "a kind of defense mechanism" against socioeconomic shock. — cited in a book chapter surfaced by search ([JSTOR TOC](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt1xw9kh)); only the search snippet was seen.
- The 1995 death: her landlord found her dead in her Westwood (Rochester Ave) apartment on 8 Sep 1995, several days after she died. She was cremated without a memorial and her ashes were scattered in the Pacific. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eileen_Chang). She was "found dead … in the Los Angeles apartment where she had lived as a virtual recluse"; the building manager raised the alarm when she did not answer her phone. — [NYT 13 Sep 1995 via Douban](https://site.douban.com/137987/widget/notes/6446811/note/671076989/)
- The fever that began in the 1980s surged again across the mainland after her death in September 1995. — [Substack essay](https://substack.com/home/post/p-160519157) (weak, single source; corroborated in substance by Wen's note on the 1995 press coverage)
- David Der-wei Wang links the 1995 death to a felt sense of loss and to her enduring stature. — [王德威](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/90955.html)

### Inferences
- The mainland wave began as an aesthetic and humanist rediscovery by intellectuals: "small history," daily life, the humanity of ordinary people. It became mass popularity through consumer-era nostalgia, biography and film. Wen explicitly argues the mass readership missed her darker core. That is direct evidence against the thesis, or at least for a two-track reception.

### Gaps
- The exact Ke Ling publication sequence (《讀書》 1984 vs 《香港文學》/《收穫》 1985) needs a primary check.
- No sourced 1995–96 sales spike figures.

## 6. 2007: Ang Lee's *Lust, Caution* (色，戒)

### Takeaway
The film won the 2007 Venice Golden Lion. It was released on the mainland on 1 Nov 2007 with about 7–12 minutes cut and still grossed roughly US$15–17 million. Nationalist critics attacked it as a "traitor movie" and an insult to Chinese women, and in March 2008 SARFT effectively blacklisted Tang Wei. The debate was about patriotism, collaboration and sex far more than about Chang's humanism. It brought her name to a new mass audience.

### Cited Findings
- Venice Golden Lion 2007 (Ang Lee). — [Akron Library catalogue record](https://catalog.akronlibrary.org/Record/98671)
- Mainland gross of US$15.1M over five weeks (China Film Group distribution), US$17.1M total, released 1 Nov 2007. — [Box Office Mojo, China 2007 W47](https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekly/2007W47/?area=CN); [Box Office Mojo, Nov 2007 China](https://www.boxofficemojo.com/month/november/2007/?area=CN)
- "More than 90 million yuan (12 million US dollars)" in its first two weeks. The film lost 12 minutes over six edits.
  - CASS scholar Huang Jisu (黃紀蘇): "'Lust, Caution' is an insult to the good women of China," and the film "has become a type of new exotic Viagra for the West."
  - Director Zhou Guojin suggested it be classified a "Chinese traitor movie."
  - Unnamed critics called it "a big poisonous weed."
  - Defender Chai Ziwen: it is about "the hopelessness and loneliness that no repression can ever suppress."
  - IPS also reports "a criticism campaign spearheaded by … 'People's Daily.'"

## 7. 2009–2018: *Little Reunions* (小團圓) and the posthumous English novels

### Takeaway
Roland Soong published *Little Reunions* in 2009 against Chang's own instruction to destroy it. It topped bestseller lists in Hong Kong and Taiwan and sold about 700,000 copies in China in six months. The controversy was over the ethics of publication and over its autobiographical exposure of the Hu Lancheng affair. HKU Press then published her English novels *The Fall of the Pagoda* (Apr 2010) and *The Book of Change* (Sep 2010), and NYRB published *Little Reunions* in English in 2018.

### Cited Findings
- "The book sold 700,000 copies in China in its first six months of publication." Its 2009 popularity is attributed to middle-class readers feeling "morally adrift." — [Chiang & Link, NYRB 2018](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2018/06/07/eileen-chang-before-the-revolution/). The NYRB Classics edition was translated by Jane Weizhen Pan & Martin Merz.
- A mainland print run of 400,000 plus pirated editions is reported in a blog source only. — [mychinesebooks.com](https://mychinesebooks.com/fifteen-years-triumph-eileen-chang/) **(weak, single source)**
- HKU and Crown (HK) held the Feb 2009 launch, where Roland Soong explained the reasons for publishing. The book topped the Hong Kong and Taiwan bestseller lists, with rush buying in Hong Kong. Soong also pledged HK$1 million for an Eileen Chang Memorial Scholarship. — [HKU press release (Chinese)](https://hku.hk/press/c_news_detail_5929.html); [HKU LEAD, Feb 2009](https://lead-cms.hku.hk/en/article/2009/02/book-launch-on-eileen-changs-autobiographical-novel-little-reunion-小團圓/)
- Chang "asked her literary executors … to destroy the manuscript." Roland Soong "made the decision not to obey the author's wishes." Stephen Soong called the book "unsympathetic." Sheng Yun: "I haven't come across anyone who hasn't read it as an autobiography." — [LRB 2019](https://lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v41/n15/sheng-yun/small-feet-were-an-advantage)
- The 2009 publication "has dismayed and divided her admirers in Taiwan and the PRC." — [Jiwei Xiao, NLR 111 (2018)](https://newleftreview.org/issues/ii111/articles/jiwei-xiao-belated-reunion)
- The Paris Review blog reports widespread criticism for its cryptic narrative and that it did "not sound like Eileen Chang," and that Stephen Soong was told in 1976 the manuscript should be burned. — [The Paris Review blog](https://www.theparisreview.org/blog/?p=171797) (search snippet only; the fetch was blocked)
- Conflicting Chang statements: a 1992 letter to the Soongs says the novel 「要銷毀」, while a 1993 letter to her publisher says it 「一定要盡早寫完」. — [UDN blog](https://blog.udn.com/powerecho/2783218) **(weak; letters not checked directly)**
- HKU Press launched *The Fall of the Pagoda* in April 2010, "47 years after it was first penned," as the English precursor to *Little Reunions*. *The Book of Change* (wartime Hong Kong) followed in September 2010. Soong donated manuscript photocopies to HKU. The "Eileen Chang Uncovered" exhibit ran 17–19 Sep 2010. — [HKU press release 6223](https://hku.hk/press/news_detail_6223.html); [HKU LEAD Sep 2010](https://lead.hku.hk/en/article/2010/09/public-exhibit-eileen-chang-uncovered/)
- Other posthumous works: *The Young Marshal* (2014, unfinished). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eileen_Chang)

### Inferences
- The 2009 wave was the purest case of biography-driven revival: a roman à clef about Hu Lancheng published against the author's will. Even so, NYRB's reviewers read its success as a moral hunger among middle-class readers, which fits the humanist thesis in part.

### Gaps
- Crown Taiwan first print run, and mainland publisher and print run, were not verified (the mainland publisher is commonly given as 北京十月文藝).
- Other NYRB titles (*Love in a Fallen City* 2007, *Naked Earth* 2015, *Written on Water*) and their dates were not verified here.

## 10. Across all waves: what drives the revivals? Evidence for and against the "humanist core" thesis

### Takeaway
Sources agree that Chang is repeatedly remade by others (David Der-wei Wang: 不斷地「被」創作). Each wave has a distinct trigger: Hsia's Cold War canonization, Crown and the Taiwan press, the post-Mao thaw, consumer nostalgia, the 1995 death, the 2007 film, the 2009 scandal, the 2020 centenary, the 2026 voice. Scholars who name a durable core point to her attention to daily life, ordinary people and moral seriousness. Several credible sources say the mass waves ran mainly on biography, gossip, commodified Old-Shanghai nostalgia and film, and that mass readers often missed her bleak core.

### Cited Findings

**Supporting the humanist thesis:**
- "A 'small history' grounded in daily life and closer to the humanity of ordinary people." — [Sixth Tone 2020](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1006607) [state-owned outlet; framing attributed to researchers]
- Hsia: "her registration of the inescapable pettiness and sadness of human endeavour is nearly always morally disturbing." — [LRB 2019](https://lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v41/n15/sheng-yun/small-feet-were-an-advantage)
- 周成蔭: 「政治對張愛玲是次要的。她批判的是人生跟社會」. 白睿文: the pandemic as 「一個很張愛玲的時刻」, people living and loving amid upheaval. — [RFA 2020](https://www.rfa.org/mandarin/yataibaodao/kejiaowen/jt-10012020103737.html)
- Wang Dewei ties her persistence to her attention to material life and ordinary detail amid historical upheaval, contrasted with Maoist 工農兵文藝 that erased the city. — [王德威](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/90955.html)
- The 1990s fever answered a demand for an alternative to post-Tiananmen entertainment fiction. *Little Reunions*' 2009 success reflected readers who felt "morally adrift." — [NYRB 2018](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2018/06/07/eileen-chang-before-the-revolution/)
- Fu Lei and Hu (1944) praised craft and sympathy for characters. — [Wang & Ma 1997](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/93502.html)

**Cutting against it:**
- The trajectory, per Wen Rumin: academic discovery, then media, then cultural symbol, then commercial fashion (「轉為一種時尚」). He says publishers branded her with vintage photos and Republican nostalgia, and that her urban white-collar readers mostly **missed the bleakness and cruelty**. — [Wen Rumin 2000](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/93380.html)
- Commercial appeal "often misled readers into thinking of her as a romance novelist." The revival rode "a rising middle class hungry for tales of urban elegance and romance" and a Republican-era nostalgia "fever." — [Sixth Tone 2020](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1006607) [state-owned outlet]
- Popular chatter about her is 「不因她是〈金鎖記〉或《秧歌》的作者，而是因她極不尋常的家族背景與個人經歷」. Taiwan papers raced to print her daily routine, 戴文采 went through her trash, and 張小虹 diagnosed "coprophilia" and "necrophilia." — [CUHK Press preface 2015](https://cup.cuhk.edu.hk/image/data/preview/9789629966768_preface.pdf)
- 何曼莊: 「比起作品，很多人追尋張愛玲是看到她的人生和形象」. — [RFA 2020](https://www.rfa.org/mandarin/yataibaodao/kejiaowen/jt-10012020103737.html)
- Her seclusion "led to a frenzy of voyeurism," and Hu Lancheng capitalized on her fame. — [LRB 2019](https://lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v41/n15/sheng-yun/small-feet-were-an-advantage)
- The Hsia canonization was a product of Cold War politics, and his praise of the USIS-commissioned anti-communist novels was criticized as politically motivated. — [CUP blog 2014](https://cupblog.org/2014/01/06/c-t-hsia-1921-2013/); [Wang & Ma 1997](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/93502.html)
- 唐文標 (1983) charged her with 「趣味主義」 and a world without 「道德的批判」. — [Wang & Ma 1997](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/93502.html)
- In 2007 the controversy was nationalist and sexual ("traitor movie," "insult to the good women of China"). — [IPS 2007](https://www.ipsnews.net/2007/11/culture-china-lust-caution-politically-incorrect-box-office-grosser/)
- Shanghai nostalgia in the 1990s was read as a "defense mechanism" against socioeconomic shock (Xudong Zhang). — [JSTOR TOC (snippet)](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt1xw9kh)
- On state toleration: officials remained cautious even in 2020. Mass consumption was tolerated while the anti-communist works stayed sensitive. — [RFA 2020](https://www.rfa.org/mandarin/yataibaodao/kejiaowen/jt-10012020103737.html)

### Inferences
- The best-supported synthesis is two layers. The **persistence** of her canon, across scholars, writers and the 張派 lineage, is explained by her style and her humane attention to small, compromised lives. The **timing and scale** of each mass wave was set by external triggers: Cold War canonization, the post-Mao thaw, consumer nostalgia, death, film, scandal and recordings. The thesis holds better for "why she survives" than for "why each fever happened."
- A mainland mass readership that was tolerated because she seemed "apolitical", while the anti-communist works stayed sensitive, suggests that state toleration of a depoliticized Chang helped the mainland waves. That inference is not explicitly stated by any single source here.

### Gaps
- Leo Ou-fan Lee (*Shanghai Modern*, 1999), Nicole Huang (*Women, War, Domesticity*), Kam Louie (ed., 2012), Shuang Shen and 張小虹's own texts were not accessed directly. Their arguments on nostalgia and on the "everyday" should be checked before quoting.
- No quantitative reader surveys found.
