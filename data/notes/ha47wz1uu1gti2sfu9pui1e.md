What happens to the ~2,000 flats and their owners after the fire is a story of its own: a government buyback of the seven ruined blocks, a long fight by owners for a statutory meeting with the government-appointed administrator, and the scramble for somewhere to live. This page gathers that thread. For the people living through it, see the displaced-resident profiles on [[hong-kong-fire-2025.people]]; for the dated sequence, [[hong-kong-fire-2025.timeline]].

## The buyback scheme

- **The plan (21 Feb 2026).** The government offered to buy back flats in the **seven fire-damaged blocks** at **HK$8,000/sq ft** (without land premium) or **HK$10,500/sq ft** (with), with **flat-for-flat** exchanges available from 10 Home Ownership Scheme / Housing Society projects. **Wang Chi House** — the one block largely spared — was initially excluded. **Redevelopment was ruled out**, the government citing a 9–10-year timeline ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/hong-kong/society/article/3344228/wang-fuk-court-buyback-plan-disappoints-some-residents-despite-generous-offer)).
- **The money (25 Feb 2026 Budget).** HK$4b public funds + HK$2.8b from the Support Fund = **HK$6.8b** earmarked. An early survey found **74%** would consider acquisition and **9%** insisted on redevelopment only.
- **Offer letters (14 May 2026)** went out at the HK$8,000–10,500/sq ft terms, with a **75% acceptance threshold** and a flat-selection deadline of **30 June**.
- **Hop On** (the administrator) began **refunding ~HK$127m** of the renovation fund that had financed the deadly works.

### Rehousing options
Transitional and permanent rehousing was spread across several estates and years: **Shing Chi Court** (Kowloon Bay, from **Sep 2026**); **Kam Tin Wui Hei Court** (late **Aug 2027**); and new Tai Po developments at **Chung Nga Road West** (**2029**) and **Kwong Fuk Park** (**2033**). Many displaced residents are elderly and wanted to stay in Tai Po; the long timelines pushed some to accept flats in other districts (see the profiles on [[hong-kong-fire-2025.people]]).

## Uptake — and the 17 holdouts

- Over **85%** of fire-hit-block owners had accepted by the **30 June** flat-selection deadline.
- By the **31 August** deadline, **99.1% (1,967 of 1,984)** had accepted — leaving **17 holdouts**, who face **compulsory acquisition**, with compensation likely pegged to the lower post-fire value and enabling legislation flagged for **2027**.
- **Wang Chi House** (the spared block) was brought in with a separate **~HK$1b** offer (extended in June): by **21 August**, **91.5%** of its owners had accepted and **67.7%** had signed sale-and-purchase agreements, against a **15 October** signing deadline (the final tally is not yet reported).

## The fight for a homeowners' meeting

After the fire, the government installed **Hop On Management** (a Chinachem subsidiary) as the estate's administrator. Owners spent months trying to force a statutory general meeting to get answers on funds, insurance and their future housing — and were repeatedly blocked.

- **Online petition → forgery referral (5–10 Apr 2026).** Owners gathered a petition (**428 valid** signatures, above the 5% Building Management Ordinance threshold of ~100 of 1,984 units) to compel a general meeting. Hop On **rejected** it on **5 April**; on **10 April** the Home Affairs Department **referred the petition to law enforcement**, alleging it "lacked authentication mechanisms and may involve people impersonating owners and forging signatures" ([HKFP](https://hongkongfp.com/2026/04/10/tai-po-fire-owners-petition-for-meeting-with-estate-administrator-may-contain-forged-signatures-govt-says/)).
- **Owners regroup (29 Apr 2026).** Undeterred, owners delivered **247 handwritten** signatures (~12% of households). Under the BMO this obliged Hop On to issue a meeting notice within 14 days (by **14 May**) and hold the meeting within 45 days ([HKFP](https://hongkongfp.com/2026/05/14/tai-po-fire-administrator-of-fire-hit-wang-fuk-court-seeks-to-extend-deadline-for-homeowners-meeting/)).
- **The administrator stalls (14–20 May 2026).** On the notice deadline, Hop On instead asked the **Lands Tribunal to extend** the statutory timeframe, citing signature verification and venue logistics, and held **online-only "information sessions"** (from 20 May) with **no voting** and reported registration glitches ([HKFP](https://hongkongfp.com/2026/05/11/tai-po-fire-wang-fuk-court-residents-hit-registration-hurdles-for-online-meeting-with-management-firm/)).
- **Tribunal rejects the delay (1–2 Jun 2026).** Lands Tribunal Judge **Gary Lam denied** the postponement: there was **no jurisdiction** under the BMO to vary the timeframe, and delay would infringe owners' right to a meeting — Hop On already held contact details for **1,601 of 1,984** owners, so the difficulty was "not insurmountable" ([HKFP](https://hongkongfp.com/2026/06/02/tai-po-fire-court-denies-estate-administrators-bid-to-postpone-owners-meeting/)).
- **Government backs the administrator (9 Jun 2026).** CE **John Lee defended Hop On** over the delay, drawing criticism that officials were shielding the administrator rather than enforcing the meeting owners had demanded since March ([HKFP](https://hongkongfp.com/2026/06/09/tai-po-fire-hong-kong-leader-defends-housing-estate-administrator-over-owners-meeting-delay/)).
- **First statutory meeting (19 Jul 2026).** The meeting finally convened across **four venues**. Owners **rejected Hop On's audit report** but backed holding a second meeting within two months.

## Belongings retrieval (20 Apr – 4 May 2026)

Announced by Deputy Chief Secretary **Warner Cheuk** (卓永興) on 27 March, retrieval ran in **3-hour slots**, stairs only, **max four people per flat**, with **1,000 personnel** deployed. On day one (Wang Sun House, 1F–10F), **264 of 267** registered residents attended; average stay 1h 44min; one resident in his 70s was hospitalised with breathing difficulty and discharged the same day; of 11 missing-item reports, 4 were recovered, 4 judged destroyed, and 3 placed under police investigation ([HKFP](https://hongkongfp.com/2026/03/27/wang-fuk-court-residents-to-get-3-hour-slots-to-retrieve-belongings-at-fire-hit-estate-from-mid-april/), [The Witness](https://thewitnesshk.com/%e5%ae%8f%e7%a6%8f%e8%8b%91%e5%b1%85%e6%b0%91%e9%a6%96%e6%97%a5%e5%9b%9e%e5%ae%b6-%e9%a6%96%e6%89%b9%e9%96%8b%e6%94%be%e5%ae%8f%e6%96%b0%e9%96%a3-%e5%a4%a7%e5%9f%94%e7%8f%be%e5%a0%b4%e7%9b%b4%e6%93%8a/)).

## Relief payments

Separate from the buyback, a government-led **Wang Fuk Court Assistance Fund** (~HK$2.3b: ~HK$2b donations + HK$300m government seed) made emergency disbursements: **HK$200,000 condolence** and **HK$50,000 funeral** per fatality, and **HK$50,000 living allowance** per affected household (announced by Secretary for Home and Youth Affairs **Alice Mak**). For the corporate and philanthropic donation roster, see **Donations and Aid** on [[hong-kong-fire-2025.activism]].
