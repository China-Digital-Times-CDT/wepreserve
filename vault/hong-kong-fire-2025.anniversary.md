The **first anniversary** of the Wang Fuk Court fire falls on **26 November 2026** — one year after the blaze killed **168 people** in Tai Po, Hong Kong's deadliest fire since 1948. This page follows how that anniversary is marked: the commemorations and the pressure brought to bear on them, and the survivors and families a year on. The **immediate** aftermath of 2025 — the three-day citywide mourning, the 頭七, and the first diaspora vigils — is recorded under [[Vigils and Mourning|hong-kong-fire-2025.activism]]; the fuller story of the cause, the report, accountability, resettlement and the criminal case lives across the [[collection hub|hong-kong-fire-2025]] and its pages.

## Commemoration, vigils and their policing

- **2026-11-12 — Memorial Masses under police scrutiny:** As the anniversary approached, Hong Kong police reportedly **questioned two Catholic churches** over planned Wang Fuk Court memorial Masses; one parish replaced its anniversary Mass with a general ceremony for the year's dead. Cardinal **Stephen Chow** (周守仁) responded by inviting **every parish** in the diocese to offer a Mass for the fire's victims on **26 Nov 2026**. Chief Secretary **Eric Chan** (陳國基) said lawful memorials would **not** be blocked, while warning that people "with ulterior motives" were promoting memorial gatherings online to draw crowds into "disruptive acts." The **Tai Po** parish (Immaculate Heart of Mary) briefly pulled its Mass publicity — Auxiliary Bishop **Joseph Ha** (夏志誠) called the removal a "misunderstanding" and confirmed police had queried the parish — while a Wan Chai priest, Fr. **Law Kwok-fai** (羅國輝), said a self-described police "friend" came to "show concern" about his Mass, which he found "too disturbing" ([HKFP](https://hongkongfp.com/2026/11/12/hong-kong-police-reportedly-question-two-catholic-churches-over-tai-po-fire-memorial-masses/) · [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/hong-kong/society/article/3375267/tai-po-fire-memorial-events-victims-wont-be-blocked-eric-chan-says) · [The Pillar](https://www.pillarcatholic.com/p/cardinal-chow-calls-for-hk-fire-memorial) · [BBC Chinese](https://www.bbc.com/zhongwen/articles/cw8dv46pv125o/trad)).
- **2026-11-21/22 — Migrant-worker survivors' PolyU event cancelled:** A book launch and stage performance by **Indonesian migrant domestic workers** who survived the fire — the second volume of *What We Carry, Under the Same Sky*, with poetry, drama and a prayer of mourning — set for **22 Nov** at **Hong Kong Polytechnic University** (Room ST111), was cancelled **less than a day beforehand**. Organisers **Microlab Collective** (a cross-disciplinary platform tied to PolyU's English & Communication department) and the **Indonesian Migrant Workers Union (印尼移工聯盟, JBMI)** cited only "**unforeseen circumstances**," gave no reason, and said they hoped to reschedule. The year-long workshop had gathered survivors' essays, poems, photographs and artwork into the book; participants had rehearsed on their days off, and some bereaved workers' families had planned to attend. The silenced work was the survivors' own testimony — a **30-minute play they wrote, directed and performed themselves** — of months of insomnia and "seeing red" on closing their eyes, of guilt over a cat left behind in the flames, of a childhood friend (**Erawati**) who died in a stairwell after a last FaceTime to say goodbye to her son, closing on the line they had written: *"you are all mothers, who came to a faraway place to work for your children."* The fire killed **10 foreign domestic workers** among its 168 dead and affected **225** Indonesian and Filipino helpers ([集誌社 The Collective](https://thecollectivehk.com/%E5%AE%8F%E7%A6%8F%E8%8B%91%E5%A4%A7%E7%81%AB%E4%B8%80%E5%B9%B4%EF%BD%9C%E5%80%96%E5%AD%98%E5%A4%96%E5%82%AD%E7%90%86%E5%A4%A7%E5%81%9A%E8%A9%B1%E5%8A%87%E3%80%81%E7%99%BC%E5%B8%83%E6%96%B0%E6%9B%B8/) · [HK01](https://www.hk01.com/%E7%A4%BE%E6%9C%83%E6%96%B0%E8%81%9E/60413496/) · [法庭線 The Witness](https://thewitnesshk.com/%E5%AE%8F%E7%A6%8F%E5%A4%A7%E7%81%AB%E4%B8%80%E5%B9%B4-%E5%80%96%E5%AD%98%E5%A4%96%E5%82%AD%E8%91%97%E6%9B%B8%E5%AF%AB%E5%8A%87%E7%99%82%E5%82%B7-%E6%B4%BB%E5%8B%95%E5%9B%A0%E4%B8%8D%E5%8F%AF%E9%A0%90/)).
- **2026-11-24 — One year on, mourning turns cautious (BBC):** With the anniversary two days away, Chief Secretary **Eric Chan** (陳國基) confirmed the government would hold **no official commemoration** but would not block lawful mourning, while Security chief **Chris Tang** (鄧炳強) said only that "any lawful activity will be allowed." *Hong Kong Economic Journal* reported that the government and the pro-establishment camp leaned against any ceremony — a mourning crowd might become a gauge of public anger over the fire — and that political and business figures and opinion leaders had been **"advised" not to attend** Wang Fuk commemorations. Bereaved relatives told the BBC they would return to the site **alone and without announcing a time**, fearing any gathering would be branded "**趁機搞事**" (exploiting the occasion to make trouble); some residents said they had been **followed or photographed** by unidentified people and had asked reporters to delete earlier criticism of the government. A flower-tribute zone was marked out at 廣福休憩處 (Kwong Fuk Rest Garden), but officials would not confirm who drew the lines or what the anniversary would hold ([BBC Chinese](https://www.bbc.com/zhongwen/articles/cw8dv46pv125o/trad)).
- **2026-11-24 — Anniversary coverage pulled; the "以災亂港" framing (BBC):** Newsroom staff told the BBC that a **finished anniversary special was pulled before its scheduled broadcast**, and that executives at another station had instructed reporters to **avoid interviewing "people" (人)**. The chill tracks Beijing's framing: the **Office for Safeguarding National Security** and the **Hong Kong and Macao Affairs Office** have cast those pressing questions over the fire as seeking to "**以災亂港**" — to use the disaster to throw Hong Kong into chaos — the same language used when a student petition-organiser was arrested in late November 2025 (recorded under [[Activism & suppression|hong-kong-fire-2025.activism]]) ([BBC Chinese](https://www.bbc.com/zhongwen/articles/cw8dv46pv125o/trad)).

## Survivors and families, one year on

- **A bereaved family (集誌社, 16 Nov 2026):** In a video record titled *"IT'S OK TO BE NOT OK"*, the survivors of a family that lost **four of its seven members** — the grandmother, the father, 15-month-old **"Yan Yan" (欣欣)** and their foreign domestic worker (the [[Wang Tai House family|hong-kong-fire-2025.people]] whose seven-hour rescue call went dead on the 14th floor) — described a year lived through grief: sorting the dead's belongings, psychiatric treatment, and visits to the cemetery. The surviving uncle **李俊豪**, the mother **"Ali"** and the grandfather said the trauma of losing family has never healed, and called for a **memorial monument (紀念碑)** to be raised at Wang Fuk Court so that this "**man-made disaster**" (人禍) warns future generations ([集誌社 The Collective](https://thecollectivehk.com/%E5%BD%B1%E5%83%8F%E8%A8%98%E9%8C%84%EF%BD%9C%E5%AE%8F%E7%A6%8F%E8%8B%91%E5%A4%A7%E7%81%AB%E4%B8%80%E5%B9%B4%E3%80%80its-ok-to-be-not-ok/)).
- **One year on, grieving under a chill (BBC, 24 Nov 2026):** Relatives who lost family described mourning quietly, out of fear. **Yip Ka-kui** (葉家駒), whose wife died, said he would return to the estate on the anniversary but **would not tell anyone the time**, wanting only "a quiet space … to mourn our departed family" and dreading any incident that could be pinned on mourners; he called the burnt-out towers a "**恥辱柱**" (pillar of shame) the government would rather demolish to make the matter "end." **Lee Kwok-hung** (李國鴻), who lost his mother and the family's two domestic workers, said he would go back **alone** — "I haven't arranged with anyone … you don't want to let them catch you out." (Both also appear in the fire's [[report|hong-kong-fire-2025.report]].) ([BBC Chinese](https://www.bbc.com/zhongwen/articles/cw8dv46pv125o/trad)).
