## 1. The Rice-Sprout Song: plot, composition, and what Chang said about its factual basis

### Takeaway
*The Rice-Sprout Song* follows one post-land-reform family near Shanghai: Gold Root 金根, a model peasant, his wife Moon Scent 月香, and their little daughter. Through them it traces hunger, a forced New Year "contribution" drive for army families, a grain riot that cadres put down with gunfire, arson, and a compulsory yangko dance. In her Chinese 跋 (postscript) Chang insisted that "人物雖然都是虛構的，事情卻都是有根有據的" (the characters are invented, but the events all have a basis). She named mostly second-hand documentary sources: a *People's Literature* self-criticism, a *Liberation Daily* news item, word of mouth, and a propaganda film. She did not cite her own participation in land reform. Her only direct claim of rural experience is a 1968 interview remark that she "lived in the countryside for three or four months, in winter" before writing.

### Cited Findings

**Plot**
- Wang Xiaojue's synopsis:
  - The story is set "1950年代初土地改革初步結束後上海周邊的一個村莊".
  - 月香 returns from domestic service in Shanghai and finds food scarce.
  - The cadre 王霖 orders households to hand over grain to support the Korean War front and to send gifts to the families of soldiers and martyrs (軍烈屬).
  - 金根 resists openly, which leads to a grain riot and violent intervention. 金根 is wounded and dies, and 月香 sets fire to the granary.
  - "在慘澹的新年，倖存的村民被迫扭起了秧歌，給軍烈屬拜年".
  - A second strand follows the Shanghai screenwriter 顧岡, who fabricates a script about landlords and KMT agents burning grain.
  - Source: [王曉珏〈測繪冷戰文化地形圖：張愛玲與《秧歌》〉, 《二十一世紀》no. 184 (Apr 2021), p. 98](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c184-202006010.pdf)
- Author check: Wang Xiaojue (王曉珏) is identified in the article as Associate Professor, Department of Asian Languages and Cultures, Rutgers University — [same PDF, author note](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c184-202006010.pdf)
- Detailed plot points from 戴晴's essay 〈從小說看土改——張愛玲的《秧歌》在大陸〉 (written 1996 per its internal references):
  - 金根's wish is just "我要那米一顆顆的數得出來" (p.104).
  - The peasant association decides that every household must give "半隻豬、四十斤年糕" for New Year visits to army families (p.109).
  - The cadre 王同志 is first to fire into the crowd. Villagers die, and 金根's little daughter is trampled to death.
  - 金根 later kills himself. A militiaman says he "看見一個女人在黑影裏奔跑，被他追着，一直把她趕到火裏去了" (p.163).
  - 王同志 then says "我們失敗了……我們對自己的老百姓開槍" (p.139).
  - Source: [戴晴, in ESWN compilation](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)

**The English ending was changed at the publisher's request**
- The Chinese version and the English first draft only hint at 月香's death.
- "斯克里布納出版社英文初稿的編輯並不滿意這樣的敍事設計，要求張愛玲增補月香死亡場景的細節，以增強小說的政治譴責力量".
- The 1955 English edition therefore adds a scene: villagers find a woman's body, still sitting upright, glowing an odd bright pink (UC Press ed. pp. 175–76).
- Source: [王曉珏 2021, p. 103](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c184-202006010.pdf)

**Publication history**
- Written in English in Hong Kong in 1954. Chang rewrote it in Chinese the same year and serialised it in the USIS-run fortnightly 《今日世界》.
- July 1954: published in Chinese as 「今日世界叢書之九」.
- 1955: published in English by Charles Scribner's Sons, New York.
- Source: [王曉珏 2021, p. 93](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c184-202006010.pdf)

**The 跋 (postscript): the sources Chang named**
- Core statement: "《秧歌》裏面的人物雖然都是虛構的，事情卻都是有根有據的". Wang notes that this kind of self-authentication is "非常少見" in Chang's career. The 跋 is on pp. 203–205 of the Crown 典藏版 (2010). — [王曉珏 2021, p. 100, n. eo/et](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c184-202006010.pdf)
- 丁爾綱 (a hostile mainland critic, 2006) lists the three "真事" the 跋 names. He quotes the 跋 directly:
  - **(1) The People's Literature self-criticism.** During the Three-Anti campaign, "人民文學上刊登過一個寫作者的自我檢討", which described spring 1950 in a small county in North China: "正值春荒，農民為飢餓所迫，聚眾搶劫政府糧倉。當地的負責幹部率領民兵開槍彈壓，屠殺了很多的農民。這老幹部也受了傷……竟頹喪地向作者說：『我們失敗了！』" Chang wrote that "這篇文字給我的印象非常深".
  - **(2) Hunger everywhere.** A *Liberation Daily* (解放日報) item on "天津設立了飢民救濟站，救濟四郊飢民"; an acquaintance's report that peasants near 南昌 lived on rice-water (吃米湯度日); and, "從1950年冬天起又不斷地從蘇北與上海近郊來的人口中聽到『鄉下簡直沒有東西吃！』".
  - **(3) The film 「遙遠的鄉村」.** Chang wrote that its granary-arson scene, "如果不是完全虛構的話，那一定是農民的報復行為，被歪曲了的".
  - Source: [丁爾綱〈張愛玲的《秧歌》及其評論的寫作策略透析〉, 紹興文理學院學報 2006, in ESWN compilation](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)
- 戴晴 quotes another line from the 跋 on why Chang trusted these sources: "說這些話的人，都是我確實知道他們沒有說謊的習慣，也沒有說謊的理由" — [戴晴, ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm). Check the exact wording against the Crown text.
- The film has been identified. The "遙遠的鄉村" Chang half-remembered is the Northeast Film Studio's 《遼遠的鄉村》 (shot 1949, released 1950, dir. 吳永剛). In that film a landlord and KMT agents try to burn the public grain and the militia catches them. In the novel Chang turns the arson into 月香's revenge, and she satirises 顧岡's script for keeping the "correct" version — [王曉珏 2021, pp. 100–102](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c184-202006010.pdf)
- 丁爾綱 says he searched *People's Literature* for 1951, 1952 and 1953 twice, and *Wenyi bao* for 1952, and could not find the self-criticism. He calls Chang's claim "無中生有" — [丁爾綱 2006, ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm). This is a partisan source. I found no independent check either confirming or refuting his search.
- Other sources for the novel:
  - The pig-slaughter scene in ch. 12 draws directly on 《異鄉記》, the notes Chang wrote after a post-war trip to the countryside of eastern Zhejiang to visit 胡蘭成.
  - The 1950 Shanghai film 《太平春》 (dir. 桑弧), which Chang reviewed as 梁京 in 《亦報》 on 23 Jun 1950, also fed into it.
  - Source: [王曉珏 2021, pp. 98–99](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c184-202006010.pdf)
- 《赤地之戀》 自序 (1954), as quoted by 丁爾綱:
  - "『赤地之戀』所寫的是真人實事，但是小說究竟不是報導文學，我除了把真正的人名與一部分地名隱去……"
  - "我有時候告訴別人一個故事的輪廓，人家聽不出好處來，我總是辯護似的加上一句：『這是真事。』……我確實愛好真實到了迷信的程度。"
  - Source: [ESWN compilation](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)

**Chang's own rural experience**
- 殷允芃's 1968 interview, 〈訪張愛玲女士〉: "寫《秧歌》前，她曾在鄉下住了三四個月。那時是冬天。" Chang added: "這也是我的膽子小……寫的時候就擔心着，如果故事發展到了春天可要怎麼寫啊？" — the story ends in winter. — [殷允芃 1968, ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)
- 戴晴, by contrast, calls Chang "從未到過鄉下的大小姐" and says she clearly misunderstood how village militia worked — [戴晴, ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm). This conflicts with the 1968 interview.
- 戴晴 also says that the writers, scholars and artists who stayed on the mainland were "幾乎無一遺漏地被送下鄉參加(或稱見習)土改" — [戴晴, ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm). This describes the general practice; it is not specific evidence about Chang.
- Chang wanted the book to read as "平淡". In a letter to Hu Shih she wrote: "《秧歌》這故事太平淡，不合我國讀者的口味——尤其是東南亞的讀者" — [王曉珏 2021, p. 102](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c184-202006010.pdf)

### Inferences
- The 跋 bases the novel's "facts" on documents and hearsay: a party journal's self-criticism, a party paper's news item, and word of mouth from Subei and the Shanghai outskirts. It does not rest them on her own eyewitness of land reform. Whatever happened during the "three or four months in the countryside", Chang herself did not present it in the 跋 as her evidence.
- The two flagship scenes both come from inverting official texts. The famine riot and "我們失敗了" are taken from a cadre's own published confession. The arson re-reads a propaganda film's plot as a peasant's act of revenge. That supports a reading of the novel as a counter-reading of party discourse rather than as invented atrocity.
- The extra detail of the burned corpse in the English text was imposed by a New York editor. This is concrete evidence that the most "propagandistic" spectacle came from the US market, not from Chang's original design.

### Gaps
- The brief asks whether Chang took part in land reform in 1950. I found **no first-hand documentary proof**. Some sources say that after Shanghai's first 文代會 (July 1950) she joined a land-reform team and spent three or four months in the north (or Subei). That claim circulates, for example in [The News Lens article 102428](https://www.thenewslens.com/article/102428), but the page returned 403 and the snippet itself framed it as a question. **UNVERIFIED.** The only verified statement is 殷允芃's 1968 "鄉下住了三四個月…冬天", which gives no place or year.
- I did not get the full text of the 跋 from a primary edition, and I could not identify the specific *People's Literature* article. Its existence is disputed only by 丁爾綱. **UNVERIFIED either way.**

## 2. Critical reception of The Rice-Sprout Song (Hu Shih, C.T. Hsia, 1955 US reviews, later scholarship)

### Takeaway
Hu Shih, writing privately, praised the novel as having reached 「平淡而近自然」 and called it the best Chinese novel of recent years: "從頭到尾，寫的是『飢餓』". C.T. Hsia's 1961 *History* canonised it; the UC Press blurb quotes him calling it "A modern Chinese classic." US reviews in 1955 were positive and stressed both its truthfulness and its sympathy even for the cadre. On the mainland it was dismissed for decades as false anti-communist propaganda, most famously by 柯靈 in 1985.

### Cited Findings

**Hu Shih**
- Chang's letter to Hu, dated 十月廿五日 [1954], pasted into Hu's diary: "我希望您肯看一遍《秧歌》。假使您認為稍稍有一點接近『平淡而近自然』的境界，那我就太高興了。這本書我還寫了一個英文本，由 Scribner's 出版" — [王曉珏 2021, p. 94, citing 《胡適日記全編》 vol. 8, p. 352](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c184-202006010.pdf)
- Hu's diary for 23 Jan 1955: "我讀了這本小說，覺得很好。後來又讀了一遍，更覺得作者確已能做到『平淡而近自然』的境界。近年所出中國小說，這本小說可算是最好的了。" — [王曉珏 2021, p. 94](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c184-202006010.pdf)
  - The diary also records that he replied on 25 Jan: "如果我提倡《醒世姻緣》與《海上花》的結果單止產生了你這本小說，我也應該很滿意了。"
  - The diary's editor notes the date mismatch: an entry dated 23 January reports a reply sent on the 25th.
- Hu's letter of 25 Jan 1955 is preserved only in Chang's memoir 〈憶胡適之〉 (in 《張看》, Crown 1991, pp. 142–44) — [王曉珏 2021, n. bn](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c184-202006010.pdf). Excerpt as quoted by a later article: "你這本《秧歌》，我仔細看了兩遍，我很高興能看見這本很有文學價值的作品。你自己說的'有一點接近平淡而近自然的境界'，我認為你在這個方面已做到了很成功的地步！這本小說，從頭到尾，寫的是'飢餓'，——也許你曾想到用《餓》做書名，寫的真好，真有'平淡而近自然'的細緻工夫" — [ESWN compilation](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)
- So the letter is **25 January 1955**, and 「平淡而近自然」 is Hu's own phrase from his preface to 《海上花列傳》, which Chang echoed back to him.

**C.T. Hsia**
- UC Press 1998 edition blurb: "A modern Chinese classic." — C.T. Hsia — [UC Press](https://www.ucpress.edu/books/the-rice-sprout-song/paper)
- After Hu, Hsia was the first literary critic to praise the novel. His *History of Modern Chinese Fiction* (1961; 3rd ed. 1999, pp. 389–431) devotes a chapter to Chang. 夏濟安 translated the *Rice-Sprout Song* section as 〈評《秧歌》〉 for 《文學雜誌》 2:6 (Aug 1957), pp. 4–11, where it "在海外中文讀者中產生很大的影響" — [王曉珏 2021, n. do](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c184-202006010.pdf)
- Wang summarises Hsia's view: the novel exposes "極權政治高壓下民眾食不果腹、噤若寒蟬的真相" — [王曉珏 2021, p. 98](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c184-202006010.pdf)
- A Chinese rendering of Hsia, quoted by the hostile critic 丁爾綱: Chang "默默地觀察上海和附近村鎮在共產黨暴政下的生活……她把自己所直接觀察到的真實情形和共產黨官方的謊話對比" — [ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm). This is a second-hand Chinese version. Check it against the English 1961 text.
- Hsia called Chang "the best and most important writer" of modern Chinese literature — [Yu-Yun Hsieh, Open Letters Monthly, 31 Jul 2015](https://www.openlettersmonthlyarchive.com/olm/eileen-changs-changes-from-love-in-redland-to-naked-earth)

**1955 US reviews**
- Source: 高全之's digest of the *Book Review Digest 1955*, which lists 8 reviews (at least 9 counting 宋淇's recollection). The quotes below are Chinese back-translations; the English originals were not seen.
  - *Kirkus*, 1 Feb 1955: "推薦這本涵涉鮮為人知主題的優秀小說"
  - *New York Times*, 3 Apr 1955: "極佳的精短的長篇小說……張愛玲既是共產中國隱密世界的透察的評論者，也為令人興奮的新起的藝術家"
  - *Library Journal*, 1 May 1955: praises the English as enviable for native speakers and "她做為小說家的誠摯與技巧"
  - *Saturday Review*, 21 May 1955: "可能是目前最近真實的，中國共產黨統治下生活的長篇小說"
  - *Wisconsin Library Bulletin*, July 1955: "張小姐並未耽溺於政治演說。她的同情甚至延伸至地方黨部領導，那人比屬下更為受制於他所管理的制度裡……誠為精敏與忠實的報導"

## 3. Naked Earth: plot, the USIS commission, and Chang's discomfort with the outline

### Takeaway
*Naked Earth* (Chinese 《赤地之戀》, 1954; English, Hong Kong Union Press, 1956; NYRB Classics, 2015) follows the student-cadre Liu Ch'üan 劉荃 through:
- land reform, in a northern village
- the Three-Anti campaign, in Shanghai
- capture as a POW in Korea

His lover Su Nan 蘇楠 dies along the way. The evidence on its origins conflicts:
- Chang said repeatedly that the outline was "someone else's".
- McCarthy (in 2002) insisted that she wrote her own outline.
- Archival research by 王梅香 shows the project began as 徐東濱's USIS proposal *Farewell to the Korean Front*, which Chang took over under the "China Reporting Program".

### Cited Findings

**Plot**
- Historical events take center stage in a way they do not in Chang's earlier work. Land Reform (1950–53) and the Three-Anti/Five-Anti campaigns frame the love story of Liu Ch'üan and Su Nan.
- The land-reform section is "the most harrowing: it is rife with grotesque spectacles of physical torture".
- Under the Three-Anti campaign Liu joins "the chorus of charges".
- Su Nan "dies in a botched abortion before Liu's release from prison".
- As a POW, Liu is offered mainland China, Taiwan or a third country. He "chooses to go back to China as a saboteur", an ending the reviewer finds "as baffling and unconvincing as can be".
- "Terror turns a thinking individual into a member of the mob."
- Criticism: the characters "remain abstractions".
- Source: [Jiwei Xiao, "Eileen Chang's Journey into Darkness: A Review of *Naked Earth*," MCLC Resource Center (Feb 2015/2016)](https://u.osu.edu/mclc/book-reviews/xiaojiwei/)
- Author check: Jiwei Xiao, Fairfield University (Modern Languages & Literature faculty) — [DigitalCommons@Fairfield](https://digitalcommons.fairfield.edu/modernlanguagesandliterature-facultypubs/34)

**Chang's own statements**
- In a 1973 interview with the writer Shuijing 水晶, Chang said the novel was "commissioned" and its story "outlined" by someone else, so these were things she couldn't change. She made the same complaint to her friend Stephen C. Soong 宋淇. — [Yu-Yun Hsieh, Open Letters Monthly, 31 Jul 2015](https://www.openlettersmonthlyarchive.com/olm/eileen-changs-changes-from-love-in-redland-to-naked-earth)
- Hsieh also reports Chang's "discontent at having been 'commissioned' to write *Love in Redland*" — [same](https://www.openlettersmonthlyarchive.com/olm/eileen-changs-changes-from-love-in-redland-to-naked-earth)
- Jiwei Xiao: Chang complained she had to follow an outline formulated by "someone else" (別人) — [Xiao](https://u.osu.edu/mclc/book-reviews/xiaojiwei/)

**McCarthy's account (2002, see §4)**
- He denied that the outline was someone else's: "那不是實情……她自己提議寫小說。她有基本的故事概念……我確知她親擬故事概要." — [高全之 interview, ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)
- On *Naked Earth* specifically: "《赤地之戀》英文版……別人校訂的，大概是宋淇，不過我確實讀過她準備好的故事大綱." — [same](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)

**Archival evidence (王梅香)**
- 王梅香, 〈不為人知的張愛玲：美國新聞處譯書計畫下的《秧歌》與《赤地之戀》〉, *EurAmerica* 《歐美研究》 45:1 (Mar 2015), pp. 73–137:
  - Both novels were "commissioned and authorized" products of the USIS book-translation programme.
  - 《秧歌》 "最初是張愛玲的自主書寫" and was absorbed into the programme mid-way.
  - 《赤地之戀》 "原是香港政論家徐東濱所申請的《告別朝鮮前線》". Chang continued under that outline, and the work was "被納入中國報告計畫".
  - Wang calls Chang's position "不自由的自由書寫".
  - Source: [EurAmerica abstract page](https://euramerica.ea.sinica.edu.tw/期刊放置處41-50/45/45-1/45-1-3王梅香); DOI [10.7015/JEAS.201503_45(1).0003](https://doi.org/10.7015/JEAS.201503_45%281%29.0003)
- Hsieh draws on the same declassified material. 徐東濱 (William Hsu, editor of *China Weekly*) proposed "Farewell to the Korean Front" in December 1953. Six months later Chang submitted the same project, and it was retitled *Naked Earth* in June 1954. Hsieh concludes: "McCarthy was not being entirely honest." — [Hsieh, OLM 2015](https://www.openlettersmonthlyarchive.com/olm/eileen-changs-changes-from-love-in-redland-to-naked-earth)
- Wang Xiaojue, summarising 王梅香: unlike 《秧歌》, 《赤地之戀》 was "受香港 USIS 的直接委託，受制於寫作大綱及進度的審查" — [王曉珏 2021, n. bl](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c184-202006010.pdf)

**Publication and texts**
- *Love in Redland* (Chinese) was published in Hong Kong in 1954, and *Naked Earth* (English) in Hong Kong in 1956.
- Because the novel touches on the KMT and Chiang Kai-shek, an abridged edition was not made public in Taiwan until 1978.
- Its first US publication was the 2015 NYRB edition.
- *Naked Earth* has 32 short chapters against the Chinese text's 11 long ones. It "is less a second-language writer's self-translation than an improved rewrite".
- Source: [Hsieh, OLM](https://www.openlettersmonthlyarchive.com/olm/eileen-changs-changes-from-love-in-redland-to-naked-earth)
- Taiwanese writers recalled that in the 1950s–60s 《赤地之戀》 was not accepted officially as proper anti-communist literature: it had to be cut before publication and was at one point a banned book — [古遠清〈海峽兩岸「看張」的政治性和戲劇化現象〉, ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)

### Inferences
- The two accounts are at odds: Chang and Soong say "someone else's outline", McCarthy says "she wrote her own", and the archives point to 徐東濱's prior proposal. 王梅香's and Hsieh's archival findings carry the most weight. They suggest that the plot skeleton of *Naked Earth*, especially the Korean War/POW arc, was the USIS's or 徐東濱's, not Chang's.
- On the thesis: Chang's complaints and the outline evidence imply that her own priorities lie in the texture of the land-reform and Shanghai sections, not in the ideological frame.

### Gaps
- I did not find the full text of the 1971/1973 Shuijing interview 〈蟬——夜訪張愛玲〉. Hsieh dates it 1973. The visit is often dated 1971, so treat the year as uncertain.
- I did not obtain the full text of 王梅香's PDF, so memo dates and circulation figures were not extracted.

## 4. Richard McCarthy (USIS Hong Kong): what he said, in a verifiable interview

### Takeaway
In 高全之's 2002 phone and email interview, McCarthy claimed three things:
- *The Rice-Sprout Song* was "另當別論" — distinct from the propaganda-oriented "China Reporting Program".
- Chang proposed and outlined her novels herself.
- USIS never tried to steer her writing.

He also admitted the agency asked about her progress, and he drew a line between truthful reporting and propaganda. Archival work since then (§3) undercuts his account of *Naked Earth*.

### Cited Findings
- **The interview:** 高全之〈張愛玲與香港美新處——專訪麥卡錫〉. Conducted May 2002 by phone and email, when McCarthy was 81. He placed "no limits" on the questions, and the transcript was checked with him. Background comes from Georgetown's foreign-affairs oral-history collection (Dec 1988). — [ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm). Book version: 《張愛玲學》 (桂林：灕江, 2015), pp. 163–70 — [cited in 王曉珏 2021, n. bl](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c184-202006010.pdf)
- **On propaganda:** "『反中共的宣傳』意指『中國報告計劃』。該計劃包括報紙新聞記事的製作與傳播、雜誌專題報導、電台難民訪問，以及學術論文。《秧歌》另當別論." — [ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)
- **On Chang's motive and knowledge:** "我們請愛玲翻譯美國文學，她自己提議寫小說……我也在中國北方待過，非常驚訝她比我還了解中國農村的情形." — [ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)
- **On influence over the writing:**
  - "她是作家，你不能規定或提示她如何寫作。不過，因我們資助她，難免會詢問進度."
  - "我們絕對沒有嘗試藉討論來操縱或『幫助』《秧歌》的寫作……我相信最佳的宣傳——如果立意可取的話——是忠實報告社會現狀."
  - Source: [ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)
- **On the novels:**
  - "我覺得《赤地之戀》不如《秧歌》."
  - Of *Naked Earth*: "《秧歌》之後，她還有話要說……她自己要寫《赤地之戀》。這部小說有真摯的情緒與感受."
  - Source: [ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)
- **On how Rice-Sprout reached a US publisher:** John P. Marquand read the first two chapters overnight at the Peninsula Hotel ("我肯定這是一流作品") and took them back to the US to promote them. The English edition is dedicated "To Richard and Marie": McCarthy and the agent Marie Rodell. — [ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)
- **On Chang's status:** "愛玲不是美新處的職員。她與我們協議提供翻譯服務，翻譯一本就算一本." He also said his Taipei office argued for publishing apolitical young Taiwanese writers, as opposed to Beijing Foreign Languages Press works "偏重政宣、疏於人味". — [ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)
- **宋淇's account** (letter to Crown editor 方麗婉, 20 Jun 1991, in 《張愛玲私語錄》):
  - Chang won the open call to translate *The Old Man and the Sea*.
  - "其時愛玲正在用英文寫《秧歌》，她拿了幾章來，麥君大為心折，催她早日完稿，並代她在美物色到一位女經紀，很快找到大出版商Scribner接受出版."
  - Source: [ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)
- **Perry Link (NYRB, 2015):** it is "far-fetched to imagine that the USIS distorted Chang's writing", and the grant's significance has been exaggerated — [Perry Link, "Mao's China: The Language Game," NYR Daily, 15 May 2015](https://www.nybooks.com/online/2015/05/15/china-language-game-eileen-chang/). Note: Link's piece is adapted from his introduction to the NYRB *Naked Earth*.

### Inferences
- McCarthy's testimony, Soong's letter, and 王梅香's finding of 「最初是張愛玲的自主書寫」 all agree that *Rice-Sprout* was begun independently, before USIS involvement. Wang Xiaojue cites "a strong textual piece of evidence" for this as well. *Naked Earth* is the contested case.

### Gaps
- I did not find 鄭樹森's interviews with McCarthy, if any exist. **UNVERIFIED.**

## 5. Propaganda or humanist witness? Chang's framing, and the 2015 NYRB reception of Naked Earth

### Takeaway
Most serious critics accept that both books came out of a US propaganda apparatus. They argue that the novels look past ideology to how ordinary people feel, speak and starve under it:
- Perry Link: "looks past" the system.
- Wisconsin 1955: sympathy "even to the local party leader".
- The "平淡" aesthetic Chang and Hu Shih shared.

Dissenters include mainland critics (柯靈; 丁爾綱; a Jiangsu Academy of Social Sciences critic) and some Western reviewers who find *Naked Earth* didactic or abstract. I found no verified statement in which Chang explicitly says she writes "people not politics" about *these* novels. Her closest statements are earlier, from the 1940s.

### Cited Findings

**Perry Link (NYRB, 2015)**
- Chang "looks past" the political system to show how people feel and behave under it. She could "read past the propaganda overlay and infer what had actually happened".
- The campaign's linguistic grid "descends on a village like a giant cookie cutter".
- "Tell the truth!" means "recite lies *correctly*"; "an unimpeachable exterior becomes everyone's goal."
- He links this to the present: "an incorrect word-performance in public can be costly."
- Source: [Perry Link, NYR Daily, 15 May 2015](https://www.nybooks.com/online/2015/05/15/china-language-game-eileen-chang/)

**Other reviews of Naked Earth**
- NYRB Classics publisher copy: "Far from being a simplistic exercise in anti-Communist propaganda… a powerfully moving, Balzacian tale". It also notes that two of Chang's earlier novels were pro-Communist. — [Publishers Weekly listing / NYRB copy via search](https://publishersweekly.com/9781590178348). Publisher marketing copy.
- Publishers Weekly: "originally commissioned in the 1950s as anticommunist propaganda by the U.S. Information Service"; "an unrelenting portrait of love and loss in Maoist China" — [Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/9781590178348)
- Yu-Yun Hsieh: "*Naked Earth* is undoubtedly didactic, but it is illuminated by Chang's distinctive strengths", and it is a better novel than *Love in Redland* — [OLM 2015](https://www.openlettersmonthlyarchive.com/olm/eileen-changs-changes-from-love-in-redland-to-naked-earth)
- Jiwei Xiao calls it "idiosyncratic political fiction… haunted by a profound indecision" — [MCLC](https://u.osu.edu/mclc/book-reviews/xiaojiwei/)
- Student review (Anni Liu, Ohio Wesleyan *Night Owl*): the novel "rises above its dubious political origins as anti-Communist propaganda" — [OWU Night Owl, Jul 2015](https://sites.owu.edu/nightowl/2015/07/07/review-of-naked-earth-by-eileen-chang/). Student publication, low weight.
- Historical Novel Society (reviewer Sherry Jones): a story of man's inhumanity to man during Mao's land reform — [HNS](https://historicalnovelsociety.org/?p=15546). Not opened; summary from search.

**Wang Xiaojue (2021)**
- Chang had to use the spectacular fire to complete the novel's "political narrative".
- But she "不厭其煩地以冗長的篇幅來記述顧岡的斟酌思量過程，來消解和嘲弄在政治限制之下不得不編寫的戲劇性高潮".
- She sees Chang as like 桑弧, 吳永剛 and 顧岡: "在或左或右的意識形態的高壓下如履薄冰、左支右絀，書寫着可能和不可能的真實".
- Source: [王曉珏 2021, p. 102](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c184-202006010.pdf)

**戴晴 (1996)**
- Chang is "對大而無當的政治理念相當疏離的人". "她注重的只是各種各樣理念的行為結果，即真的發生了什麼，以及人們出於人的本性對周邊發生的一切的反應."
- She keeps "對美與醜的鑒別，對幼弱的同情，對惡勢力的憎惡".
- 戴晴 also links the cadre's shooting to Tiananmen: "雖然這槍不是開在天安門廣場".
- Source: [戴晴, ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)

**A mainland defence (anonymous, via ESWN)**
- "與其說《秧歌》反共，不如說《秧歌》反迫害，反對任何一種制度的迫害，這是對制度的批判，更是一種對人性的批判."
- Swap "同志" for "老爺" and 金根's family would still be destroyed.
- Source: [article 〈基於生存的苦難與真相的角度辯張愛玲《秧歌》「反共傾向」〉, ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm). Author and venue not identified in what I read.

**Chang's own framing**
- 1946, 〈有幾句話同讀者說〉: "我所寫的文章從來沒有涉及政治，也沒有拿過津貼" — quoted (to attack her) in a 2007 article by a Jiangsu Academy of Social Sciences critic — [ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm).
- That critic's own position, for contrast: Chang "在1952年到香港後炮製反共小說，露骨地反共反人民". The author is probably 陳遼, who wrote 「張愛玲熱」要降溫; attribution **UNVERIFIED**.
- 1968, to 殷允芃: "寫小說，她認為最重要的，是要對所寫的事物有了真感情，然後才下筆寫", and she distrusted mere "研究工作" — [殷允芃 1968, ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)

**Wang Meixiang's formula**
- "不自由的自由書寫" (free writing under an unfree institution): Chang kept her style inside the USIS framework — [王梅香 2015 abstract](https://euramerica.ea.sinica.edu.tw/期刊放置處41-50/45/45-1/45-1-3王梅香)

### Inferences
- There is broad critical agreement that the novels endure because their power sits in hunger, family, and the small compromises of cadres and intellectuals, not in their anti-communist argument. That fits the thesis of care for individual lives.
- Evidence against the thesis must be acknowledged:
  - *Naked Earth*'s externally shaped plot and its schematic ending.
  - The English *Rice-Sprout* ending, added at an editor's demand for political force.
  - Reviewers such as Xiao, who find the characters "abstractions".

### Gaps
- The well-known lines from 〈自己的文章〉 (1944) — that she writes only "男女間的小事情" and that her works have "no war and no revolution" — would be the obvious self-statement. **I could not verify them against a source URL in this session. Treat as UNVERIFIED until checked in 《流言》.**
- The brief mentions 3 Quarks Daily and LARB reviews of *Naked Earth*. I found **no such reviews**; searches returned none, so they may not exist.

## 6. PRC treatment: labels, bans, current availability; Hong Kong libraries

### Takeaway
On the mainland the two novels have been labelled 「反共小說」 and left out of the standard Chang collections. In the mid-1990s they appeared briefly in two semi-marginal "complete works", one of them altered. Wang Xiaojue still wrote in 2021 that they "無法在中國大陸出版". I found no documented post-NSL removal of Chang's books from Hong Kong public libraries.

### Cited Findings
- "至今……《秧歌》與《赤地之戀》無法在中國大陸出版" (as of 2021) — [王曉珏 2021, p. 97](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/21c/media/articles/c184-202006010.pdf)
- Because they were "被中國大陸指認為『反共小說』", ordinary mainland publishers left them out. For example, the 安徽文藝出版社 《張愛玲文集》 (4 vols, 1992, eds. 金宏達 & 于青) omitted them. A blogger found a 大連出版社 1996 printing and wondered "不知是不是非法出版物？". — [ESWN compilation (blog post 〈張愛玲的《秧歌》為何被封殺？〉, 2010)](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)
- 戴晴, after Chang's death in 1995: only 大連出版社 and 內蒙古人民出版社 printed "全集" containing both novels. The latter altered the text, for example rendering 陳毅's name as □□. Booksellers reacted with "一臉茫然……一臉惶然", and 戴晴 decided to hide her copies. She jokes that the responsible editor was named 李鵬. — [戴晴, ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)
- The *Los Angeles Times* obituary (16 Sep 1995), as translated by 高全之: Chang's works had "最近才在中國大陸解禁", and she "作品經常批判共產主義" — [ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm). The original English text was not seen.
- Hostile mainland framings:
  - 丁爾綱 calls the novel's grain-requisition story "弥天大謊" meant "製造軍民矛盾、黨群矛盾" — [ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)
  - The 2007 Jiangsu critic calls them "炮製反共小說，露骨地反共反人民" — [ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)
- Hong Kong after the NSL:
  - Public libraries removed hundreds of politically sensitive titles. About 40% of politics books were reportedly pulled between 2020 and 2023 — [Wikipedia: Book censorship in Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_censorship_in_Hong_Kong); [RFA, 16 May 2023](https://www.rfa.org/english/news/china/hong-kong-libraries-05162023134256.html)
  - No source found names Eileen Chang.
  - The Hong Kong Public Libraries still host Eileen Chang literary-appreciation material — [HKPL Eileen Chang subtitle file](https://www.hkpl.gov.hk/tc/common/attachments/extension-activities/literature/Appreciation-of-Literature/EileenChang-Subtitle-EP1.pdf)

### Inferences
- "Banned" is too blunt a word. The pattern looks like exclusion from mainstream mainland publication, with brief marginal printings in the 1990s and continued absence noted in 2021, rather than a formally announced ban.

### Gaps
- The current (2026) mainland availability of 《秧歌》 and 《赤地之戀》 is not verified. For example, I did not confirm whether the 北京十月文藝 《張愛玲全集》 includes them, and I did not check Douban or JD listings. **UNVERIFIED.**
- I found no evidence of HKPL removing 《秧歌》 or 《赤地之戀》. A catalogue check would settle it.

## 7. Contemporary relevance: Great Leap famine and the 2022 Shanghai lockdown

### Takeaway
There is sourced commentary linking *The Rice-Sprout Song* to the 2022 Shanghai lockdown hunger: the commentator 長平, writing for DW, as reported by Taiwan's CNA. There is also a weak blog-level link to the Great Leap famine. 戴晴 explicitly linked the novel's shooting of villagers to Tiananmen.

### Cited Findings
- **Shanghai 2022.** CNA, 21 Apr 2022:
  - During the Shanghai lockdown, netizens began "worshipping" Chang as 「跑路天后」 (queen of getting out).
  - The commentator 長平 argued on Deutsche Welle that "人們應該讀一讀張愛玲描述飢餓的「秧歌」，因為「秧歌」與當下上海的境遇密切相關", and that "「秧歌」不僅講述了一個政權製造的饑餓和迫害等人權災難".
  - Source: [CNA 中央社, 2022-04-21](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/acn/202204210239.aspx)
  - Other reports say 長平's DW piece of 19 Apr 2022 called *Rice-Sprout* a Chinese *1984*, and noted that writers like 蘇青, 關露 and 周瘦鵑, who stayed, did not escape persecution — [NTDTV report](https://www.ntdtv.com/gb/2022/04/20/a103405863.html/amp). NTDTV is a partisan outlet; use the CNA report or the DW original.
- **Great Leap.** "eerily enough, a foreshadowing of the horrible consequences of the Great Leap Forward, which started three years after the book was published" — [Susan Blumberg-Kason blog review, Oct 2010](https://www.susanbkason.com/2010/10/06/book-of-the-week-the-rice-sprout-song-2/). Blog-level, low weight.
- **Tiananmen.** 戴晴 on the cadre's shooting: "雖然這槍不是開在天安門廣場". She also notes that Chang's mainland readership grew after 1989 "得益於讀者目睹坦克而後的信念崩潰" — [戴晴, ESWN](http://www.zonaeuropa.com/culture/c20101221_1.htm)
- **Language control today.** Perry Link argues that public "word-performance" remains costly in today's China, citing Chinese students in California and spending on "stability maintenance" — [Link, NYR Daily 2015](https://www.nybooks.com/online/2015/05/15/china-language-game-eileen-chang/)

### Inferences
- Commentators use the novel's core image — compulsory festivity and "contributions" amid hunger — as a template for later state-made hunger. The 2022 Shanghai link is sourced to a named commentator via a mainstream wire.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the DW original by 長平 (URL not found). I found no scholarly article linking the novel to the Great Leap famine.
