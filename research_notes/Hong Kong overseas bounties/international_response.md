## 1. Government statements for each round (UK, US, Canada, Australia, NZ, EU, Japan, Germany; joint statements)

### Takeaway
The UK, US and (once Canadians were targeted, from Dec 2024) Canada answered every round with a dated statement on the same day or the next. The language hardened from condemning the National Security Law's (NSL) "extraterritorial reach" (2023) to explicitly naming "transnational repression" (US from July 2023, UK from July 2025). Australia spoke in 2023 and 2025. The EU's only dedicated statement was in July 2025, when an EU citizen was first targeted. The only multilateral statement is the G7 Rapid Response Mechanism (G7 RRM) statement of 8 Aug 2025. Japan, Germany, France, Italy, NZ, the Netherlands and Sweden appear only as its signatories. No Five Eyes joint statement on the bounties was found.

### Inferences
- The UK escalated in steps: a démarche by officials (Jul 2023), then formal summonses in which bounties were named (May 2024, Dec 2025 in a statement to Parliament, May 2026). It has not yet sanctioned anyone. The US moved from statements to sanctions (Mar 2025).
- Canada's statements begin only when Canadians are named (Dec 2024). The EU's begin only when an EU citizen is named (Jul 2025). This suggests responses keyed to each country's own nationals and residents rather than to the practice as such.
- The G7 RRM statement is the first and so far only multilateral text to explicitly call the Hong Kong bounties "transnational repression". Japan, Germany, France and Italy have endorsed that framing only collectively.

- No **EEAS** statement on the Dec 2023 or Dec 2024 rounds was found. EU officials' Dec 2024 comments are attested only by the HK government's rebuttal.

## 2. Concrete measures (sanctions, US Hong Kong Policy Act reports, UK FIRS/designations/police guidance, Australia, Canada, Joseph Tay)

### Takeaway
The only coercive measure aimed at officials over the bounties is the **US designation of six Beijing and Hong Kong officials on 31 March 2025** under E.O. 13936. The UK has preferred protective and legal measures:
- completing the removal of Hong Kong from the Extradition Act 2003 (Jul 2025);
- a Defending Democracy Taskforce review of transnational repression (TNR) (May 2025), TNR guidance, and police training for all 45 forces;
- prosecuting HKETO-linked spies under the National Security Act (convicted May 2026, jailed June 2026);
- asylum grants.

As of June 2026 the UK had **not** placed China on the enhanced tier of the Foreign Influence Registration Scheme (FIRS) and had sanctioned no Hong Kong official. Australia granted asylum to Ted Hui (Aug 2025). Canada's foreign-influence registry came into force on 4 Aug 2026, and Canada resettled a "Hong Kong Parliament" target stranded in Thailand (Aug 2026).

## 5. NGO responses (Amnesty, HRW, Hong Kong Watch, Freedom House, PEN, RSF, CPJ, Safeguard Defenders)

## 6. Hong Kong and Beijing responses to criticism (incl. "absconders", "rats", counter-sanctions)

### Takeaway
After every round the HKSAR government issued same-day or next-day rebuttals. The core claims are that extraterritoriality is "a common feature of national security laws" (the UK, US and Australia are named), that critics show "double standards", and that "No country should harbour criminals". Targets are labelled "absconders" (the SNSO legal term) or "fugitives". In July 2023 John Lee said the eight should be treated like "street rats" and would be "pursued for life". In August 2025 Hong Kong summoned the Australian and British consuls-general over asylum grants. Beijing's counter-measure against the March 2025 US sanctions was an announcement on 21 April 2025, under the Anti-Foreign Sanctions Law, of sanctions on unnamed US Congress members, officials and NGO heads.

### Inferences
- Hong Kong's rebuttals are formulaic and escalate in whom they name: "certain countries" (2023), then the US, UK, Canada and EU (2024), then every G7 RRM member (2025). This shows the international response widening even where statements are perfunctory.
