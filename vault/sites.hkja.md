The **Hong Kong Journalists Association** (香港記者協會, "記協"; HKJA) is the city's principal trade union for working journalists. Founded in **1968**, it is an affiliate of the **International Federation of Journalists** (IFJ) and a member of the global free-expression network **IFEX**, and for more than half a century it was the main independent voice defending Hong Kong's press — its stated purpose being "to enhance press freedom and the integrity of news coverage" and to improve journalists' working conditions.

Through the decades the HKJA became the institution that *measured* the freedom it defended. It ran an annual **Press Freedom Index** survey of journalists and the public, co-organised the regional **Human Rights Press Awards** (with the [[Foreign Correspondents' Club|sites.fcchk]] and Amnesty International), published yearly reports on the state of the media, and provided legal and practical support to reporters facing arrest, assault or intimidation while covering the news.

## A union under siege

Since the 2019 protests and the 2020 National Security Law, the HKJA has itself become a target of the crackdown it documents — part of the wider story told on the [[Press Freedom under Siege|press-freedom]] hub.

- **A shrinking membership.** As the newsrooms that employed its members were [[shut down|press-freedom]] — Apple Daily, [[Stand News|sites.standnews]], Citizen News — and journalists left the profession or the city, membership and finances collapsed. By **June 2024** the association reported just **338 members**, and successive chairs have warned of severe financial strain.
- **Smears and official pressure.** State-affiliated media and senior officials have repeatedly attacked the group, questioning its legitimacy and representativeness. In June 2024 the **Security Bureau publicly admonished** newly elected committee members, and two nominees withdrew.
- **The Human Rights Press Awards, abandoned.** The awards the HKJA had co-run for decades were effectively ended in **2022** after the FCC pulled out, citing legal risk under the security law.

## Its chairs, prosecuted and pressured

The people who have led the HKJA trace the pressure on the whole profession:

- **Ronson Chan** (陳朗昇), chair **2021–2024** and a former deputy assignment editor at [[Stand News|sites.standnews]], was arrested in **September 2022** while reporting on a homeowners' meeting in Mong Kok and charged with **obstructing a police officer** who had demanded his ID. Convicted in 2023 and sentenced to **5 days in jail**, he **lost his appeal on 29 May 2026** and was jailed immediately, later taking the case to the **Court of Final Appeal**. RSF called his imprisonment "a new low for press freedom in Hong Kong." He had declined to seek re-election in 2024, saying the combined attacks on him and the HKJA had grown too damaging.
- **Selina Cheng** (鄭嘉如), a *Wall Street Journal* reporter, was elected chair in **July 2024** — and **fired by the WSJ days later**, having said her employer pressured her not to stand. She has led the association since.

The HKJA continues to issue statements defending reporters — in October 2026 insisting that "**reporting is not sedition**" after an online journalist's arrest (see [[Press Freedom under Siege|press-freedom]]) — even as its own survival is in question.
